# Copy brief: S1 Directory essays

Each company page on s1directory.com has an essay. The reader's job is simple: keep reading to the end,
then click "Read the original S-1". Every line must pull the reader to the next one.
Use this brief to rewrite existing entries, and to write new ones (it overrides the writing rules in WRITER_BRIEF.md).

## What to rewrite

Only these fields: `tagline`, `dek`, `lede`, `sections[].heading`, `sections[].body`.
Do not change any other field (slug, display, space, founders, founded, hq, fate, cover, …).
Keep exactly two sections.

## Facts

- Keep every fact the current text states. Do not add new facts from memory.
- You may add a new fact only if you confirm it with a web search in this session.
- If a current fact looks wrong, check it with a web search. Fix it if it is wrong. If you cannot confirm it, cut it.
- Never invent a quote, a number, or a detail to make a line punchier.

## The fields

**tagline** (3–7 words, italic title, no company name). It must be specific to this company's story.
A reader should guess the company from it. "Earth's Biggest Bookstore" is good. "Shipping Content Safely" is generic: fix it.

**dek** (one sentence, at most 20 words). What the company did for people, in plain words.
Lead with the benefit, not a feature list.
- Before: "Cloud content management for businesses, letting teams store, share and secure files with strong controls."
- After: "Box let big companies keep their files in the cloud without scaring their security teams."

**lede** (60–90 words). The first sentence is the hook. Open with the most interesting, specific, or surprising
detail in the whole entry. It is often buried in a later section. Do not open with a date ("In 1999, …"),
"In the late 1990s", or a definition of the company. The lede still has to set the scene when the company filed.

**sections** (two, 60–100 words each).
- `heading`: 2–5 words, specific to this company. Never use "The Original Bet", "After the Offering",
  "After the Bell", or "The Road to the Bell": every page already uses them. Make headings a reader would want
  to read under, e.g. "A Hedge Fund Job, Left Behind", "The Kindle and the Cloud", "Ninety Days of Cash".
- Section 1: the founding idea and the bet. Section 2: the IPO (price, date) and what happened after, ending with where the company is today.
- End each section on a concrete fact. Do not end on a moral or a summary
  ("Few companies of its era changed course so completely." → cut).

## Line rules

- One thought per sentence. Vary sentence length: some short, some longer.
- Write how a smart person talks to a friend. Simple words a 12-year-old follows.
- No adverbs that prop up a weak verb ("very", "really", "strongly", "seamlessly").
- No clichés or hype ("pioneer", "game-changer", "revolutionary", "at the forefront", "took the world by storm").
- No contrastive "not X, but Y" or "not just X" constructions. State the claim directly.
- No em dashes. No rhetorical questions.
- Prefer a specific number or name over a vague phrase ("many customers" → "3,000 customers", only if the fact is in the text or confirmed).
- Avoid "helped", "would go on to", "at the time", and "in the late 1990s" where a sharper word works.
- Use American spelling. Use "percent" in prose, "$18 a share" for prices.

## Example

Before (8x8 lede): "In 1997 video over phone lines was a dream that kept almost arriving. 8x8, a Silicon Valley chip company, designed processors…"

After: "8x8 built chips to put your face on a phone call, a decade before anyone wanted one. The Silicon Valley company designed processors that squeezed video and audio down small enough to travel over ordinary phone lines. Its chips went into videophones and conference-room systems. When it went public in July 1997, 8x8 was a small semiconductor maker betting that people would want to see each other when they called."

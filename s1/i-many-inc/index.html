Keeping Count of Every Deal

Priced July 13, 2000

Drug makers sign thousands of contracts with wholesalers, hospitals, pharmacies and insurers, each with its own rebates and price terms. Tracking all of it on spreadsheets led to errors and lost money. I-many built software to manage those contracts and calculate what was owed. It went public in July 2000, as the business software market was still hot. Investors saw contract software as a fast-growing niche.

The Original Bet

I-many bet that contract management in health care was complex enough to need its own software. Its products tracked prices, rebates and compliance across many trading partners. Its customers were mainly pharmaceutical and life sciences firms. The company later tried to widen its market to other industries. Its tools helped clients find money they were owed and avoid paying rebates twice. That made the value of the software easy to measure for finance teams.

Back to Private

I-many priced its IPO at $9 a share in July 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. Growth slowed after the dot-com crash and the company posted repeated losses. It kept a loyal base of pharmaceutical customers. In 2009 the private equity firm LLR Partners acquired I-many for about $55 million. The deal ended its time as a public company. The business continued under private ownership, still serving drug makers and other life sciences firms.

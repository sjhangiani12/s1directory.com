Ads Around the Clock

Priced August 13, 1998

In 1998 thousands of websites had audiences but no sales staff to sell ads. Advertisers, for their part, did not want to call each site one by one. 24/7 Media solved both problems. It gathered many sites into a network, sold ad space across all of them, and served the ads through its own technology. It went public in August 1998 in New York, as online advertising grew into a real industry.

The Original Bet

The bet was that the web's long tail of sites could be packaged and sold like a television network. 24/7 Media signed up publishers, split ad revenue with them, and used targeting tools to place ads where they would perform. It competed with DoubleClick and other networks for both advertisers and publishers. Each new site added to the network made it more useful to advertisers.

After the Offering

24/7 Media priced its offering at $14 a share in August 1998. The stock rose through the boom and then fell sharply when online ad spending collapsed in 2000 and 2001. The company merged with Real Media in 2001 and became 24/7 Real Media. WPP acquired it in 2007 for about $649 million. The deal put the network inside one of the world's largest advertising groups.

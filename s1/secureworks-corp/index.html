Armor for the Network

Priced April 22, 2016

SecureWorks watched over other companies' networks. Its analysts monitored alerts around the clock, hunted for intruders and helped clients respond when attacks happened. Dell bought the company in 2011 and later spun off a small piece of it in a public offering. When SecureWorks filed in 2016, data breaches were front page news, but the tech offering market had nearly frozen.

The Original Bet

SecureWorks began in Atlanta in 1999 and grew by selling security monitoring to banks and other firms that could not staff their own security teams. Its counter threat unit became known for research on hacking groups. The bet was that most companies would rather rent expert defenders than build them in-house. Dell saw the business as a way to add security to its enterprise offerings.

Under Dell's Wing

SecureWorks priced at $14 a share in April 2016, below its target range, in the first tech offering of the year. Dell kept most of the shares and voting control. The company later shifted toward its own extended detection and response software. In 2025 the British security firm Sophos completed its purchase of SecureWorks for about $859 million. Dell remained the majority owner until the sale.

City Hall, Open All Night

Priced July 15, 1999

In 1999 most dealings with a state government still meant a line at an office counter. NIC offered states a different deal. It would build and run their official websites at no upfront cost, and it would earn its money from small fees on services like driving records and business filings. The company began in Kansas and by its offering it had signed several states, betting that public services would follow commerce onto the web.

The Original Bet

Jeffery Fraser started the business in Kansas in the early 1990s as the Kansas Information Consortium. The model was self-funding: a state gave NIC a contract, NIC paid to build the portal, and fees from frequent users, often lawyers, insurers, and lenders, covered the cost. That removed the biggest obstacle for state agencies, which rarely had technology budgets to spare. Each new state contract became a long-running source of revenue.

The Road to the Bell

NIC priced its offering at $12 a share in July 1999. Like many internet stocks it fell hard in 2000, but the business underneath kept growing quietly. State by state, NIC added portals and services until it ran official websites for a large share of American states. Tyler Technologies, a maker of software for local governments, bought NIC in 2021 for about $2.3 billion.

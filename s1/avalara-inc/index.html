Rendering Unto Caesar

Priced June 15, 2018

Avalara automated one of the dullest and most painful jobs in commerce. A business that sold in many places had to know thousands of different sales tax rates and rules. Avalara's software looked up the right tax for each sale, filed returns and kept records. The company filed to go public in 2018, just as the Supreme Court weighed the Wayfair case, which would let states tax online sellers with no local presence.

The Original Bet

Avalara began in 2004 as a small team on Bainbridge Island, near Seattle. Scott McFarlane and his co-founders saw that sales tax software was mostly built for large companies and was hard to use. They bet that a cloud service could plug into shopping carts and accounting tools and do the work for businesses of any size. The more complicated tax rules became, the more valuable the service was.

A Ruling Arrives

Avalara priced at $24 a share in June 2018. Days later the Supreme Court decided South Dakota v. Wayfair, which let states require online sellers to collect sales tax. The ruling made Avalara's product essential for many more businesses, and the stock climbed for years. In 2022 Vista Equity Partners bought the company for about $8.4 billion and took it private.

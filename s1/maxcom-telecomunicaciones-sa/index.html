A New Line Across Mexico

Priced October 18, 2007

Maxcom was a challenger in Mexican telecom. It offered local phone, internet and later pay television to homes and small businesses in Mexico City, Puebla and other cities, in a market long dominated by Telmex. It listed its shares on the New York Stock Exchange in 2007, when emerging markets were in favor and investors were looking for growth in Latin America.

The Original Bet

Maxcom was founded in 1996, after Mexico opened local telephone service to competition. The bet was that a nimble new carrier could win customers from a slow incumbent by building modern networks in selected cities and bundling services. It invested heavily in its own lines and fiber. Like many challengers, it faced the hard economics of building physical networks street by street.

After the Offering

Maxcom priced its New York listing at $17.50 per American depositary share in October 2007. The financial crisis and fierce competition hurt the business, and the company went through debt troubles and restructuring. It delisted from the New York Stock Exchange in 2014. Its shares continued to trade in Mexico and over the counter in the United States after that. The company never matched the growth its 2007 investors had hoped for.

Curing the Paperwork

Priced February 10, 1999

By 1999 Jim Clark had already co-founded Silicon Graphics and Netscape. His third company, Healtheon, took aim at the most paper-bound industry in America. Healthcare spent huge sums on claims, referrals, and approvals that moved by fax and phone. Healtheon wanted to connect every doctor, insurer, lab, and patient over the internet so these steps happened automatically. Its offering in February 1999 drew attention largely because of Clark's track record.

The Original Bet

Jim Clark founded Healtheon in 1995. The bet was that the internet could take a large share of the waste out of healthcare administration. The company built software to check eligibility, submit claims, and handle referrals online. Progress was slower than planned, because healthcare had many players and few shared standards. Doctors, insurers, and hospitals each used different systems, and few saw a reason to change quickly. The vision was large, and the work to reach it was slow.

After the Offering

Healtheon priced its offering at $8 a share in February 1999, and the stock rose sharply that spring. Later in 1999 it merged with WebMD and several other companies, and the combined business took the WebMD name. WebMD became the best-known consumer health site in America and remained a public company until it was taken private in 2017. The original goal of cutting paperwork remained a large part of the business.

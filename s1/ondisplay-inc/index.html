Everything in the Window

Priced December 17, 1999

Online stores in 1999 had a basic problem: the product information they needed lived in dozens of places, in different formats, owned by different suppliers. OnDisplay made software that gathered that content and put it in shape for the web. When it filed to go public in late 1999, e-commerce sites were multiplying, and every one of them needed something in the window.

The Original Bet

The bet was that content integration would become a core layer of online commerce. A retailer or marketplace could not sell what it could not describe, and suppliers kept their catalogs in databases, spreadsheets and paper. OnDisplay's software pulled that information together, cleaned it, and kept it current, so sites could show accurate products and prices without rebuilding their systems.

A Quick Sale

OnDisplay priced at $28 a share in December 1999 and rose quickly in the final rush of the dot-com market. It did not stay independent for long. In 2000 Vignette, the content management company, agreed to buy OnDisplay in an all-stock deal valued at roughly $1.7 billion at announcement. The value of that stock fell sharply as the market collapsed later that year.

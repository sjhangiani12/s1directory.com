Every Option, Correctly Chosen

Priced October 06, 1999

Buying a computer server or a phone system meant choosing among many options that had to fit together. Calico Commerce sold software that guided customers and sales teams through those choices online, producing a correct configuration and price. When it filed to go public in the fall of 1999, companies were rushing to sell complex products on the web and needed tools to do it without errors.

The Original Bet

The bet was that interactive selling would move to the web, and that configuration would be its hardest part. Calico's software captured the rules behind a product line so that a buyer could not order a combination that would not work. It sold mainly to technology and telecom companies with large, complex catalogs and long sales cycles. Many of its customers were Silicon Valley hardware firms.

A Short Run

Calico priced at $14 a share in October 1999. The fall of e-business spending in 2000 and 2001 hit it hard, and it restated earnings and was delisted from Nasdaq in 2001. That year it sold its trading exchange unit to Digital River. Calico then filed for Chapter 11, and PeopleSoft bought its software and intellectual property in 2002. Its offering came and went in barely two years.

The Doctor Will See You Online

Priced September 28, 2005

WebMD was where Americans went to look up their symptoms. Its site explained conditions, drugs and treatments in plain language, and its sister site Medscape served doctors. Pharmaceutical and health companies paid to advertise to these readers. In 2005 the company was a unit of Emdeon, the former Healtheon/WebMD, and this offering sold a minority stake in its online health business.

The Original Bet

WebMD came out of the dot-com era, when Jeff Arnold founded it in 1998 and it merged with Jim Clark's Healtheon. The early dream was to wire the whole health care system. What lasted was simpler: a trusted source of health information for consumers, paid for by advertising and sponsorships. Its consumer site and Medscape together gave it reach to both patients and doctors.

After the Offering

WebMD Health priced its IPO at $17.50 a share in September 2005 on the Nasdaq. It later became fully independent of its parent and stayed the top health site for years. In 2017 Internet Brands, owned by KKR, bought WebMD for about $2.8 billion. The site remains one of the most visited health information sources in the United States. Its brand is still widely known.

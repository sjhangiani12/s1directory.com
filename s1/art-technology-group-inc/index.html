The Art of the Storefront

Priced July 20, 1999

Art Technology Group began in Cambridge as a small technology consulting firm with an artful name. By the late 1990s, it sold software that large companies used to build online stores that remembered customers and tailored what they saw. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, big brands were racing to sell on the web, and ATG offered them a serious platform for doing it.

The Original Bet

Jeet Singh and Joe Chung, both with roots at MIT, founded ATG in 1991. The bet was that large online businesses would need a powerful platform built for personalization, not just a catalog and a cart. ATG's Dynamo software let sites show each visitor different products and offers based on their behavior, which made it popular with big retailers and media companies.

After the Offering

ATG priced at $12 a share in July 1999 and became one of the hottest software stocks of the boom. The crash hit it hard and forced deep cuts. The company recovered as large retailers kept investing in online sales, and its commerce platform became a common choice for major brands. In 2011 Oracle bought ATG for about $1 billion.

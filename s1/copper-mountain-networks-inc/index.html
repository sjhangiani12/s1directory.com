Mining Speed from Copper

Priced May 12, 1999

By 1999 a new kind of phone company was racing to sell DSL. These competitive carriers rented copper phone lines from the old monopolies and installed their own equipment in central offices. Copper Mountain Networks made that equipment, the access concentrators that turned plain copper into broadband. Its customers were the fastest-growing carriers of the moment, and it went public in May 1999 with sales rising quickly.

The Original Bet

Copper Mountain was founded in 1996. The bet was that the copper already running into every home and office could carry far more than voice. Its systems packed many DSL lines into a small space, which mattered in crowded central offices where carriers paid by the square foot. Covad and NorthPoint, two of the largest new DSL carriers, were major buyers.

After the Offering

Copper Mountain priced its offering at $21 a share in May 1999, and the stock rose sharply through the following year. Then its customers began to fail. When the competitive carriers went bankrupt or slowed in 2000 and 2001, Copper Mountain's sales collapsed. Tut Systems acquired the company in 2005 in a stock deal valued at about $10 million. Its fate tracked its customers' fate almost exactly.

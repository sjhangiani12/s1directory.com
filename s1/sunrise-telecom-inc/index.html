Testing the Lines at Dawn

Priced July 12, 2000

Every telephone and cable network needs technicians who can test lines, find faults and confirm service is working. Sunrise Telecom, based in San Jose, built handheld and rack-mounted testers for that work. In 2000, carriers were building new networks across the country, and test gear was in demand. Sunrise Telecom went public in July 2000, when telecom equipment stocks were still near their highs.

The Original Bet

Sunrise bet that each new network technology would need new tools to test it, from older digital phone lines to DSL, fiber and cable. It built a broad range of instruments and sold them to carriers and equipment makers. The work was steady but tied closely to how much carriers chose to spend. Its testers were used both in labs and in the field.

A Long Afternoon

Sunrise Telecom priced its IPO at $15 a share in July 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. Carrier spending fell sharply the next year. In 2005 Nasdaq delisted the company after it failed to file reports during an internal investigation, and it moved to the over-the-counter market. In 2013 VeEX bought most of its operating assets. Its products lived on under the new owner.

Straight From the Field

Priced December 17, 2003

In the late 1990s most flowers sent across the country passed through a wire service and a local florist, often sitting in coolers for days. Jared Polis, a young internet entrepreneur, founded ProFlowers in 1998 on a different plan: take the order online and ship the stems in a box straight from the farm. By the time its parent, Provide Commerce, went public in December 2003, ProFlowers had become one of the largest flower sellers online.

The Original Bet

The bet was that cutting out the middle of the supply chain would mean fresher flowers and lower prices. ProFlowers carried almost no inventory. Growers packed and shipped orders directly, coordinated by Provide Commerce's software. The company later applied the same model to other perishables, including gourmet foods and fruit, and bought rival gift sellers. Seasonal peaks were steep, and the software had to scale for a few huge days each year.

Petals and Profits

Provide Commerce priced its IPO at $15 a share in December 2003 and listed on Nasdaq. The company grew around big gift holidays such as Valentine's Day and Mother's Day, and it expanded through acquisitions. In 2006 Liberty Media bought Provide Commerce for about $477 million. Jared Polis went on to a career in politics. The ProFlowers brand continued under later owners for many years.

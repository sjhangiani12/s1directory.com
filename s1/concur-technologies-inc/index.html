The Traveler's Receipt

Priced December 16, 1998

Every business trip ends with a pile of receipts and an expense report. In the 1990s that report was usually a paper form or a spreadsheet, checked by hand in a finance office. Concur sold software that moved the process online, so employees could file expenses and managers could approve them quickly. When it went public in December 1998, Concur was a small company in the Seattle area betting on a dull but universal chore.

The Original Bet

Steve Singh, Rajeev Singh, and Mike Hilton founded the company in 1993. The bet was that expense reporting was a costly, error-prone task that every company shared. Concur first sold software that customers installed themselves, then moved to delivering it over the internet. That shift made it one of the early successes of software as a service. Large companies with many traveling employees were the natural first customers.

After the Offering

Concur priced its offering at $12.50 a share in December 1998. The stock fell hard after 2000, and the company nearly ran out of room. It survived by moving to a subscription service and later added travel booking through its purchase of Outtask. Concur grew into the standard tool for business travel and expenses. SAP acquired it in 2014 for about $8.3 billion. It was a long road from a small offering to one of the largest software deals of its year.

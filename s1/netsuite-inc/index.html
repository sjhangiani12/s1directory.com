Running the Business in the Clouds

Priced December 19, 2007

NetSuite put a company's books in the browser. Its software handled accounting, orders, inventory and customer records in a single system, hosted on its own servers and sold by subscription. In 2007, enterprise software still mostly meant installing large packages on company computers. NetSuite was one of the first to show that the core financial system of a business could live in the cloud.

The Original Bet

Evan Goldberg, a former Oracle executive, founded the company in 1998 with backing from Larry Ellison. The bet was that small and midsize businesses would trade the cost and hassle of installed software for one integrated online service. NetSuite spent years building out its product while the market for cloud software slowly matured. Ellison's backing gave the young company money and credibility while it waited for the market.

The Road to the Bell

NetSuite priced its IPO at $26 a share in December 2007 on the New York Stock Exchange, under the ticker N. The stock fell hard in the 2008 crash. Growth returned as cloud software won acceptance. In 2016 Oracle bought NetSuite for about $9.3 billion. By then NetSuite served thousands of midsize businesses, and the deal made Oracle a major player in cloud business software.

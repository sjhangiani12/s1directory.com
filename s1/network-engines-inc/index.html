Servers by the Slice

Priced July 13, 2000

In 2000, companies were filling data centers with servers as fast as they could, and space was expensive. Network Engines, based outside Boston, built thin, dense servers that packed more computing into each rack. It went public in July 2000, at a time when data center space was scarce and the companies building websites seemed to have unlimited budgets. Its servers were built for speed, density and reliability.

The Original Bet

Network Engines first bet that web companies would want dense, reliable servers for their growing data centers. When that market fell away, it changed course. It began building ready-to-ship appliances for software makers, who loaded their own programs and sold the boxes under their own names. That shift gave it a steadier business. Security and storage software companies became its main customers.

A New Engine

Network Engines priced its IPO at $17 a share in July 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. Its shares fell sharply after the dot-com crash as web company customers vanished. The appliance business kept the company going for another decade. In 2012 Unicom Systems acquired Network Engines. Through all its changes, it stayed based outside Boston. Its story shows how a hardware company can survive by finding a new kind of customer after its first market disappears.

Fast Lines over Old Copper

Priced June 04, 1999

In 1999 a new law and a new technology opened the local phone network to competition. The Telecommunications Act of 1996 let upstart carriers rent lines from the regional phone companies, and DSL let those old copper lines carry fast internet. Network Access Solutions was one of these competitive carriers. Based in Virginia, it focused on businesses in the Bell Atlantic region and went public in June 1999 to fund a rapid build-out.

The Original Bet

The bet was that small and medium businesses would pay for always-on internet that was far faster than dial-up and cheaper than a leased line. Network Access Solutions placed its equipment in phone company central offices and sold service directly and through internet providers. Growth required heavy spending before customers arrived, which made the business dependent on open capital markets.

After the Offering

Network Access Solutions priced its offering at $12 a share in June 1999. When the telecom bubble burst in 2000, the money that funded carriers like it dried up. The company filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in June 2002. DSL.net bought its network assets and business customers in early 2003. Its network lived on, but the company that built it did not. It joined a long list of DSL carriers that failed when the money ran out.

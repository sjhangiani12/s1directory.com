Every File, Everywhere

Priced March 23, 2018

Dropbox made files follow their owners. Put a document in a special folder, and it appeared on every other computer and phone you used. That simple trick won hundreds of millions of users, most of them on free plans. By the time Dropbox filed to go public in 2018, it was one of the largest private tech companies of its era. The question was whether enough of those users would pay.

The Original Bet

Drew Houston started Dropbox in 2007 after forgetting his USB drive on a bus trip. He and Arash Ferdowsi joined Y Combinator and built a product that hid all the hard parts of syncing. A short demo video spread widely and filled a waiting list. Their bet was that a free product people loved would turn enough of them into paying subscribers to build a real business.

Through the Shower

Dropbox priced at $21 a share in March 2018, above its expected range, and the stock jumped on its first day. Before the offering, it had moved most of its storage off Amazon's cloud and onto its own servers to cut costs. Growth slowed in later years as rivals bundled storage with other software. Dropbox remains a public company and is still led by Drew Houston.

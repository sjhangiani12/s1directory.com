Running the Rapids

Priced July 17, 2015

Rapid7 helped companies see where they were exposed. Its software scanned networks for weak spots, tested defenses and later watched for signs of an attack. It also backed Metasploit, a widely used open source tool for testing security. When Rapid7 filed to go public in 2015, major breaches had pushed security to the top of company agendas. Companies had more systems to defend each year.

The Original Bet

Rapid7 was founded in Boston in 2000 and began with vulnerability scanning. In 2009 it bought the Metasploit project, which brought it close to the community of security researchers. The bet was that defenders needed the same insight as attackers, delivered in software that IT teams could use daily. The company later moved its products to the cloud. That purchase gave Rapid7 a large following.

After the Offering

Rapid7 priced at $16 a share in July 2015 and rose on its first day. It expanded into detection and response and sold more of its products as cloud subscriptions. The company grew steadily, though profit came slowly. Rapid7 remains a public company with its headquarters in Boston. Corey Thomas led the company as chief executive through most of its public years. In 2026 it bought Kenzo Security, a small AI security startup.

The Stream of Software

Priced August 11, 1998

In 1998 software was still sold mostly in boxes on store shelves. Digital River, based in Minneapolis, believed it would soon arrive over the internet instead. The company built and ran online stores for software publishers, handled payments, and delivered the software as a download. It also ran a network of resellers that could sell those products on their own sites. Digital River went public in August 1998.

The Original Bet

Joel Ronning founded Digital River in 1994. The bet was that software makers would rather hand off e-commerce than build it, and that downloads would replace physical boxes. Digital River took a cut of each sale and handled the hard parts: payment, fraud, taxes, and delivery. It later served game makers, electronics brands, and other companies selling directly online. Its model gave publishers a ready-made store without building one themselves.

After the Offering

Digital River priced its offering at $8.50 a share in August 1998, and the stock rose sharply in the boom. It survived the crash because its revenue came from transactions. Over time it became the online store behind many large software brands. Siris Capital took Digital River private in 2015 for about $840 million. It continues to process online sales for brands around the world.

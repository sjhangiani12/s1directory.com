Counting Every Minute Online

Priced May 05, 1999

In 1999 internet providers faced a basic problem. Old telephone billing systems were built to charge for minutes of voice calls, not for web access, email accounts, or content. Portal Software sold a billing platform designed for the internet. It could track usage, apply complex pricing plans, and produce bills for millions of customers. As every carrier rushed to sell online services, Portal went public in May 1999 promising to be the cash register behind them.

The Original Bet

John Little started the company in 1985 as a consulting firm, and it built billing tools for early online services. The bet was that internet services would need flexible, real-time billing, and that carriers would buy it rather than build it. Portal's Infranet product ran on standard servers and could change pricing quickly, which carriers launching new services valued. That speed mattered in a market where new products appeared every month.

After the Offering

Portal priced its offering at $14 a share in May 1999, and its stock rose sharply in the boom. When carriers cut spending in 2001, Portal's sales fell and its losses grew. Accounting problems later forced restatements and a delisting from the Nasdaq. Oracle acquired Portal in 2006, and its billing software became part of Oracle's communications products. The billing problem Portal set out to solve never went away.

Finding the Needle Fast

Priced October 05, 2018

Elastic grew out of a piece of open source software called Elasticsearch. Developers used it to search through huge piles of text, logs and events in near real time. By 2018 it ran inside thousands of companies, often before anyone in purchasing knew it was there. Elastic's business was to sell support, hosted services and extra features on top of the free core. Its public offering tested whether that open source model could carry a large public company.

The Original Bet

Shay Banon began writing search software while building a recipe search tool for his wife, who was training as a chef. He released Elasticsearch as open source in 2010, and it spread quickly among developers. In 2012 he and his co-founders formed a company around it. The bet was that free software would win the users, and that companies would pay for help running it at scale.

Searching the Stars

Elastic priced at $36 a share in October 2018, and the stock nearly doubled on its first day. The company kept growing as logs, security and observability became its main markets. It later changed its license to stop cloud providers from offering Elasticsearch as their own service. Elastic remains a public company with headquarters split between the Netherlands and California.

The Campus in a Browser

Priced December 14, 1999

In the late 1990s, a few colleges began to offer whole courses over the internet, but most had no idea how to build or run them. eCollege, founded in Denver as Real Education, offered to do it for them. When it filed to go public at the end of 1999, online learning was a promising experiment, and the idea of earning a degree without stepping on campus still struck many people as strange.

The Original Bet

The bet was that colleges would want to reach students online but would not want to build the technology themselves. eCollege supplied the course platform, hosting and support, and in many cases shared in the tuition revenue from online programs. It aimed to be the quiet partner behind a school's brand, letting the college focus on teaching while eCollege kept the systems running.

Growing With Online Degrees

eCollege priced at $11 a share in December 1999. Its shares fell with the market, but its business grew steadily as online degree programs spread, especially at for-profit and continuing education schools. The company also bought Datamark, a marketing services firm. In 2007 Pearson agreed to buy eCollege for $22.45 a share, about $538 million, to build its digital education business.

Paperwork for a Trading Nation

Priced December 02, 2004

In 2004 China was becoming the workshop of the world, and every container leaving its ports needed paperwork. Ninetowns, based in Beijing, built software that let companies file inspection and quarantine forms online with the government agency in charge, instead of carrying stacks of paper to an office. Its American depositary shares were offered in December 2004, when investors were eager for a piece of China's growing internet and software market.

The Original Bet

Ninetowns bet that as China's trade boomed, the state would move its customs and inspection processes onto computers. By building the software that companies used to talk to the inspection system, Ninetowns placed itself in the path of a large and growing flow of filings. Its customers were thousands of exporters who needed the tools to keep goods moving through the ports.

A Quiet Exit

Ninetowns priced its ADSs at $11 in December 2004 and listed on Nasdaq under the ticker NINE. The stock fell well below its offering price in later years as the company's core business slowed and it tried new ventures. In 2012 a group of directors and officers led by chief executive Wang Shuang proposed to buy out the public shareholders. The deal closed in 2014 at $1.80 per share.

The Picture Show of Technology

Priced July 01, 1996

In 1996 the computer industry was booming, and millions of people were buying their first PC or going online for the first time. CNET set out to explain it all. It produced television shows about technology on cable channels and built a group of websites with news, reviews, and software downloads. The San Francisco company went public in July 1996, one of the first media companies built for the internet age.

The Original Bet

Halsey Minor founded CNET in 1992, and Shelby Bonnie joined him early and later ran the company. The bet was that technology was becoming a mainstream subject, worthy of its own network. CNET used television to build its brand and the web to reach readers directly. Its sites, including Download.com and News.com, became essential stops for computer users. It also sold advertising to the technology companies its readers followed.

After the Offering

CNET priced its offering at $16 a share in July 1996. It grew through the boom, bought comparison shopping site mySimon and the media company ZDNet, and survived the crash as one of the largest web publishers. CBS acquired CNET in 2008 for about $1.8 billion. The brand later passed to Red Ventures and continues today. Its reviews still guide shoppers.

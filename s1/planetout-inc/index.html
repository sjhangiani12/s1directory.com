A Home Page for a Community

Priced October 13, 2004

In the mid-1990s the web offered something many gay and lesbian people had never had: a way to find each other privately and safely. PlanetOut, founded in San Francisco, became one of the first online homes for that community. It later merged with Gay.com and added magazines such as The Advocate and Out. When it went public in October 2004, it was the largest media company aimed at LGBT readers.

The Original Bet

PlanetOut bet that a community underserved by mainstream media would gather online and that advertisers would follow. Its sites mixed news, personals and forums, and paid memberships became an important source of revenue. The company saw itself as a media brand for a whole audience, and it expanded into print and travel to reach that audience in more places. It also built a strong paid subscriber base among its users.

Turning the Page

PlanetOut priced its IPO at $9 a share in October 2004 and listed on Nasdaq under the ticker LGBT. Growth slowed as social networks and free dating sites drew users away. The print and travel businesses also struggled, and the company sold them off. In 2009 PlanetOut combined with Here Media, ending its run as an independent public company. The brands it gathered still exist in other hands today.

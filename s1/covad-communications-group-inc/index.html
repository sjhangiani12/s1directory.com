Value Carried on Copper

Priced January 21, 1999

The Telecommunications Act of 1996 forced the old Bell phone companies to rent their lines to competitors. Covad was one of the boldest companies to take up that offer. It installed DSL equipment in central offices across the country and sold fast internet over ordinary phone lines, mostly through internet providers. When Covad went public in January 1999, it was building one of the largest DSL networks in the United States.

The Original Bet

Charles McMinn, a Silicon Valley executive, co-founded Covad in 1996. The name came from "copper value added." The bet was that the regulated phone network could become a platform for new broadband carriers. Covad raced to be first into the most markets, spending heavily on equipment and staff before most customers had signed up. It hoped to win the market before the Bell companies could respond.

After the Offering

Covad priced its offering at $18 a share in January 1999, and its stock rose steeply through the year. The pace was hard to sustain. Disputes with the Bell companies, unpaid bills from failing internet providers, and the telecom crash pushed Covad into Chapter 11 in 2001. It emerged and kept operating, and Platinum Equity acquired it in 2008. Its network continued to serve business customers for years after.

Wrapped and Ready to Go

Priced September 24, 2003

RedEnvelope began in the late 1990s as an online gift shop and grew into a brand known for its red packaging and hand-picked selection. The pitch was taste and convenience: a store that did the choosing for you and shipped the gift wrapped. It went public in September 2003, a cautious time for e-commerce offerings, as one of the few online retailers willing to test the market after the crash.

The Original Bet

RedEnvelope bet that busy shoppers would pay for a well-edited gift and a strong brand. It sold a mix of engraved keepsakes, home goods and holiday items, many of them exclusive. Catalogs drove much of its traffic. The business, though, was heavily seasonal, with a large share of each year's sales landing in the weeks before Christmas. Free gift wrapping and personalized items set it apart from larger stores.

The Last Delivery

RedEnvelope priced its IPO at $14 a share in September 2003 and listed on Nasdaq. It struggled to turn a steady profit as marketing and shipping costs stayed high. In 2008 the company filed for bankruptcy. Provide Commerce, the parent of ProFlowers, bought the RedEnvelope brand and assets. The RedEnvelope name lived on as a gift brand under its new owner, sold alongside flowers and other gifts.

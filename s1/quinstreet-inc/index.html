Where the Customer Meets the Road

Priced February 10, 2010

QuinStreet sold customers, not ad space. It ran websites and campaigns that drew in people shopping for a degree, an insurance policy or a loan, then passed their interest to schools and companies that paid per qualified lead or click. In early 2010 its biggest market was for-profit education, which was booming as unemployed adults went back to school. Few consumers knew its name, though many had filled out one of its forms.

The Original Bet

Doug Valenti and Bronwyn Syiek founded QuinStreet in 1999. Their bet was that advertisers would rather pay for results than for impressions, and that a company could earn a margin by matching high-intent shoppers to buyers. QuinStreet built and bought content sites in its chosen fields and invested in the data needed to price each lead. Advertisers liked that they paid only for results, and QuinStreet carried the risk of turning ad spending into qualified leads.

After the Offering

QuinStreet priced its IPO at $15 a share in February 2010. New federal rules on for-profit colleges soon cut into its largest business, and the stock fell. The company shifted toward insurance and financial services, where demand grew. It remains publicly traded on the Nasdaq. Its model, paid for results rather than for attention, stayed much the same through the changes in its markets.

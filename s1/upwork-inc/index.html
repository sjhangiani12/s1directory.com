A Workshop Without Walls

Priced October 03, 2018

Upwork is the online marketplace where companies hire freelancers for projects of every size. A business posts a job, freelancers around the world send proposals, and Upwork handles contracts and payment. It was formed from the merger of Elance and oDesk, two pioneers of online freelance work. When it went public in 2018, many people already worked remotely for some of their income, and Upwork was the largest market for that kind of work.

The Original Bet

Elance and oDesk each started from the same idea. Skilled work does not need to happen in the same building as the client. Elance launched in 1999, and oDesk followed a few years later with tools to track remote work hours. The two rivals merged in 2013 and took the name Upwork in 2015. The combined company bet that trust, payments and reviews in one place would bring both sides back again.

Hired by the Hour

Upwork priced its shares at $15 in October 2018 and opened well above that price. The company spent the following years shifting toward larger business clients and higher fees. Remote work became normal during the 2020 pandemic, which brought new attention to the marketplace. Upwork remains a public company and one of the best known names in freelance work. Its chief executive at the offering, Stephane Kasriel, had led the company since 2015.

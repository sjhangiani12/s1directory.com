The Warehouse Without Shelves

Priced February 25, 1999

In 1999 business software still arrived in boxes, on CDs, with license keys printed on paper. Intraware, based in the San Francisco Bay Area, wanted to move that process online. It resold software from major vendors and delivered it over the internet, with the licenses tracked in one place. When Intraware went public in February 1999, it was betting that software would become something companies downloaded, not something they unpacked.

The Original Bet

The bet was that software delivery and licensing were messy enough to be a business of their own. Intraware built a service called SubscribeNet that let software makers deliver products and updates to customers, and track who was entitled to what. Over time this platform became more important than its reselling business. Its customers included large companies that bought software from many vendors.

After the Offering

Intraware priced its offering at $16 a share in February 1999. The stock rose in the boom and then fell hard. The company dropped reselling and focused on SubscribeNet, selling it to software makers as a service. Acresso Software, later renamed Flexera, acquired Intraware in January 2009 for $4.00 a share in cash. SubscribeNet lived on inside Flexera's licensing products, which still serve many software makers.

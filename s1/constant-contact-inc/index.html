Letters to the Regulars

Priced October 02, 2007

Constant Contact taught small businesses to send email newsletters. Its online service gave a florist, a dentist or a church easy templates, a place to keep a mailing list and reports on who opened each message. The company backed this with seminars and phone coaching across the country. By 2007 it had tens of thousands of customers paying monthly. Its customers were mostly very small organizations.

The Original Bet

Randy Parker founded the company in 1995 as Roving Software. The bet was that small organizations wanted to keep in touch with their customers but lacked the skills of big marketers. Gail Goodman, who became chief executive in 1999, focused the company on email marketing and built its sales engine around free trials and radio ads. Customer service and education became central to how it sold.

After the Offering

Constant Contact priced its IPO at $16 a share in October 2007 on the Nasdaq. It added social media and event tools and kept growing through the downturn. In 2016 Endurance International Group bought the company for about $1.1 billion. Endurance kept Constant Contact as a separate brand, and it later became a separate company again under private equity ownership.

A Bouquet by Wire

Priced August 02, 1999

Jim McCann bought a single flower shop in New York in 1976 and later built it into a national brand around an easy phone number. By the late 1990s, 1-800-Flowers was adding the web to the phone, and it became one of the early merchants to sell seriously online. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, it was a real business with real customers in a market crowded with promises.

The Original Bet

McCann's bet was that people sending flowers wanted one trusted brand they could reach anywhere. The phone number did that first, and the company used a network of local florists to deliver nationwide. When the web arrived, he moved early, partnering with online services and portals so that 1-800-Flowers was a familiar name before most shoppers had bought anything online.

Still Blooming

1-800-Flowers priced at $21 a share in August 1999. Its stock fell with the market, but the business kept selling through the downturn. Over the years it bought many gift brands, including Harry & David and Shari's Berries, and became a broad gifting company. It still trades on Nasdaq under the ticker FLWS. Few retailers from the 1999 class can say the same, and it remains one of the best-known gift brands in the United States.

The Small Firm at City Hall

Priced February 29, 2000

Onvia started in Seattle as a one-stop online shop for small businesses, selling supplies and services to firms too small for a purchasing department. By the time it filed to go public in early 2000, it was also pointing those businesses toward a large and confusing customer: government. Local, state and federal agencies spent huge sums every year, and finding the right bid was mostly a matter of luck and paperwork.

The Original Bet

Glenn Ballman, a young Canadian entrepreneur, founded the company in 1996. The bet was that small businesses were badly served online and would gather in one place if someone made buying and selling easier. Over time the most valuable part of that idea proved to be information: knowing which agencies were buying what, and when, so a small firm could compete for public contracts.

From Store to Signal

Onvia priced at $21 a share in March 2000, just before the dot-com market turned. The broad small-business marketplace did not survive the downturn, and the company narrowed its focus to government procurement data. It spent nearly two decades as a smaller public company selling that intelligence. In 2017 Deltek bought Onvia for $9 a share, about $70 million, and folded it into its GovWin service.

Signals on the Wind

Priced May 03, 2011

Boingo was the name on the screen when a traveler opened a laptop at the gate. The company ran paid Wi-Fi in many of the busiest airports in the United States and resold access to hotspots around the world. By 2011, smartphones were changing the business. Boingo was also building cellular antenna systems inside airports and stadiums, so that phone carriers could serve the crowds there.

The Original Bet

Sky Dayton, who had founded the internet provider EarthLink, started Boingo in 2001, when public Wi-Fi was new and scattered. His bet was that one account could work across thousands of hotspots, the way a phone worked across cell towers. Boingo won long contracts to run the networks in major airports, which gave it steady and defensible positions. Roaming deals with other hotspot operators let Boingo customers log in far beyond its own locations.

After the Offering

Boingo priced its IPO at $13.50 a share in May 2011. Free Wi-Fi spread through airports and coffee shops, so the company leaned into building wireless infrastructure for venues, carriers and military bases. In 2021 it was acquired by an investment group led by Digital Colony and taken private. Its long airport contracts and military base networks gave it steady revenue, even as consumer Wi-Fi fees faded.

Loans Under an Open Sky

Priced May 24, 2018

GreenSky made consumer loans at the point of sale. A homeowner buying a new roof or kitchen could apply on a contractor's phone and get a loan decision in seconds. Partner banks funded the loans, and GreenSky ran the technology and collected fees. When it filed to go public in 2018, fintech lenders were popular with investors, and GreenSky was one of the most profitable among them.

The Original Bet

David Zalik founded GreenSky in Atlanta in 2006. He had built and sold a software business before, and he saw that home improvement contractors lost sales when customers could not pay up front. The bet was to give merchants a simple financing tool and leave the lending itself to banks. That approach kept the loans off GreenSky's own books and let the company grow quickly with little capital.

Clouds Gather

GreenSky priced at $23 a share in May 2018, but the stock fell soon after. Investors worried about rising funding costs and the company's reliance on a few bank partners. Shares traded far below the offering price for most of its public life. In 2022 Goldman Sachs bought GreenSky for about $2.2 billion to grow its consumer lending business, then agreed to sell it the next year.

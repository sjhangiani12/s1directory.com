Mail Along the Road

Priced March 29, 1999

In 1999 email was becoming the most important application on the internet, and running it well was hard. Internet providers, phone companies, and large businesses all needed mail servers that could handle millions of accounts without failing. Critical Path offered to run email for them. Customers kept their own brand, and Critical Path handled the servers, storage, and spam. It went public in March 1999 as one of the first companies to sell email as a hosted service.

The Original Bet

David Hayden founded Critical Path in San Francisco in 1997. The bet was that email was too important and too complex for most companies to run themselves. Critical Path built large data centers for mail and signed deals with carriers and portals. Its business was an early version of what later became cloud computing. Customers paid by the mailbox, so revenue grew with every new account.

After the Offering

Critical Path priced its offering at $24 a share in March 1999, and the stock soared through 2000. Then an accounting scandal in 2001 forced restatements, and several former employees were later convicted. The company survived and shifted to selling messaging software to carriers. Its major shareholders took it private in 2008, and it later merged with Mirapoint. Openwave Messaging bought the combined company in 2013.

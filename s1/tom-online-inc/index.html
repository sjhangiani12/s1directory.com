A Portal in Every Pocket

Priced March 05, 2004

In early 2004 China had more mobile phones than any other country, and most people went online through them. TOM Online, part of the Hong Kong conglomerate TOM Group, ran a web portal but earned most of its money from wireless services: ringtones, pictures, games and text message alerts sold through China Mobile. It listed in New York and Hong Kong in March 2004, in the middle of a rush of Chinese internet offerings.

The Original Bet

TOM Online bet that Chinese users would pay small amounts, over and over, for content on their phones. The carriers collected the fees and passed a share back. The model could grow quickly, but it depended on the rules set by the carriers, who could change billing policies with little warning. Many Chinese internet companies of the time depended on the same kind of mobile revenue.

Back to the Parent

TOM Online priced its shares at the equivalent of about $15.55 per ADS in March 2004 and traded on Nasdaq and the Hong Kong exchange. Changes in China Mobile's billing rules hit the wireless business hard over the next few years. In 2007 the parent company, TOM Group, bought out the minority shareholders and took TOM Online private. Its story marks a brief era when phone carriers decided which content companies thrived in China.

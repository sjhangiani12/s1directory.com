A Canoe on Still Water

Priced July 19, 2012

KAYAK searched travel sites so travelers did not have to. A single query showed flights, hotels and rental cars from airlines, booking sites and hotel chains side by side. KAYAK earned money when users clicked through to book and from ads. It filed to go public in late 2010 but waited until 2012 to list, and by then it was one of the best known travel brands online.

The Original Bet

Steve Hafner and Paul English founded KAYAK in 2004. Both had earlier worked on travel and consumer sites. They believed travel shopping was too slow and scattered, and that clean, fast search would win. The bet was to stay a search engine and not book trips itself, which kept it neutral among sellers. Its clean design drew many loyal users.

Bought Before Long

KAYAK priced at $26 a share in July 2012 and rose on its first day. Only a few months later, Priceline agreed to buy the company for about $1.8 billion. The deal closed in 2013. KAYAK continued as a separate brand inside Priceline, now Booking Holdings. Steve Hafner stayed on to lead KAYAK for years after the sale. The brand is still widely used to compare travel prices.

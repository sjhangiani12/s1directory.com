Songs from the Café

Priced March 28, 2012

CafePress let anyone sell T-shirts, mugs and posters with their own designs. A user uploaded art, and CafePress printed each item when someone ordered it. That meant no inventory for sellers. The site hosted millions of small shops and became known for gifts, slogans and fan merchandise. When it filed to go public in 2012, it was one of the pioneers of print on demand.

The Original Bet

Fred Durham and Maheesh Jain founded CafePress in 1999. They saw that digital printing could make single items cheaply. The bet was that people would pay a premium for custom products, and that a marketplace of designers would fill the catalog. The company grew through the 2000s and moved much of its production to Kentucky. Shop owners earned a share of each sale.

Fading Ink

CafePress priced at $19 a share in March 2012. Its stock fell soon after as competition from sites like Etsy and Zazzle grew. The company sold off several businesses to focus on its core. In 2018 Snapfish bought CafePress for about $25 million. Many former shop owners moved to other print on demand sites. Under Snapfish, CafePress continued to sell custom products online.

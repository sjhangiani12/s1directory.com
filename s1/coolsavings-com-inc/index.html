Coupons Without Scissors

Priced May 19, 2000

For generations, Americans clipped coupons from Sunday newspapers. CoolSavings, based in Chicago, moved the coupon online. Members signed up, chose offers from brands and stores, and printed coupons at home. Advertisers paid CoolSavings for each shopper who saw or used an offer. The company went public in May 2000, just after internet stocks had begun to fall from their peak. Shoppers could browse offers by category and save their favorites.

The Original Bet

CoolSavings bet that coupons would be a strong lure for online shoppers and a precise tool for advertisers. Because members registered, the company knew who was using each offer. That data made it more valuable to brands than a scattered newspaper insert. The model, though, depended on online advertising budgets, which shrank sharply after 2000. It also collected detailed profiles from members who signed up. Those profiles let it target offers by place and interest.

A New Name

CoolSavings priced its IPO at $7 a share in May 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. The dot-com crash cut its advertising revenue, and the company relied on investment from the media firm Landmark Communications to stay afloat. It later moved into broader online marketing and renamed itself Q Interactive. Landmark eventually sold the business to an investment firm. Its idea of digital coupons later became common on phones and store apps.

Bonds Without the Phone Call

Priced November 04, 2004

Stocks had moved onto screens by 2004, but corporate bonds still traded mostly by telephone, one dealer at a time. MarketAxess, founded in New York in 2000, gave big investors a way to ask many dealers for prices at once and trade electronically. When it went public in November 2004, it was one of the few survivors of a crowded field of bond-trading startups from the dot-com years.

The Original Bet

The bet was that corporate bonds would follow stocks onto electronic markets, even though bonds are traded less often and in far more varieties. MarketAxess began by letting investors request quotes from several dealers in one step, which saved time and improved prices. Over time it added ways for investors to trade with each other, and it built data products from the trading flowing through its system.

A Slow Revolution

MarketAxess priced its shares at $11 in November 2004 and listed on Nasdaq. Electronic bond trading spread steadily rather than all at once, and the company grew with it, adding high-yield, emerging-market and international bonds. It became one of the leading venues for credit trading worldwide. More than twenty years after the offering, it is still public and trades as MKTX.

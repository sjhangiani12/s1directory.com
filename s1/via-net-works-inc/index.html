All Roads Lead Online

Priced February 11, 2000

In the late 1990s, much of Europe's business internet access was sold by small local providers, each with a few thousand customers. VIA NET.WORKS set out to buy many of them and join them under one name. When it filed to go public in early 2000, the plan seemed simple: buy cheaply, share costs, and sell more services to small and mid-sized firms across many countries.

The Original Bet

The bet was a roll-up. Local providers had loyal customers and local knowledge but little scale. By buying them and combining their back offices, VIA hoped to offer hosting, connectivity and other services more cheaply than any single provider could. The company bought firms in several European countries and in Latin America, aiming to become the default internet partner for small businesses.

A Turn in the Road

VIA priced at $21 a share in February 2000. Integrating so many small companies proved hard, and the market for internet stocks collapsed soon after the offering. The company spent the next years selling units, cutting costs and searching for a buyer. In 2005 it sold most of its operations, with parts going to Claranet and the remainder to Interoute.

Cover: On the Road, Jules Dupré. Art Institute of Chicago, public domain. Chosen for travelers on the road (all roads lead online).

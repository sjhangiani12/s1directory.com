The Road to the Great Market

Priced August 09, 2007

MercadoLibre built the marketplace of Latin America. Its site let people in Argentina, Brazil, Mexico and other countries buy and sell new and used goods online, and its payment service, MercadoPago, helped them trust strangers with their money. When it went public in August 2007, it was one of very few Latin American internet companies to list in New York.

The Original Bet

Marcos Galperin started MercadoLibre in 1999 while studying at Stanford Business School, and launched it in Buenos Aires with Hernán Kazah. The bet was that e-commerce in Latin America needed a local champion that understood its markets, currencies and logistics. In 2001 eBay bought a stake in the company and agreed to stay out of the region. The company later moved its base to Montevideo, Uruguay.

After the Offering

MercadoLibre priced its IPO at $18 a share in August 2007 on the Nasdaq. It built its own shipping network and turned MercadoPago into a major fintech business. It became one of the most valuable companies in Latin America and remains publicly traded. Its fintech arm now offers digital wallets, loans and payments across the region, far beyond its marketplace.

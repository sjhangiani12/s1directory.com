Bidding on the Floor

Priced September 21, 2016

The Trade Desk gave advertising agencies a trading screen. Its software let buyers bid for ad space across thousands of websites, apps and video services in real time. Unlike Google and Facebook, it did not own any media of its own, so it pitched itself as a neutral tool for buyers. When it filed to go public in 2016, ad tech stocks had fallen out of favor after several weak offerings.

The Original Bet

Jeff Green and Dave Pickles founded The Trade Desk in Ventura, California, in 2009. Green had earlier started AdECN, an ad exchange that Microsoft bought. The two saw that online advertising was turning into a market much like stock trading. They bet that buyers would want an independent platform that worked only for them, with no conflict from selling ads.

Up From the Floor

The Trade Desk priced at $18 a share in September 2016 and rose strongly on its first day. Unlike many ad tech peers, it was already profitable. The stock climbed for years as connected TV advertising grew. It remains a public company led by Jeff Green. Its customers are mostly ad agencies and large brands. The company has stayed independent and kept its focus on serving ad buyers.

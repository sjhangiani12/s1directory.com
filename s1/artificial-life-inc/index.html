The Clockwork Assistant

Priced December 17, 1998

Long before chatbots became common, Artificial Life tried to sell them. In 1998 the company offered software "robots" that could answer questions on a website, guide shoppers, or search for information on a user's behalf. The ideas drew on research in artificial intelligence and agents. The company was small, based in Boston, and its founder had roots in German AI research. It went public in December 1998 as interest in the web was surging.

The Original Bet

Eberhard Schoneburg founded the company. The bet was that websites would need intelligent assistants to help visitors, and that companies would license software agents to provide them. Artificial Life built bots for customer service, shopping, and information retrieval. The technology was early, and the products were limited by what software of the time could understand. The company's products were early steps toward the chatbots and virtual assistants that later became common.

After the Offering

Artificial Life priced its offering at $8.50 a share in December 1998. The company never grew large, and the dot-com crash hit it hard. It later moved its base to Hong Kong and turned to mobile games and applications. Its shares traded on the over-the-counter market in its later years. Its early bots now read as a preview of the conversational software that arrived decades later.

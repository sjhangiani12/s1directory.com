The Merchant's Street

Priced March 14, 2012

Demandware ran online stores for big brands. Retailers used its cloud software to build sites, manage products and process orders without running their own servers. It charged a share of the sales that passed through. When Demandware filed to go public in 2012, many retailers still ran older e-commerce software in their own data centers. Online shopping was growing quickly.

The Original Bet

Stephan Schambach founded Demandware in 2004. He had earlier founded Intershop, a German e-commerce software company. The bet was that retailers would rather rent a fully managed store in the cloud than build and maintain their own systems. Tying its fees to customer sales aligned Demandware with the retailers it served. Its customers included apparel, footwear and consumer brands. The company was based near Boston.

Bought by Salesforce

Demandware priced at $16 a share in March 2012 and rose strongly on its first day. It grew as more retailers moved to the cloud. In 2016 Salesforce bought Demandware for about $2.8 billion, and it became Salesforce Commerce Cloud. The deal was one of the largest Salesforce had made at the time. Retailers continued to use the platform under its new name.

Treasure in the Old Wires

Priced January 28, 1999

In 1999 a great deal of copper wire ran through apartment buildings, hotels, and campuses, installed for telephones. Tut Systems built products that let that wiring carry fast data as well. A hotel could offer guests internet access, or an apartment building could offer broadband, without pulling new cable. Tut also worked on home networking over phone lines. It went public in January 1999, as everyone looked for ways to reach the last few hundred feet.

The Original Bet

The bet was that the wires already in place were worth more than people thought. Rewiring a building was costly and slow, while Tut's equipment worked with what was already there. It sold to building owners, service providers, and phone companies that wanted to offer broadband in multi-tenant buildings. That approach saved time and money, and it suited the rush to wire buildings for the internet. Tut's products fit the needs of property owners who wanted to add a service without tearing open walls.

After the Offering

Tut priced its offering at $18 a share in January 1999, and the stock rose through the boom before collapsing in 2000 and 2001. The company shifted to video processing systems for telecom carriers offering television over broadband. It bought Copper Mountain Networks in 2005. Motorola acquired Tut Systems in 2007. By then Tut had become a supplier of video gear for telecom carriers, far from its roots in building wiring.

The Engine Room of Small Banks

Priced November 25, 2003

Every bank needs a core system: the software that keeps track of deposits, loans and every transaction. Large banks built their own or bought from a few giant vendors. Open Solutions, based in Glastonbury, Connecticut, focused on community banks and credit unions, offering modern software built on open, standard databases. It went public in November 2003, as smaller banks were replacing aging systems to compete online with bigger rivals.

The Original Bet

Open Solutions bet that smaller banks wanted the same kind of flexible, modern technology as the giants. Its core platform used a single shared database, which made it easier for banks to add new products and connect other software. The company grew by winning new banks and by buying other vendors that served the same market. Its customers tended to stay for years, since replacing a bank's core system is slow and risky.

Into Larger Hands

Open Solutions priced its IPO at $17 a share in November 2003 and listed on Nasdaq. It continued to buy smaller companies to widen its offering. In 2007 the private equity firms Carlyle Group and Providence Equity Partners took Open Solutions private. Fiserv, one of the largest providers of bank technology, acquired the company in 2013. Its software kept running inside many small banks and credit unions.

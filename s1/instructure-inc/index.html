A Fresh Canvas for the Classroom

Priced November 13, 2015

Instructure made Canvas, the software where teachers post assignments and students turn them in. Universities and school districts used it to run courses, grade work and share materials online. It competed with older systems that teachers often found clumsy. When Instructure filed to go public in 2015, Canvas was winning many campuses away from Blackboard, the long-time leader. Its growth among schools had been fast.

The Original Bet

Brian Whitmer and Devlin Daley started Instructure in 2008 while they were graduate students at Brigham Young University. They believed the tools schools used were hard to use and slow to improve. The bet was that a modern, open, cloud-based product would win over teachers and students first, and that schools would follow. Canvas launched in 2011 with an open source version.

Many Owners, One Canvas

Instructure priced at $16 a share in November 2015. Canvas kept gaining schools, though the company also built a corporate training product. Thoma Bravo took Instructure private in 2020, and it returned to the public market in 2021. In 2024 KKR bought the company for about $4.8 billion, and it became private again. Canvas remains one of the most widely used learning systems in American higher education.

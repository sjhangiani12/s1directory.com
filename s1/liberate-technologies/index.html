Cranes Waiting to Fly

Priced July 27, 1999

In the late 1990s, many believed the future of the internet would arrive through the television, not the computer. Liberate Technologies, which grew out of Oracle's network computer effort, made software for cable set-top boxes that would bring email, shopping and interactive programs to the living room. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, cable companies were promising interactive TV everywhere.

The Original Bet

The bet was that cable operators would need a standard software platform for interactive television, and that they would want one not controlled by Microsoft. Liberate offered that platform and signed deals with major cable and telecom companies around the world, often granting them warrants in return. If interactive TV took off, Liberate would sit inside millions of homes. It was a long bet.

Waiting for the Living Room

Liberate priced at $16 a share in July 1999 and became one of the most valuable software companies of the boom. Interactive TV arrived far more slowly than promised, and the company later restated revenue. In 2005 it sold its North American business to a Comcast-led venture and its international business to SeaChange, then paid its remaining cash to shareholders.

Broadband for the Small Towns

Priced June 04, 1999

In 1999 cable-modem internet was the fastest way to get online at home, but it reached mostly big cities. Large cable companies had partners and capital to offer it. Smaller cable systems in small towns often did not. High Speed Access Corp. filled that gap. It upgraded those systems, ran the service, handled customer support, and split the revenue with the cable operator. It went public in June 1999 with strong backing from Paul Allen's Vulcan Ventures.

The Original Bet

The bet was that broadband would spread beyond the big cities, and that someone would need to bring it there. High Speed Access offered small cable operators a turnkey service, so they did not need their own technical teams. Its largest partner was Charter Communications, another company in Paul Allen's orbit, which made the relationship both a strength and a risk.

After the Offering

High Speed Access priced its offering at $13 a share in June 1999. The model needed heavy spending in each new town, and losses mounted when capital grew scarce in 2000. In 2001 the company agreed to sell its operations to Charter, which completed the purchase in February 2002 for about $73 million in cash and assumed liabilities. The deal left the company with little more than cash and the task of winding down its affairs.

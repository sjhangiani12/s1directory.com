News That Finds You

Priced June 07, 1999

In the late 1990s, "push" was one of the most hyped ideas on the internet. Instead of making people visit websites, push software would send news, updates, and files straight to their computers. BackWeb, an Israeli company, built one of the leading push systems. By the time it went public in 1999 the consumer hype had faded, and BackWeb sold its technology to companies that needed to send information and software to their own employees.

The Original Bet

Eli Barkat co-founded BackWeb in 1995 with backing from BRM, the Israeli technology group he ran with his brother Nir. The bet was that the web should come to the user. BackWeb's software used spare network capacity to deliver content quietly, so it did not slow people down. Large companies used it to send sales materials, software patches, and alerts across their networks.

After the Offering

BackWeb priced its offering at $12 a share in June 1999. The stock rose with the market and then fell when the bubble burst and companies cut spending. BackWeb moved toward software that let laptop users work offline with web applications. Eli Barkat left the chief executive role in 2003 and returned to venture capital. BackWeb remained a small company in the years that followed.

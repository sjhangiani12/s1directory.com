A Bang Heard Briefly

Priced November 15, 2001

At the turn of the millennium, a new generation of consoles was arriving: the PlayStation 2, Xbox, GameCube and Game Boy Advance. BAM! Entertainment, founded in San Jose in 1999 by Ray Musci, set out to publish games for all of them, often using well-known licensed characters. It went public in November 2001, only weeks after the September 11 attacks, in one of the quietest markets for new offerings in years.

The Original Bet

BAM bet that familiar brands would help a young publisher stand out on crowded store shelves. It signed licenses for cartoon and movie properties and paid outside studios to build the games. That approach let it release titles quickly. It also meant paying large sums up front for licenses, with no guarantee the games would sell. It released games for nearly every major console and handheld of the period.

Game Over

BAM priced its IPO at $8 a share in November 2001 and listed on Nasdaq. Several of its games sold poorly, and the costs of licenses and development outran revenue. The company cut staff, sold and later bought back a London studio, and was delisted from Nasdaq. It continued releasing a few titles until 2005, when it went out of business after a bankruptcy filing.

Many Voices on One Line

Priced July 24, 2000

Evoke Communications, based near Boulder, Colorado, sold conference calls and online meetings to businesses at a time when both were moving from operators and switchboards to the web. Customers could set up a call themselves, share slides, and invite others without help. Evoke went public in July 2000, in the same busy month as its rival WebEx, while the market for online collaboration was still young.

The Original Bet

Evoke bet that conferencing would become a self-service product. Its system let users schedule and run their own calls and add web presentations, cutting the cost of each meeting. It sold to companies large and small. The business depended heavily on voice minutes, a market where prices kept falling. Its rival WebEx focused more on web sharing, while Evoke leaned on voice. Both were betting that meetings would move away from conference rooms.

A New Name, a New Owner

Evoke priced its IPO at $8 a share in July 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. In 2001 it renamed itself Raindance Communications and focused on its business conferencing services. Raindance stayed public for several more years. In 2006 West Corporation, a large provider of call services, acquired Raindance. Conference calling remained a large business, but one dominated by bigger players with their own networks.

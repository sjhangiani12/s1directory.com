All Hands on Deck

Priced December 17, 2014

OnDeck lent money to small businesses online. Instead of a long bank process, an owner could apply in minutes and get funds within a day. OnDeck judged credit using data from bank accounts, sales and payments. When it filed to go public in late 2014, online lending was a hot topic, and OnDeck was one of the first such lenders to reach the public market.

The Original Bet

Mitch Jacobs founded OnDeck in New York in 2006. He had run small businesses himself and found that banks often ignored them. The bet was that a business's daily cash flow told more about its credit than old-fashioned paperwork. OnDeck built its own scoring system and grew through brokers and partnerships with banks. Its loans were often short term, with daily or weekly payments.

Rough Seas

OnDeck priced at $20 a share in December 2014 and rose on its first day. The stock then fell for years as funding costs and loan losses mattered more than growth. The 2020 pandemic hit small businesses hard. Later that year Enova International bought OnDeck for about $90 million, far below its debut value. OnDeck continued as a brand inside Enova.

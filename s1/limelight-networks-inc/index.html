Every Stream in the Spotlight

Priced June 07, 2007

Limelight Networks moved the internet's heaviest files. It ran a global network of servers that delivered video streams, software downloads and web pages quickly to users around the world. Its customers included major media and technology companies. In 2007 online video was exploding, and Limelight was the main challenger to Akamai, the leader in content delivery. Its customers ranged from video sites to software and game companies.

The Original Bet

Limelight was founded in Arizona in 2001. Its bet was that a new network, built with large data centers and its own fiber links, could deliver rich media more cheaply than older designs. The company won big video customers early and grew quickly alongside them. It focused on video and large files, where speed and capacity mattered most. That focus brought fast growth, along with a few very large customers that made up much of its revenue.

After the Offering

Limelight priced its IPO at $15 a share in June 2007 on the Nasdaq. A patent lawsuit from Akamai, filed in 2006, dragged on for years. Pricing pressure kept profits thin. In 2022 Limelight bought Yahoo's Edgecast and renamed itself Edgio, which filed for bankruptcy in 2024. Through all of it, the company never matched the scale or profits of its larger rival.

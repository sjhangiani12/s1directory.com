Help Before You Ask

Priced July 18, 2000

When home and office computers broke in 2000, people called a help desk and waited. Support.com, based in Redwood City, built software that let companies diagnose and fix problems automatically or remotely. Its tools could check a computer's settings and repair common faults without a technician on site. It went public in July 2000, as companies were spending heavily on support for a fast-growing number of PCs.

The Original Bet

Support.com bet that much of tech support could be automated. Its software sat on users' machines and connected back to a company's help desk, which cut the number of calls and visits. The company first sold to large corporations. It later shifted to offering remote help services directly to consumers and through partners. Its customers included large technology companies and internet providers.

Many Turns

Support.com priced its IPO at $14 a share in July 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. It renamed itself SupportSoft for several years before returning to the Support.com name. The company moved from enterprise software into consumer tech support and outsourced customer service. In 2021 it merged with Greenidge Generation, a bitcoin mining company. The deal ended its long run as a tech support company.

Keeping the Web in Tune

Priced September 24, 1999

In 1999, a company could spend millions on a website and still have no idea how fast it loaded for real customers. Keynote Systems answered that by placing measurement computers across the internet and testing sites around the clock. When it filed to go public in the fall of 1999, slow pages were costing online businesses sales, and Keynote's speed rankings were starting to matter.

The Original Bet

The bet was that web performance would need an outside referee. Keynote measured download times and availability from many locations, the way a customer would experience them, and sold the results as a subscription service. Its public index of major sites' speed gave it visibility, and it turned performance into something executives could track and compare. It was a simple idea that gave online businesses a clear number to improve.

After the Offering

Keynote priced at $14 a share in September 1999, under longtime chief executive Umang Gupta. It raised a large amount of cash during the boom and kept much of it through the downturn, using some to buy related businesses, including mobile testing. In 2013 Thoma Bravo took Keynote private for $20 a share, about $395 million. Gupta retired as chief executive after the sale closed.

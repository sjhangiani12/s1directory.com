Bargains by the Pallet

Priced August 27, 2004

eCOST.com began as part of PC Mall, a catalog seller of computers and gear, and was spun out as its own public company in August 2004. The idea was a web store built around bargains: refurbished laptops, closeout cameras and daily deals that disappeared when stock ran out. It went public at a time when online electronics shopping was growing fast but margins were famously thin.

The Original Bet

eCOST bet that price-hunting shoppers would come back again and again for short-lived deals. It used the buying power and warehouse of its former parent to offer a wide range of tech products at low prices. The trouble with that model is that it rewards whoever is willing to earn the least on each sale, and the field was crowded. Free shipping offers and email deals kept customers returning.

Short-Lived Deals

eCOST priced its IPO at $5.80 a share in August 2004 and listed on Nasdaq. Sales grew, but losses followed and the stock fell. In early 2006 PFSweb, an e-commerce services company, completed a stock merger with eCOST. PFSweb later sold the eCOST assets back to PC Mall in 2011. The brand passed through several owners after that, while larger rivals such as Amazon and Newegg took over the market.

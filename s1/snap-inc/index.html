The Ghost in the Phone

Priced March 02, 2017

Snap built Snapchat, the app that made photos vanish. Young people used it to talk in pictures rather than text, and its Stories format was soon copied across the industry. The company called itself a camera company and sold ads between friends' posts. When Snap filed to go public in 2017, it was the largest tech offering since Alibaba, and it offered public shareholders no voting rights.

The Original Bet

Evan Spiegel and Bobby Murphy started the app in 2011 while Spiegel was a student at Stanford. Its ghost logo hinted at messages that disappeared after viewing. The bet was that people wanted a place to share casual moments without a permanent record. That idea set Snapchat apart from Facebook, and Spiegel turned down an offer from Facebook to buy the company in 2013.

From the Venice Boardwalk

Snap priced at $17 a share in March 2017 and rose sharply on its first day. The stock soon fell as Instagram copied Stories and user growth slowed. Snap rebuilt its Android app, invested in augmented reality and launched Spectacles glasses. The share price swung widely over the years. Snap remains a public company led by its founders. Its daily users later grew to several hundred million people.

A Fortress for the Family Photos

Priced August 10, 2011

Carbonite sold peace of mind. For a flat yearly price, its software quietly copied everything on a home computer to servers in a data center, so a dead hard drive or a stolen laptop no longer meant lost photos and tax returns. By the summer of 2011 broadband was everywhere, the cloud was a household word, and Carbonite had become one of the most familiar voices on American talk radio, selling backup to people who had never thought about it.

The Original Bet

David Friend and Jeff Flowers, serial founders from the Boston tech scene, bet that ordinary people would pay a small subscription to never think about backup again. Most consumers had no backup at all. Carbonite made it automatic and unlimited, and spent heavily on radio and podcast ads to reach people who would never read a tech review. Later it added plans for small businesses, which proved to be the steadier customers.

Into the Vault

Carbonite priced its IPO at $10 a share in August 2011, in a jittery market, and traded unevenly for years as consumer growth slowed. The company shifted toward business customers and bought several rivals, including Webroot in 2019. Later that year OpenText agreed to acquire Carbonite for about $1.42 billion, folding the backup pioneer into a large enterprise software group.

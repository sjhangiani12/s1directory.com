The Bridge Between Colleagues

Priced May 18, 2011

LinkedIn turned the business card and the résumé into a living network. Members kept an online profile of their career and connected with colleagues, and recruiters paid to search that map of the working world. By 2011 it had more than 100 million members. It was the first major American social network to go public, a year ahead of Facebook, and the market treated its listing as a test of the whole social media wave.

The Original Bet

Reid Hoffman, an early PayPal executive, started LinkedIn with a small group of friends from SocialNet and PayPal, and launched it in 2003. The bet was that professionals would put their real identities and work histories online if the network helped them get jobs and deals. LinkedIn made money three ways: recruiting tools, advertising and premium subscriptions. The recruiting business became the engine.

The Road to the Bell

LinkedIn priced its shares at $45 in May 2011 and listed on the New York Stock Exchange. The stock more than doubled on its first day of trading. The company grew steadily as a public company and bought Lynda.com in 2015. In 2016 Microsoft acquired LinkedIn for $26.2 billion, its largest purchase at the time, and kept it running as an independent brand.

What Is My Home Worth

Priced December 09, 2004

In 2004 the American housing market was in full boom, and homeowners were curious about what their houses might fetch. HouseValues, based near Seattle, answered that question online. A homeowner typed in an address, and a local agent followed up with a market report. Agents paid HouseValues for those contacts. The company filed to go public that December, at the height of a market that seemed like it would only go up.

The Original Bet

HouseValues bet that the internet could fill an agent's pipeline better than mailers or open houses. A curious homeowner was an early sign of a future listing, and the company sold those signals to agents by territory and subscription. As the business grew it added buyer leads and tools to help agents manage contacts. Its fortunes, though, were tied tightly to how many agents were busy and willing to spend.

After the Boom

The company priced its IPO at $15 a share in December 2004 and traded on Nasdaq under the ticker SOLD. When the housing market turned in 2006 and 2007, agents cut spending and revenue fell. HouseValues reworked itself into a software company for agents and renamed itself Market Leader. In 2013 Trulia bought Market Leader for about $355 million, and Zillow later acquired Trulia.

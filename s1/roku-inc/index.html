The Sixth River

Priced September 28, 2017

Roku put streaming on the television. Its small boxes and sticks plugged into a TV and showed apps from Netflix, Hulu and hundreds of others. Later, TV makers built Roku's software right into their sets. When Roku filed to go public in 2017, cord cutting was well underway, and the company was starting to earn more from advertising and platform fees than from hardware.

The Original Bet

Anthony Wood founded Roku in 2002. He had earlier invented an early digital video recorder. The name means six in Japanese, because Roku was the sixth company he had started. Its first streaming box, in 2008, was built with Netflix. The bet was that a neutral home screen would win viewers, and that owning that screen would be worth more than selling devices.

On the Home Screen

Roku priced at $14 a share in September 2017 and soared on its first day of trading. The stock rose many times over during the streaming boom of 2020, then fell back. The company kept adding active accounts and building its ad business. Roku remains a public company and is still led by its founder. Its software now runs inside many of the TV sets sold in the United States.

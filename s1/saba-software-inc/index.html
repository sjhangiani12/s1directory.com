A Queen's Court of Learning

Priced April 06, 2000

In the late 1990s, large companies spent heavily on employee training but had little idea what it achieved. Saba, founded in 1997 by Bobby Yazdani, built software to plan, deliver and track learning across an entire workforce. It went public in April 2000, as e-learning was one of the hottest ideas in business software and companies were moving training from classrooms to screens.

The Original Bet

Saba bet that learning would become a managed business process, tied to each employee's skills and goals. Its learning management system let companies assign courses, track completion and link training to performance. Large corporations and government agencies were early customers. The company later widened its products to cover hiring, reviews and succession planning. Its customers included some of the largest companies and agencies in the world.

Out of the Spotlight

Saba priced its IPO at $15 a share in April 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. The shares fell sharply in the crash, but the company survived and grew. In the early 2010s it faced an accounting scandal and restated years of results. In 2015 Vector Capital took Saba private, and in 2020 Cornerstone OnDemand bought it. Its name came from the ancient kingdom of Sheba.

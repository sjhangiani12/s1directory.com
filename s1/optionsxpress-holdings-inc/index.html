Fast Lane to the Options Pit

Priced January 26, 2005

Options trading once belonged to floor traders in Chicago's crowded pits. optionsXpress, founded in that city in 2000, put the same contracts in front of ordinary investors through a web browser, with tools for pricing and strategy that had been rare outside professional desks. It filed to go public in early 2005, as online brokers recovered from the dot-com crash and a new wave of active traders went looking for cheaper, faster ways to trade.

The Original Bet

The founders bet that retail investors would trade options if someone made it easy and cheap. Instead of treating options as an add-on to stock trading, optionsXpress built its whole platform around them, with chains, calculators and education aimed at self-directed traders. Low flat commissions drew active customers, and the firm earned well on each account. It stayed lean and profitable, which set it apart from many brokers of its era.

From Pit to Portfolio

The company priced its IPO at $16.50 a share in January 2005 and traded on Nasdaq as OXPS. It stayed profitable through the following years, added futures trading, and bought other brokerage businesses. In 2011 Charles Schwab acquired optionsXpress in a stock deal worth about $1 billion, folding its options tools and its active-trader customers into one of the largest brokers in the United States.

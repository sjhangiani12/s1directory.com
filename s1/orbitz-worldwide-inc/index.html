A Comet Across the Booking Sky

Priced July 19, 2007

Orbitz sold flights, hotels and vacation packages online. The site began as a project of the major American airlines, which wanted their own answer to Expedia and Travelocity. By 2007 Orbitz Worldwide also included ebookers in Europe and HotelClub in Asia Pacific, and it was one of the largest online travel companies in the world. Its name was familiar to a generation of travelers.

The Original Bet

Five big airlines formed the company in 1999 and launched the site in 2001. Their bet was that the airlines together could sell their own tickets online more cheaply than through outside agents. Orbitz won customers with a fast search engine and a clear display of fares. Travelport, owned by Blackstone, bought it in 2004 and later spun it out.

After the Offering

Orbitz Worldwide priced its IPO at $15 a share in July 2007 on the New York Stock Exchange. The recession and heavy debt soon pressed on the business, and the stock fell far below its offering price. In 2015 Expedia bought Orbitz for about $1.6 billion. Orbitz kept running as a separate brand within Expedia Group, alongside Travelocity and CheapTickets.

Faces in the Pages

Priced May 17, 2012

Facebook connected people online. Users shared photos, news and messages with friends, and the News Feed kept them coming back. By 2012 it had about 900 million users and was moving fast to earn money from mobile ads. Its public offering was one of the most anticipated in history and one of the largest. It valued the company at more than $100 billion.

The Original Bet

Mark Zuckerberg launched the site in his Harvard dorm room in 2004 with roommates and classmates. It spread to other colleges, then to everyone. Zuckerberg turned down offers to sell. The bet was that real identity and real friends would make a social network more valuable than anonymous ones. That idea gave Facebook a huge, loyal audience. Facebook opened to everyone in 2006.

A Shaky Debut

Facebook priced at $38 a share in May 2012. Technical glitches at Nasdaq marred the first day, and the stock barely held its price. Shares then fell by about half that year. Facebook bought Instagram and WhatsApp and moved to mobile ads, which brought rapid growth. In 2021 it renamed itself Meta Platforms. Mark Zuckerberg still leads the company. Its users now number in the billions.

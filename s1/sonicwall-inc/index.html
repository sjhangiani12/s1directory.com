A Wall for Every Small Office

Priced November 10, 1999

In 1999, big companies had security teams and expensive firewalls. Small offices, newly connected by DSL and cable modems, had almost nothing. SonicWALL sold them a small box that sat between their network and the internet and kept intruders out. When it filed to go public in late 1999, always-on broadband was spreading fast and so were the attacks that came with it.

The Original Bet

The brothers Sreekanth and Sudhakar Ravi started the company in 1991 as Sonic Systems, making networking products for Apple computers. The bet that defined SonicWALL was that small businesses would pay for real security if it came as a simple appliance at an affordable price. The company sold through resellers who installed and supported the boxes for local customers. That reseller network became one of its biggest strengths.

Many Owners, One Wall

SonicWALL priced at $14 a share in November 1999. It survived the downturn and remained a public company for a decade. In 2010 Thoma Bravo took it private, and in 2012 Dell bought it. When Dell sold its software businesses in 2016, SonicWALL became independent again under private equity ownership. Through all those changes, the brand and its focus on smaller customers stayed largely the same.

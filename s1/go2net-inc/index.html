Casting a Wider Net

Priced April 23, 1997

In 1997 the web had many search engines, and each one covered only part of it. A search on one engine could miss pages that another found. Go2Net's MetaCrawler sent a single query to several engines at once, then combined the results. The Seattle company added other sites and services around it. Go2Net went public in April 1997, early in the internet boom, with a small offering.

The Original Bet

Russell Horowitz founded Go2Net in 1996. The bet was that the web needed a layer on top of existing tools, one that saved people time by gathering results into one page. Go2Net licensed MetaCrawler from the University of Washington, where it began as a research project. It earned money from advertising on its growing network of sites. Go2Net also ran other web properties built around games and finance.

After the Offering

Go2Net priced its offering at $8 a share in April 1997. The stock rose many times over during the boom, helped by an investment from Paul Allen's Vulcan Ventures. In 2000 Go2Net merged with InfoSpace in a stock deal worth about $4 billion at announcement. MetaCrawler and Dogpile, which Go2Net had acquired, lived on under InfoSpace. It was one of the final big deals of the boom.

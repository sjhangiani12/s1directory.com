Lessons for the Night Shift

Priced April 11, 2000

Hospitals must train their staff constantly, from new safety rules to required certifications, and nurses on night shifts cannot easily attend a classroom. HealthStream, founded in Nashville in 1990, brought that training online. Its courses and tracking tools helped hospitals prove that staff had completed required learning. It went public in April 2000, during the dot-com era, as one of a wave of companies bringing health care onto the internet.

The Original Bet

HealthStream bet that hospitals would move required training onto the web and pay for it by subscription. Its platform delivered courses and kept records of who had completed what. Over time it added tools for scheduling, credentialing and measuring patient experience. Its focus on a single industry let it build deep relationships with hospital systems. Its customers range from small community hospitals to large health systems.

Steady Through the Years

HealthStream priced its IPO at $9 a share in April 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. Its shares fell in the dot-com crash, but the company kept growing as hospitals adopted online learning. It expanded into workforce and credentialing software. HealthStream is still public, still based in Nashville, and trades as HSTM. It is one of the few health care internet companies of 2000 still operating independently.

The Post Office on Your Desk

Priced June 25, 1999

For more than a century, buying postage meant a trip to the post office or a leased postage meter. In 1999 the U.S. Postal Service began testing a new idea: postage that a customer could buy over the internet and print with an ordinary printer. Stamps.com was one of the first companies approved to sell it. It went public that June, aiming at small businesses and home offices that mailed often but had little reason to own a meter.

The Original Bet

The bet was that a printer and an internet connection could replace the postage meter. Stamps.com handled the security the Postal Service demanded, encoding each printed stamp with a barcode that proved it was paid for. Customers paid a monthly fee for the software and service. The pitch was convenience: no lines, no meter lease, and postage ready any time of day.

After the Offering

Stamps.com priced its offering at $11 a share in June 1999, and the stock rose fast before falling hard in the crash that followed. The company cut costs, outlasted rivals, and found steady growth in shipping labels for online sellers. It bought ShipStation and other shipping tools as e-commerce grew. Thoma Bravo took Stamps.com private in 2021 for about $6.6 billion.

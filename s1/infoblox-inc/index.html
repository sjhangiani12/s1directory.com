A Light for the Harbor

Priced April 19, 2012

Infoblox managed the core services of a network. Every device needs an address, and every name like a web address needs to be turned into a number. Infoblox sold appliances and software that handled these jobs, called DNS, DHCP and IP address management. When it filed to go public in 2012, networks were growing more complex, and many companies still tracked addresses in spreadsheets.

The Original Bet

Stuart Bailey founded Infoblox in Chicago in 1999. He saw that these basic network services were usually built from free software and maintained by hand. The bet was that a dedicated appliance would make them easier to run and more reliable. The company later moved its headquarters to Silicon Valley. Its customers included large enterprises, service providers and government agencies.

Into Private Hands

Infoblox priced at $16 a share in April 2012 and rose on its first day. It added security products built on its DNS knowledge. Growth slowed in later years. In 2016 Vista Equity Partners bought the company for about $1.6 billion and took it private. Its DNS security tools became a growing part of the business. Infoblox continues as a private company focused on networking and security.

The Tallest Tree in the Forest

Priced October 22, 1999

In 1999, carriers were laying fiber as fast as they could, convinced internet traffic would double every few months. Sycamore Networks promised to make that fiber smarter, with optical switches that could set up and change high-speed connections through software instead of by hand. When it filed to go public in the fall of 1999, few companies captured the fiber-optic dream so completely.

The Original Bet

Desh Deshpande and Daniel Smith, who had built Cascade Communications together, founded Sycamore in 1998. Their bet was that optical networks needed intelligence. Instead of fixed connections configured over weeks, carriers could provision bandwidth in minutes with Sycamore's switches. That promised to turn raw fiber into a flexible service and to save carriers large sums in equipment and labor. Carriers lined up to try it.

Peak and Fall

Sycamore priced at $38 a share in October 1999 and closed its first day above $184, giving a year-old company a market value in the tens of billions. When the telecom bubble burst, carrier spending collapsed and so did Sycamore's sales. The company held large cash reserves for years. In 2013 it sold its remaining business and began to dissolve, returning cash to shareholders.

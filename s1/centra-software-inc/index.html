A Classroom With No Walls

Priced February 02, 2000

Corporate training in the 1990s usually meant flying employees to a hotel ballroom. Centra offered another way: a virtual classroom where an instructor could speak, show slides, and take questions from people at their own desks. When it filed to go public in early 2000, e-learning was a hot category, and the idea of live teaching over ordinary internet connections felt almost futuristic.

The Original Bet

Leon Navickas founded Centra in 1995 near Boston. The bet was that live, interactive learning could work over the internet if the software handled voice, slides and participation well on slow connections. Companies spent large sums on travel for training, and Centra argued it could cut those costs while reaching more employees, more often, wherever they were. Its customers were mostly large companies with workforces spread across many offices.

Ahead of Its Time

Centra priced at $14 a share in February 2000. The company kept building its virtual classroom and meeting tools through the downturn, but it faced growing competition from web conferencing firms with simpler products. In 2006 the learning software company Saba acquired Centra in a deal valued at about $60 million. Years later, live online classes became an everyday part of work and school.

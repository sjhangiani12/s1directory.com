The Door to Every Mortgage

Priced April 14, 2011

Ellie Mae built the software behind the American mortgage. Its Encompass system let lenders and brokers take in an application, check it, price it and close it, while its network connected them to the appraisers, credit bureaus and investors involved in every loan. It filed to go public in 2011, while the housing market was still recovering, and few investors wanted anything to do with mortgages.

The Original Bet

Sig Anderman and Limin Hu started the company in 1997, when a mortgage still traveled by fax and courier. They bet that loan origination could move onto a single shared platform, and that lenders would pay per loan for it. Ellie Mae grew by selling Encompass to small and midsize lenders, then to larger ones as regulation made careful records essential.

After the Offering

Ellie Mae priced its IPO at $6 a share in April 2011, below its planned range, and listed on the NYSE Amex exchange. As refinancing and new rules drove lenders to its software, the stock rose many times over. Thoma Bravo took the company private in 2019 for about $3.7 billion. In 2020 Intercontinental Exchange bought it for about $11 billion.

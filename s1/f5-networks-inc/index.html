Calm at the Eye of the Storm

Priced June 04, 1999

In 1999 the busiest websites were learning that one server was never enough. Traffic arrived in surges, and a single overloaded machine could take a whole site down. F5 Networks sold a box that sat in front of a group of servers and sent each visitor to the one best able to respond. The company named itself after the strongest class of tornado, and it went public as websites raced to handle the flood of new users.

The Original Bet

Michael Almquist and Jeff Hussey founded F5 in Seattle in 1996. The bet was that websites would become critical services that could not fail. Their product, BIG-IP, checked the health of each server and routed traffic around failures. It turned a rack of ordinary machines into one reliable website. Online retailers, portals, and banks were among the early customers. Its customers needed their sites up at every hour of the day.

After the Offering

F5 priced its offering at $10 a share in June 1999. The stock climbed through the boom and then fell sharply in 2000 and 2001, when many of its dot-com customers disappeared. F5 rebuilt around large enterprises and service providers and added security and application delivery features. It became one of the long-term survivors of its class and remains a public company today.

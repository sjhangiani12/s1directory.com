Gathering the Many to Give

Priced April 29, 2010

Convio helped charities raise money on the internet. Its hosted software ran donation pages, email campaigns, event registrations and donor databases for nonprofits of every size, from local food banks to national health charities. By 2010 online giving was growing every year, and charities were learning to treat their websites as a front door for supporters. Its customers ranged from small community groups to some of the largest charities in the country.

The Original Bet

Convio started in Austin in 1999 with a bet that nonprofits would need the same online tools that retailers were adopting, but could not build them alone. It sold its software as a subscription service, years before that model was common. Charity walks and runs, where participants raise money from friends online, became one of its signature products. Its tools also helped charities keep in touch with donors between campaigns, through email newsletters and online advocacy.

After the Offering

Convio priced its IPO at $9 a share in April 2010. It grew modestly as a public company in a market led by Blackbaud, the larger nonprofit software maker. In 2012 Blackbaud agreed to buy Convio for about $275 million and combined the two product lines. Blackbaud kept parts of the Convio platform, which became the basis of its Luminate products for large nonprofits. The deal joined the two best-known names in nonprofit software.

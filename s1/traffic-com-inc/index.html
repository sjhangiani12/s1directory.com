Reading the Flow of the City

Priced January 25, 2006

Traffic.com told drivers where the jams were. It installed its own sensors along major roads in dozens of American cities, combined that data with other sources and sent live traffic reports to radio and TV stations, websites, phones and in-car navigation. In early 2006 GPS devices were spreading fast, and live traffic was the feature everyone wanted next. Its reports reached millions of listeners each day.

The Original Bet

Traffic.com began in 1998 with a bet that accurate, real-time traffic data would be valuable to many outlets at once. Building its own sensor network gave it data rivals did not have. It earned money from licensing that data and from selling advertising in the traffic reports it produced for broadcasters. Selling the same data many times over was the core of its plan.

After the Offering

Traffic.com priced its IPO at $12 a share in January 2006 on the Nasdaq. The company struggled to turn its costly sensor network into a profit. Within a year Navteq, the digital map maker, agreed to buy it, and the deal closed in 2007 at about $179 million. Navteq, later bought by Nokia, folded its traffic data into the maps used by car navigation systems.

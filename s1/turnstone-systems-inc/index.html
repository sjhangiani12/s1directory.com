A Shorebird on the Copper Line

Priced February 01, 2000

In the late 1990s, phone companies and their new competitors raced to sell DSL, broadband delivered over old copper phone lines. Every line had to be tested and connected by hand in crowded central offices. Turnstone Systems built equipment that automated that work. When it filed to go public at the start of 2000, DSL was spreading fast and its customers were raising money at a remarkable pace.

The Original Bet

The bet was that the DSL boom would create a bottleneck in the central office, where technicians had to connect and test each copper line. Turnstone's equipment let carriers switch and test lines remotely, saving truck rolls and time. Many of its biggest customers were the competitive local carriers that had sprung up after the Telecommunications Act of 1996. Its fortunes were tied closely to how fast those customers could grow.

When the Tide Went Out

Turnstone priced at $29 a share in February 2000 and was richly valued at first. Within a year many of the young DSL carriers it served ran out of money and failed, taking much of Turnstone's demand with them. The company decided to wind down rather than chase a shrinking market. Shareholders approved a liquidation, and the company returned its remaining cash in a series of distributions.

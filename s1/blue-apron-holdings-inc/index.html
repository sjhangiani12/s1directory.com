Dinner in a Box

Priced June 29, 2017

Blue Apron mailed dinner. Each week a box arrived with fresh ingredients in exact amounts and illustrated recipe cards. Customers cooked the meal themselves and skipped the grocery store. The company led a wave of meal kit startups. When it filed to go public in June 2017, Amazon had just agreed to buy Whole Foods, and investors suddenly worried about competition in food.

The Original Bet

Matt Salzberg, Ilia Papas and Matt Wadiak started Blue Apron in New York in 2012. Wadiak was a chef, and the founders wanted to make cooking at home easier and less wasteful. They built their own warehouses and supply chain to buy food directly from farms. The bet was that a subscription would keep customers cooking week after week. Meal kits looked like a fresh answer to the daily question of dinner.

Cooling on the Counter

Blue Apron cut its price range and priced at $10 a share in late June 2017. The stock fell steadily as customers canceled and marketing costs stayed high. The company did a reverse stock split and changed leaders several times. In 2023 Wonder Group, the food hall and delivery startup, bought Blue Apron for about $103 million. Under Wonder, Blue Apron kept selling meal kits.

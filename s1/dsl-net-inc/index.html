Fast Lines on Old Wires

Priced October 06, 1999

In 1999, most small businesses still connected to the internet by dial-up, or paid dearly for a dedicated line. DSL promised fast, always-on access over the copper phone lines they already had. DSL.net was one of the young carriers racing to sell it. When it filed to go public in the fall of 1999, the market was wide open and investors were eager to fund the buildout.

The Original Bet

The bet was that new rules from the Telecommunications Act of 1996 would let small carriers compete with the regional phone giants. DSL.net placed its own equipment in phone company central offices and leased the copper lines to reach customers. It focused on small businesses in smaller cities and towns, where it hoped to face less competition. It was a capital-hungry plan that depended on cheap money.

After the Offering

DSL.net priced at $7.50 a share in October 1999. The DSL boom turned to bust within two years, as capital dried up and many competitive carriers went bankrupt. DSL.net cut back, survived on fresh financing, and kept serving business customers. In 2007 it merged into a subsidiary of MegaPath, an internet provider that already controlled it, and its public shares stopped trading.

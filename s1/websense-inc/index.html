A Watchful Eye on the Wire

Priced March 27, 2000

When offices connected to the internet in the late 1990s, employers soon found staff browsing shopping, sports and worse during work hours. Websense, based in San Diego, built software that sat on a company's network and blocked or logged visits to sites by category. Its database classified millions of web pages. It went public in March 2000, as businesses rushed to put rules around their new internet connections.

The Original Bet

Websense bet that every company with an internet connection would need a way to manage how it was used. It sold subscriptions to its constantly updated list of categorized sites. That recurring revenue made the business steady. Over time it moved into broader security, protecting against malware and data leaks as well as unwanted browsing. Schools and government agencies also became large customers.

Bought and Rebuilt

Websense priced its IPO at $18 a share in March 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. It was one of the few offerings of that month to grow into a lasting business. In 2013 Thoma Bravo took Websense private for about $906 million. It later combined with Raytheon's cyber business and was renamed Forcepoint. The brand lives on in the security products that followed.

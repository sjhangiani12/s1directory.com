A Front Door for the Company

Priced June 04, 2002

In the late 1990s, people at work had to hunt across dozens of systems for the information they needed. Plumtree, founded in San Francisco, offered a single web page for each employee, built from small windows called portlets that drew data from many sources. The idea borrowed from consumer portals like My Yahoo and brought it inside the firewall. Plumtree went public in June 2002, a difficult moment for any technology offering.

The Original Bet

Plumtree bet that companies would want one front door to all their software. Its portal could pull in content from different vendors, so large organizations with mixed systems found it useful. Big customers, including government agencies, adopted it widely. Co-founder Glenn Kelman later became the chief executive of Redfin. Its portals often became the internal home page that thousands of employees saw each morning.

Bought by a Bigger Player

Plumtree priced its IPO at $8.50 a share in June 2002 and listed on Nasdaq. It was one of very few software companies to go public that year, and it grew modestly as the economy recovered. Larger vendors began bundling portals with their own platforms. In 2005 BEA Systems acquired Plumtree for about $200 million. BEA folded Plumtree into its own portal products, and Oracle later bought BEA.

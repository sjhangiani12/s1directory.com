Calls That Ride the River

Priced November 22, 1999

In the late 1990s, an international phone call could cost dollars a minute. deltathree was one of the early companies to carry those calls over the internet instead, slashing the price. Controlled at the time by RSL Communications, it filed to go public in late 1999, when internet telephony was still crackly and new, and long distance carriers were only starting to worry.

The Original Bet

The bet was that voice would become just another kind of internet traffic. By turning calls into data packets and routing them over its own network, deltathree could undercut traditional carriers, especially on international routes. It sold services both to consumers, through a PC-to-phone product, and to other carriers and businesses that wanted cheap capacity. The company built its own network for carrying calls rather than relying only on the public internet.

A Long, Quiet Decline

deltathree priced at $15 a share in November 1999. Internet calling did take off, but prices fell to almost nothing and larger players such as Skype changed the market. deltathree shrank over the decade that followed. In 2009 D4 Holdings, an investment fund linked to the owners of ACN, bought a majority stake for about $1.2 million. By then it was a small company carrying heavy losses.

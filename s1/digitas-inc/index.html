Marketing by Hand and Data

Priced March 13, 2000

Digitas began in 1980 as Bronner Slosberg Humphrey, a Boston direct-mail agency founded by Michael Bronner. By the late 1990s it had reinvented itself as a digital marketing firm, helping large companies use data to reach customers by mail, email and the web. It went public in March 2000, as big brands were scrambling to figure out how to market online.

The Original Bet

Digitas bet that marketing would become more personal and measurable, and that big companies would need a partner to make sense of their customer data. It combined strategy, data analysis and creative work in one firm. Its clients included banks, telecom companies and car makers, and they spent heavily on its services. Its work blended the discipline of direct mail with the reach of the web.

Into a Global Network

Digitas priced its IPO at $24 a share in March 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. Revenue fell in the downturn, but the company recovered as online advertising grew. It bought other agencies, including Modem Media. In 2007 Publicis Groupe acquired Digitas for about $1.3 billion, and it became a core part of Publicis's digital business. The Digitas name is still used by the agency today.

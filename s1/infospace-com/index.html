A Directory Among the Stars

Priced December 15, 1998

In 1998 many websites wanted yellow pages, white pages, maps, and news, but few wanted to build them. InfoSpace supplied those services to other sites, which ran them under their own names. It was part of the plumbing behind many portals. When it went public in December 1998, InfoSpace was a young Seattle-area company growing fast on deals with major websites and carriers.

The Original Bet

Naveen Jain, a former Microsoft manager, founded InfoSpace in 1996. The bet was to stay behind the scenes, licensing content and services to sites that already had users. InfoSpace earned money from advertising and fees as those sites grew. It later supplied similar services to wireless carriers as phones began to reach the internet. This model kept costs low and let the company grow quickly through partnerships.

After the Offering

InfoSpace priced its offering at $15 a share in December 1998, and the stock rose dramatically. By early 2000 it was among the most valuable internet companies, and then it lost most of that value. It bought Go2Net in 2000. Lawsuits and losses followed. InfoSpace later focused on search through its Dogpile brand and renamed itself Blucora in 2012. The new name reflected a move into tax and wealth services.

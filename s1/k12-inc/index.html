The Schoolroom at the Kitchen Table

Priced December 12, 2007

K12 brought public school into the home. It created an online curriculum and ran virtual charter schools for states and districts, so students could take a full course load from a computer at home with certified teachers on the other end. When it went public in December 2007, online learning was still unfamiliar to most parents, and the idea of a public company running schools drew sharp debate.

The Original Bet

Ron Packard, a former banker and McKinsey consultant, founded K12 in 2000 with backing from Knowledge Universe, and former Education Secretary William Bennett was its first chairman. The bet was that families wanted more choice in schooling, and that a strong online curriculum could serve students anywhere. K12 earned most of its revenue from public funds that followed students to its schools.

After the Offering

K12 priced its IPO at $18 a share in December 2007 on the New York Stock Exchange. Enrollment grew for years, along with criticism of student results at some of its schools. In 2020 the company renamed itself Stride, and the pandemic sent enrollment sharply higher. It remains publicly traded under its original ticker. Its business now spans online public schools, private online schools and career training programs.

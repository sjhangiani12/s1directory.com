Flying in Formation

Priced August 04, 2005

Eschelon Telecom sold phone lines, internet access and phone systems to small and midsize businesses. It operated in cities across the West and Midwest, from Minneapolis to Seattle and Phoenix, competing with the large regional Bell company. It filed to go public in 2005, after the telecom crash had wiped out many of its peers. Its customers were mostly small and midsize businesses in growing cities.

The Original Bet

Eschelon began in 1996, soon after the Telecommunications Act opened local phone markets to competition. Its bet was that small businesses wanted one local provider with good service for all their communications. It leased parts of the incumbent's network and bundled phone equipment with its lines. Like many competitive carriers of its era, it depended on access rules that regulators could change.

After the Offering

Eschelon priced its IPO at $14 a share in August 2005 on the Nasdaq. It grew through acquisitions of smaller carriers. In 2007 Integra Telecom bought Eschelon for about $710 million, including debt. The merger made Integra one of the largest competitive carriers in the western United States. The Eschelon name faded as its customers and networks were folded into Integra.

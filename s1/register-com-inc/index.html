Every Address Has an Owner

Priced March 02, 2000

In 1999, owning a .com felt like owning land in a city still being drawn. Register.com was one of the first companies allowed to compete with Network Solutions in registering those names, and it moved quickly to make the process friendly for small businesses and ordinary people. It filed to go public in early 2000, when millions of new names were being claimed every year and the land rush looked like it might never end.

The Original Bet

The Forman brothers started out running an internet service business in New York and saw that the hardest part for most customers was the first step: getting a name. When the domain registry opened to competition, they bet that a clean, fast storefront for names could become the front door to everything else a small business needed online, from email to hosting to a basic website.

After the Land Rush

Register.com priced at $24 a share in March 2000. Competition among registrars soon drove name prices down, and the company spent the following years leaning on add-on services to hold its customers. Richard Forman stepped down as chief executive in 2003. In 2005, Vector Capital agreed to buy the company for $7.81 a share in cash, a deal valued at about $200 million.

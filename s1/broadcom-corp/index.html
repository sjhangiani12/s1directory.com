Lightning in Silicon

Priced April 16, 1998

In 1998 the race to bring broadband into homes depended on chips that could send data over cable TV lines and phone wires. Broadcom designed many of those chips. Its products sat inside cable modems, digital set-top boxes, and networking gear, turning messy analog signals into clean digital data. Broadcom did not run its own factories, it designed chips and paid others to make them. It went public in April 1998.

The Original Bet

Henry Samueli, a UCLA professor, and Henry Nicholas, his former student, founded Broadcom in 1991. The bet was that broadband would need complete systems on a chip, combining many functions in one piece of silicon. That approach cut costs for equipment makers and made Broadcom a key supplier for cable modems and Ethernet. Broadcom also built a reputation for hiring top engineers and for aggressive competition.

After the Offering

Broadcom priced its offering at $24 a share in April 1998, and the stock soared with the broadband boom. It grew into one of the largest chip companies in the world, adding Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and networking chips. In 2016 Avago Technologies acquired Broadcom for about $37 billion. The combined company took the Broadcom name. The deal was one of the largest in the history of the chip industry.

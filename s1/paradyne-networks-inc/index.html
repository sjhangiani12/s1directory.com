Lightning in the Copper

Priced July 16, 1999

Paradyne had made data communications equipment for decades, first as an independent company and later as part of AT&T and Lucent. By the late 1990s it was an early leader in DSL, the technology for sending broadband over ordinary copper phone lines. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, phone companies were rushing to sell DSL, and Paradyne's equipment sat at the heart of many deployments.

The Original Bet

After Texas Pacific Group bought Paradyne from Lucent in 1996, the bet was that a focused company could move faster than a giant parent. Paradyne pushed its broadband access products, which let carriers reuse the copper already running into homes and offices. Fast data over old wires promised to turn the phone network into a broadband network without digging new lines.

After the Offering

Paradyne priced at $17 a share in July 1999 and soared during the DSL boom. When many young DSL carriers failed in 2000 and 2001, demand for its equipment fell sharply. The company cut costs and kept selling broadband access gear to carriers. In 2005 Zhone Technologies bought Paradyne in an all-stock deal valued at about $180 million. Its equipment lived on in Zhone's broadband access line.

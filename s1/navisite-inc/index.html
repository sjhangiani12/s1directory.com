Steering the Servers

Priced October 22, 1999

By 1999, every company wanted a website, and few wanted to run the servers behind it. NaviSite, born inside the internet holding company CMGI, offered to host and manage those sites in its own data centers. When it filed to go public in the fall of 1999, outsourced hosting looked like a sure way to profit from the web without betting on any single website.

The Original Bet

NaviSite began as the in-house hosting operation for CMGI's family of internet companies. The bet was that this skill could become a business of its own, serving outside customers who needed reliable, managed hosting. Customers would rent space, bandwidth and expertise instead of building data centers, and NaviSite would earn steady monthly fees. It was a way to profit from the web without betting on any single website's success.

Steady Through Rough Water

NaviSite priced at $14 a share in October 1999. Its dot-com customers vanished in the crash, and the company went through years of losses, restructuring and acquisitions. It gradually shifted toward managed services and application hosting for larger businesses. In 2011 Time Warner Cable bought NaviSite for about $230 million to expand its business services. For a company born in the dot-com boom, it was a long and steady survival.

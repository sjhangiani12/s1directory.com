One Login for the Road

Priced July 23, 2003

Before smartphones and free Wi-Fi, a business traveler's laptop was only as useful as the next phone jack. iPass built a global network of access points by striking deals with hundreds of internet providers, then sold companies one simple account that worked almost anywhere. Its software found a local connection and handled billing behind the scenes. iPass went public in July 2003, one of the first tech offerings to do well after the crash.

The Original Bet

iPass bet that it could stitch together other companies' networks into one service and sell it to corporate IT departments. It owned little infrastructure of its own. Its value lay in the agreements, the client software and the security that made remote access safe for large employers. As travelers moved from dial-up to hotel broadband and Wi-Fi, iPass followed them. Large corporations signed on to give their whole traveling workforce one way to connect.

A Long Way From Dial-Up

iPass priced its IPO at $14 a share in July 2003 and listed on Nasdaq. The shares rose strongly at first. Over the next decade, free Wi-Fi and mobile data eroded the need for its service, and the company shifted toward Wi-Fi software and data. Pareteum, a telecom software company, acquired iPass in early 2019. By then, its original dial-up service was a memory.

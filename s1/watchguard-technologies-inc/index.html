The Sentinel at the Gate

Priced July 30, 1999

As small businesses connected to the internet full time in the late 1990s, they became easy targets. WatchGuard, which started in Seattle as Seattle Software Labs, sold them a bright red security appliance called the Firebox that stood guard between their network and the outside world. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, network security was moving from the big enterprise into every office.

The Original Bet

The bet was that small and mid-sized companies needed serious network security but could not afford complex systems or expert staff. WatchGuard built firewalls into simple appliances that resellers could install and manage. It also sold subscriptions for updates and services, so each box brought recurring revenue as long as the customer kept it. The red Firebox became a familiar sight in small server closets.

Standing Guard

WatchGuard priced at $13 a share in July 1999. It survived the crash but struggled to grow profitably as competition increased. In 2006 Vector Capital and Francisco Partners took it private for about $151 million. As a private company it kept growing, expanding into wireless, endpoint and identity security, and it remains in business today under Vector Capital's ownership. It is still based in Seattle.

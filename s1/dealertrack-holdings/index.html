Off to the Races

Priced December 12, 2005

DealerTrack connected car dealers to the banks that financed their customers. Before it, a dealer faxed a buyer's credit application to lenders one at a time. DealerTrack's network let the dealer send it to many lenders at once and get answers back in minutes. By 2005 a large share of American franchised dealers used it, and the company was selling more software to run the rest of the dealership.

The Original Bet

DealerTrack was formed in 2001 with backing from several large auto lenders. The bet was that a neutral online network for credit applications would speed up car sales for everyone. Lenders paid a fee for each application, and dealers signed on because the system was simple and saved time on the showroom floor. Its scale soon became an advantage, since every new lender made the network more useful to dealers.

After the Offering

DealerTrack priced its IPO at $17 a share in December 2005 on the Nasdaq. It grew through many acquisitions, adding dealer management systems, compliance tools and Dealer.com. In 2015 Cox Automotive bought DealerTrack for about $4 billion. Cox combined it with brands such as Autotrader and Kelley Blue Book, building one of the largest companies in automotive software. Many dealers still use its tools today.

A Knight for the Database

Priced August 13, 1999

Behind every large business application sat a database, and keeping it fast and healthy was a full-time job. Quest Software made tools for the people who did that job, beginning with Oracle databases. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, companies were running more critical systems than ever, and the administrators keeping them alive were stretched thin.

The Original Bet

Quest was founded in 1987 in Southern California. The bet that shaped it was simple: database and application administrators needed better tools than the vendors supplied. Quest sold practical utilities, such as its TOAD tool for Oracle developers, that solved daily problems. Many of its products spread inside companies from the ground up, one administrator at a time. It later moved its headquarters within Orange County.

Growth by Acquisition

Quest priced at $14 a share in August 1999. Unlike many peers, it kept growing after the crash, buying dozens of smaller software companies and expanding into Windows management, security and data protection. It became a large, profitable software company. In 2012, after a bidding contest with private equity firms, Dell bought Quest for about $2.4 billion. Quest's name later returned as an independent software company after Dell sold the business.

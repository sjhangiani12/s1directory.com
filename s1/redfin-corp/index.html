Swimming Against the Current

Priced July 28, 2017

Redfin tried to rebuild the real estate agent around technology. Its website showed listings with maps and data, and its own salaried agents helped people buy and sell for lower fees. Most brokerages paid agents by commission and treated software as an afterthought. When Redfin filed to go public in 2017, the housing market was strong, and the company was one of the few online brokerages that had lasted.

The Original Bet

David Eraker and Michael Dougherty started Redfin in Seattle in 2004 as a map-based home search site. Glenn Kelman joined as chief executive soon after and turned it into a full brokerage. The bet was that customers wanted honest data and lower costs, and that employees with salaries and reviews would serve them better than independent agents chasing commissions. Glenn Kelman led the company for about two decades.

The Market Turns

Redfin priced at $15 a share in July 2017 and rose on its first day. It later tried buying and reselling homes directly, then shut that business in 2022 as rates rose. The housing slowdown hit revenue and the stock. In 2025 Rocket Companies, the mortgage lender, bought Redfin in an all-stock deal valued at about $1.75 billion. The Redfin brand continues under Rocket.

Tending the Long Harvest

Priced July 28, 2010

Envestnet worked behind the financial adviser's desk. Its platform let advisers open accounts, build portfolios from many managers, rebalance them and report to clients, all in one place. As more advisers left big brokerages to go independent, they needed the tools those firms had provided. Envestnet filed to go public in the summer of 2010, as markets were recovering from the crisis.

The Original Bet

Judson Bergman started Envestnet in 1999 with a bet on the independent adviser. He saw that wealth management was moving toward fee-based accounts and away from commissions, and that advisers would pay for technology that made it run. Envestnet charged a small fee on the assets on its platform, so it grew as markets and its adviser base grew. Its open architecture let advisers choose from many outside money managers, which set it apart from captive brokerage platforms.

After the Offering

Envestnet priced its IPO at $9 a share in July 2010 and listed on the New York Stock Exchange. It grew through acquisitions, including the data business Yodlee in 2015, and assets on its platform rose into the trillions of dollars. In 2024 Bain Capital took the company private in a deal valued at about $4.5 billion. The Yodlee deal gave Envestnet a large store of financial account data, which it sold to banks and fintech companies.

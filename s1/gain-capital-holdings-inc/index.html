Every Currency, Every Hour

Priced December 15, 2010

GAIN Capital opened the currency market to individual traders. Through FOREX.com and other platforms, customers could trade dollars, euros and yen from home, with leverage, around the clock. By late 2010 retail forex was a fast-growing and lightly understood corner of finance. New American rules were just beginning to cap leverage and tighten oversight. Most of its customers were individuals trading from home, drawn by low minimums and round-the-clock markets.

The Original Bet

Mark Galant founded GAIN in 1999 with a simple idea: the professional foreign exchange market could be packaged for individuals online. The company acted as the counterparty to many customer trades and earned money from spreads. It grew in the United States, then expanded into the United Kingdom, Japan and other markets. Retail forex was young and loosely regulated when GAIN started, and the company grew into one of the largest brokers in the field.

After the Offering

GAIN priced its IPO at $9 a share in December 2010 and listed on the New York Stock Exchange. It bought rivals including GFT and City Index, but tighter regulation and low currency volatility weighed on the business. In 2020 it was acquired by INTL FCStone, which renamed itself StoneX. Its share price spent most of the decade below the offering price, and the sale to StoneX ended its run as a public company.

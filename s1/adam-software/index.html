The Body, Layer by Layer

Priced November 10, 1995

In 1995 the CD-ROM was the new medium of learning, and A.D.A.M. used it to open the human body. Its software let students peel back layers of anatomy, from skin to bone, on a personal computer. The name stood for Animated Dissection of Anatomy for Medicine. The Atlanta company sold to schools, medical students, and families, and it went public in November 1995, just as the web began to draw attention away from CD-ROMs.

The Original Bet

A.D.A.M. was founded in Atlanta in 1990. The bet was that detailed medical illustration, delivered by computer, could teach anatomy better than textbooks. The company built a large library of accurate images and animations. It sold versions for medical schools and simpler ones for home and school use. The images became its main asset, and the company would reuse them for decades in new formats.

After the Offering

A.D.A.M. priced its offering at $12 a share in November 1995. As CD-ROMs faded, the company turned its library into online health content, which it licensed to hospitals, health sites, and insurers. It later added benefits technology for employers. Ebix, the Atlanta insurance software company, acquired A.D.A.M. in 2011 for about $66 million. Its anatomy library outlived the medium it was built for.

The Plumbing Behind the Screen

Priced February 14, 2000

Savvis sold something most people never saw: the private networks and data centers that carried business traffic. Banks, trading firms and large companies needed connections faster and more reliable than the public internet. When Savvis filed to go public in early 2000, it was closely tied to Bridge Information Systems, a financial data company, and infrastructure was where many investors thought the safest internet money lay.

The Original Bet

The bet was that businesses would pay a premium for a managed network that avoided the congested public exchange points. Savvis routed traffic over its own backbone and ran the data centers where customers placed their servers. It was a capital-heavy business, built on the assumption that demand for bandwidth and hosting would keep rising fast enough to fill the capacity.

Out of the Wreckage

Savvis priced at $24 a share in February 2000. The telecom bust that followed nearly broke it, and the bankruptcy of Bridge in 2001 added more strain. Savvis survived, bought assets from failed rivals, and grew into a major hosting and early cloud provider. In 2011 CenturyLink bought the company in a deal valued at about $3.2 billion, including debt.

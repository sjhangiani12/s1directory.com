Searching Inside the Picture

Priced June 28, 2000

In 2000, video was spreading online, but it was nearly impossible to search. A news archive might hold thousands of hours of tape with only a few written notes. Virage, based in San Mateo, built software that watched and listened to video, logged the speech and scenes, and made the footage searchable like text. It went public in June 2000, just as media companies began moving archives to the web.

The Original Bet

Virage bet that video would become as searchable as documents. Its software combined speech recognition, face recognition and scene detection to create an index of each clip. Broadcasters, government agencies and corporations used it to find footage quickly. The market for such tools grew more slowly than the company hoped. It also offered hosted services that let websites publish and search video clips.

A Turn in the Road

Virage priced its IPO at $11 a share in June 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. Like many young software companies, it struggled as tech spending fell after 2001. In 2003 Autonomy, the British search software company, bought Virage and added its video tools to its own products. Its ideas about video search were early, and later services built on them at a much larger scale.

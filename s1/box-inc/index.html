Shipping Content Safely

Priced January 23, 2015

Box helped companies move their files to the cloud. It offered storage and sharing like consumer services, but with the security, permissions and audit trails that large firms needed. Its customers included banks, drug makers and government agencies. When Box filed to go public in 2014, it was famous for heavy spending on sales and growing losses, and the offering was delayed into 2015.

The Original Bet

Aaron Levie and Dylan Smith started Box in 2005 while in college, with help from friends Jeff Queisser and Sam Ghods. Dallas billionaire Mark Cuban was an early investor. The company first targeted consumers, then shifted to businesses. The bet was that companies would trust the cloud with sensitive files if a vendor made it safe and simple. Levie became chief executive.

Down at the Wharf

Box priced at $14 a share in January 2015 and rose strongly on its first day. The company then worked for years to cut losses and turn a profit. It survived a challenge from an activist investor in 2021 and later added AI features. Box remains a public company led by Aaron Levie. Its customers include many of the largest companies in the world.

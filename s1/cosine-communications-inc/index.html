Riding the Top of the Wave

Priced September 25, 2000

CoSine Communications was one of the most hyped networking startups of the boom. It built large systems for telecom carriers that let them offer business customers firewalls and private networks from inside the carrier's own network. When it went public in September 2000, near the very top of the telecom bubble, its shares jumped sharply on the first day of trading.

The Original Bet

CoSine bet that carriers would sell security and networking as services, so that businesses would not need to buy and run their own equipment. Its systems let one carrier serve many customers from shared hardware. The idea anticipated later cloud services. It depended, though, on carriers with money to spend on new equipment. Large carriers in the United States and abroad tested its systems.

After the Wave Broke

CoSine priced its IPO at $23 a share in September 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. Months later, the telecom market collapsed, and carriers stopped buying. Revenue never came close to the expectations set at the offering. In 2004 the company wound down its operations, and the remaining shell kept its cash. Its story became a symbol of how quickly the telecom boom turned to bust.

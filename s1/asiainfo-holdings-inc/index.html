Wiring the Middle Kingdom

Priced March 02, 2000

AsiaInfo began as a small company started by Chinese students who had studied in the United States. By the late 1990s it had become the firm China's state telecom carriers hired to build their internet backbones and the software that ran them. When it filed to go public in early 2000, China had only a few million people online, and investors were hungry for any way to own a piece of what came next.

The Original Bet

The founders bet that China would build its internet the way it built roads: fast, at national scale, and through a few large state carriers. Those carriers had money and mandate but little experience with routers, network design, or customer billing. AsiaInfo set out to be the systems integrator in the middle, turning imported hardware into working networks and later writing the operations and billing software that kept them running.

A Bridge Between Markets

AsiaInfo priced at $24 a share in March 2000 and more than tripled on its first day, one of the last great openings before the dot-com peak broke. The company shifted over the next decade from building networks to selling telecom software. It merged with Linkage Technologies in 2010 to form AsiaInfo-Linkage, and a consortium led by CITIC Capital, including co-founder Edward Tian, took it private in 2014.

A Steady Engine for Retirement

Priced March 15, 2010

Financial Engines brought Nobel-prize finance to the office 401(k). It offered workers advice on how to invest their retirement savings and, for a fee, would manage those accounts for them. It sold through employers and plan providers rather than directly to the public. When it filed in early 2010, the crisis had just wiped out much of many workers' savings, and the case for professional help felt urgent.

The Original Bet

William Sharpe, who won the Nobel Prize in economics in 1990, co-founded the company in 1996 with securities law professor Joseph Grundfest and venture capitalist Craig Johnson. Their bet was that rigorous portfolio models could be delivered cheaply over the internet to millions of ordinary savers. The Pension Protection Act of 2006, which encouraged automatic advice in retirement plans, opened the door wider.

The Road to the Bell

Financial Engines priced its IPO at $12 a share in March 2010, one of the first technology offerings after the crisis, and rose sharply on its first day. Assets under its management grew for years. In 2018 Hellman & Friedman bought the company for about $3 billion and merged it with Edelman Financial. The combined firm brought together online advice for workplace savers and in-person planning for wealthier clients.

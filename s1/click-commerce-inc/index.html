Selling Through Partners

Priced June 26, 2000

Many manufacturers do not sell directly to customers. They sell through dealers and distributors, and coordinating that network is hard. Click Commerce, based in Chicago, built web software that linked manufacturers with their partners for ordering, pricing and support. It went public in June 2000, at the peak of excitement over business-to-business commerce on the internet. The company was one of several Chicago tech firms to list in that period.

The Original Bet

Click Commerce bet that manufacturers would move their partner relationships onto the web. Its software gave dealers a single place to check prices, place orders and get information. After the dot-com crash, the company bought other software businesses, including tools for research institutions and health care, and became steadily profitable. Its customers included large manufacturers of equipment and vehicles. Its software ran large parts of their dealer networks.

Home in Chicago

Click Commerce priced its IPO at $10 a share in June 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. The shares collapsed after the bubble burst, but the company rebuilt itself through acquisitions and a tight focus on profits. In 2006 Illinois Tool Works, another Chicago-area company, bought Click Commerce. The deal gave ITW a profitable software business. It also gave Click Commerce shareholders a better outcome than most of their peers from the class of 2000.

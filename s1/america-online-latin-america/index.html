Dial-Up South of the Border

Priced August 07, 2000

By 2000 America Online had brought tens of millions of Americans online, and it wanted to repeat the feat in Latin America. It formed a joint venture with the Cisneros Group, a Venezuelan media family, to launch AOL in Brazil, Mexico and Argentina. The venture went public in August 2000, when internet stocks were already falling, with plans to spend heavily on marketing and free trial discs.

The Original Bet

AOL Latin America bet that AOL's easy software and brand would win in markets where few people were online yet. It localized content and spent heavily on promotions. Local rivals, however, offered free internet access, and many users were reluctant to pay a monthly fee. Phone and broadband companies also began bundling their own access. Its parent companies kept funding it for years in the hope of a turnaround.

Lights Out

AOL Latin America priced its IPO at $8 a share in August 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. Subscribers grew, but losses were large and constant, and it relied on funding from its parents. Its share price sank as rival free and broadband services spread. In 2005 the company filed for bankruptcy and wound down its service. Its rise and fall marked the end of AOL's global dial-up ambitions.

The Mortgage Without the Middleman

Priced June 28, 1999

In 1999 buying a house usually meant a mortgage broker, a stack of paper, and fees that few borrowers understood. E-Loan put the process on the web. Borrowers could compare rates from many lenders, apply online, and track their loan as it moved toward closing. The company was part of a wave of firms trying to bring finance online, and it argued that open pricing would win the trust of buyers who felt brokers kept them in the dark.

The Original Bet

Chris Larsen and Janina Pawlowski, who had worked together in mortgage brokerage, founded E-Loan in the San Francisco Bay Area in 1996. Their bet was transparency. E-Loan showed borrowers the rates it got and the fees it charged, which was unusual at the time. It earned money as a broker, and the internet let it serve borrowers anywhere at a lower cost than a branch office.

After the Offering

E-Loan priced its offering at $14 a share in June 1999. The stock fell steeply when the bubble burst, but the business survived and later turned a profit as low interest rates drove a refinancing boom. Popular, Inc., the Puerto Rico banking group, acquired E-Loan in 2005. Chris Larsen went on to co-found the lending marketplace Prosper and later Ripple.

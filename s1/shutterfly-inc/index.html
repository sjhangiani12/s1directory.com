Pictures Given Wings

Priced September 28, 2006

Shutterfly turned digital photos into things you could hold. As cameras went digital, people stopped printing film, and their pictures piled up on hard drives. Shutterfly let them upload photos, store them free and order prints, photo books, cards and calendars, made in its own plants. By 2006 photo books and holiday cards were its fastest-growing products. Its customers were mostly families saving their memories.

The Original Bet

Shutterfly was founded in 1999 with backing from Netscape co-founder Jim Clark, and Eva Manolis was among its founders. The bet was that digital photography would create a large market for high-quality printed products. Shutterfly ran its own manufacturing to control quality, and it treated free storage as a way to keep customers coming back. Free unlimited photo storage was part of its offer from early on.

After the Offering

Shutterfly priced its IPO at $15 a share in September 2006 on the Nasdaq. It bought Tiny Prints and the school photo company Lifetouch, and its holiday season became a major retail event. In 2019 Apollo took Shutterfly private for about $2.7 billion. Its busiest weeks came each December, when holiday cards and photo gifts drove a large share of the year's sales.

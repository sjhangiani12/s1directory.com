Billboards That Think

Priced November 19, 1999

Early web advertising was mostly banners bought by hand and measured loosely. Mediaplex, based in San Francisco, set out to give advertisers real machinery: technology to decide which ad to show, when and to whom, and to measure the result. When it filed to go public in late 1999, internet ad spending was rising fast, and marketers wanted proof that the money worked.

The Original Bet

Gregory Raifman founded Mediaplex in San Francisco in 1996. The bet was that online advertising would become driven by data and software, with messages that changed based on what was known about the viewer and the moment. Mediaplex combined the services of an agency with its own ad-serving technology, called MOJO, so clients could run complex campaigns across many sites.

Swept Into a Merger

Mediaplex priced at $12 a share in November 1999 and reached a market value in the billions within months. The online ad market collapsed in 2000 and 2001, and many of its dot-com clients disappeared. In 2001 ValueClick acquired Mediaplex in a stock deal. The Mediaplex ad-serving technology lived on inside ValueClick for years. Raifman stayed on the ValueClick board for about a year after the merger.

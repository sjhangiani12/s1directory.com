Where the Networks Meet

Priced August 10, 2000

The internet is made of many separate networks, and they need neutral places to connect. Equinix, founded in 1998 by Jay Adelson and Al Avery, built those meeting points: secure buildings where carriers, content companies and businesses could rent space and link their equipment directly. Its name nods to equality and exchange. Equinix went public in August 2000, a few months after the market peak, with only a handful of sites open.

The Original Bet

Equinix bet that neutrality was the product. Because it was not a carrier itself, competing networks were willing to meet inside its buildings. Each new customer made the site more valuable to the next, since there were more partners to connect with. The idea took years and large amounts of capital to prove. Its buildings became key points where the internet's networks join.

Through the Bust, Around the World

Equinix priced its IPO at $12 a share in August 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. The telecom crash nearly sank it, and the company survived through a restructuring and a merger with assets from rivals. It then grew steadily into a worldwide network of data centers and became a real estate investment trust in 2015. It is still public and trades as EQIX.

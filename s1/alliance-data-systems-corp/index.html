Points, Cards and Loyalty

Priced June 07, 2001

Alliance Data was assembled in 1996 when the investment firm Welsh, Carson, Anderson & Stowe combined a retail credit card bank with a transaction processing business. It added the Air Miles loyalty program in Canada soon after. By June 2001, when it went public, it was a quiet giant behind the store credit cards and rewards programs used by millions of shoppers. The offering came in a weak market, months after the dot-com crash.

The Original Bet

Alliance Data bet that retailers would hand off their loyalty and credit card programs to a specialist. Store cards and rewards programs generated detailed data about what customers bought. Alliance Data used that data to help retailers market more precisely, while earning interest and fees on the cards it issued. It later added the Epsilon marketing business. Its programs reached millions of households through familiar store brands.

Breaking Into Pieces

Alliance Data priced its IPO at $12 a share in June 2001 and listed on the New York Stock Exchange. It grew for years through acquisitions and became a member of the S&P 500. In the late 2010s it sold Epsilon and spun off its loyalty business. In 2022 the remaining card business renamed itself Bread Financial. Bread Financial is still public and trades on the New York Stock Exchange.

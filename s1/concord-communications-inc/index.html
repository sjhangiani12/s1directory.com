Keeping the Network in Concord

Priced October 15, 1997

In 1997 corporate networks were growing large and complex, and the people who ran them had little insight into how well they worked. Concord sold software called Network Health. It gathered data from routers, switches, and servers and turned it into clear reports on traffic, errors, and capacity. Managers could see trouble coming and plan upgrades. The Massachusetts company went public in October 1997.

The Original Bet

The bet was that networks had become critical to business, and that managers would pay for clear measures of their health. Concord's software worked with equipment from many vendors and produced reports that non-specialists could read. It sold to large companies and to service providers who needed to prove they met their promises to customers. It sold licenses and support, and its customers renewed year after year.

After the Offering

Concord priced its offering at $14 a share in October 1997. It grew through the internet boom and bought Empire Technologies and Aprisma to widen its products. Computer Associates acquired Concord in 2005, and its tools became part of CA's network management line. The offering gave it the funds to grow during the boom years. Concord was one of the steadier companies in this list, a software maker that sold to network managers and kept its focus on a single problem it understood well.

Asking Millions of People

Priced July 15, 2004

Market research once meant phone calls during dinner and clipboards in shopping malls. Greenfield Online, based in Wilton, Connecticut, moved surveys onto the internet, building large panels of people who agreed to answer questions online. Research firms and brands paid for the responses. When the company went public in July 2004, it had also bought Ciao, a European site where shoppers compared prices and wrote reviews.

The Original Bet

Greenfield bet that the internet would make surveys faster and cheaper than any phone bank. A large, well-profiled online panel let researchers reach the right people in days instead of weeks. The Ciao purchase added a second business built on consumer opinion, with revenue from shopping referrals and its own survey community in Europe. Survey panels became the standard tool of the market research trade in the years that followed.

The Ciao Prize

Greenfield Online priced its IPO at $13 a share in July 2004 and listed on Nasdaq under the ticker SRVY. The Ciao business grew into a major part of the company. In 2008 Microsoft agreed to buy Greenfield for about $486 million, mainly to add Ciao to its search and shopping efforts in Europe. Microsoft later sold the survey business.

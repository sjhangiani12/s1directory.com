Quick on Its Feet

Priced August 19, 1999

By the late 1990s, electronics makers relied on long chains of contract manufacturers and suppliers spread around the world. Keeping all of them working from the same parts list and the latest design changes was a constant struggle. Agile Software built tools to share that product information over the internet. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, outsourced manufacturing was booming.

The Original Bet

Bryan Stolle founded Agile in 1995. The bet was that product information, such as bills of materials and engineering change orders, should live in one shared system that every partner could reach. If a design changed, every supplier would know at once. That promised fewer errors, faster product launches and less scrap for companies that no longer built everything themselves.

After the Offering

Agile priced at $21 a share in August 1999 and climbed steeply during the boom. Its sales fell when the technology industry cut spending after 2000, but it kept growing its product lifecycle management business across more industries. In 2007 Oracle bought Agile for about $495 million, adding its software to Oracle's applications for manufacturers. Agile's products lived on inside Oracle's lineup for years afterward.

A Plan for Every Desk

Priced October 12, 2018

Anaplan sold a simple promise to big companies: stop planning the business in a thousand disconnected spreadsheets. Its cloud platform let a finance team, a sales team and a supply chain team work from one live model of the company. When Anaplan filed to go public in 2018, cloud software was the market's favorite category. Investors were paying rich prices for any subscription business that could show steady growth and loyal enterprise customers.

The Original Bet

The company began in Yorkshire, England, where Guy Haddleton and Michael Gould built a new calculation engine for business models. Gould had worked on planning software before and believed the older tools were too slow and rigid. Anaplan's engine held huge models in memory, so a change in one assumption could flow through the whole plan in seconds. The company later moved its headquarters to San Francisco to be closer to customers and capital.

Planning in Public

Anaplan priced its offering at $17 a share in October 2018, and the stock rose sharply on its first day of trading. Revenue kept climbing over the next few years, though losses remained large. In 2022 Thoma Bravo agreed to buy the company. The price was trimmed to about $10.4 billion after a dispute over employee pay, and Anaplan became a private company again.

Switching Yards of the Internet

Priced September 29, 1999

The internet of the late 1990s was a patchwork of networks that handed traffic to one another at crowded exchange points, where data often slowed or got lost. InterNAP built private access points connected to many major backbones and used software to pick the best path for each packet. When it filed to go public in the fall of 1999, businesses wanted the internet to behave as reliably as a private line.

The Original Bet

Anthony Naughtin and Chris Wheeler founded InterNAP in Seattle in 1996. The bet was that businesses would pay more for internet traffic that avoided congestion. InterNAP bought capacity from many backbone providers and steered each customer's traffic over the route performing best at that moment, much like a dispatcher sending trains down the clearest track. Customers paid a premium for that performance, and many were content and media businesses.

After the Offering

InterNAP priced at $20 a share in September 1999 and rose fast with the boom. The crash left it with heavy debt and falling prices, but it survived and added data center, hosting and cloud services over the following years. Debt remained a burden. The company filed for Chapter 11 in 2020 and came out of the process as a private company.

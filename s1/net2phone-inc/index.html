A Conversation Across the Sea

Priced July 30, 1999

In the late 1990s, a call from New York to India could cost more than a dollar a minute. Net2Phone, spun out of the discount long distance carrier IDT, let people make those calls over the internet for a fraction of the price. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, internet telephony was a young idea and Net2Phone was one of its best-known names.

The Original Bet

Howard Jonas built IDT by selling cheap international calling, and Net2Phone extended the same idea to the internet. The bet was that voice would move onto data networks, where calls could be carried far more cheaply. Net2Phone started with calls from a PC to a regular phone, then sold its service through partners, devices and businesses around the world. It was an early mover.

Home Again

Net2Phone priced at $15 a share in July 1999 and rose sharply in the months after. In 2000 AT&T took a large stake in the company. As internet calling became common and prices fell, Net2Phone struggled to make money from it. In 2006 IDT bought back the shares it did not already own, bringing Net2Phone home. The service continued under IDT's ownership for years after the deal.

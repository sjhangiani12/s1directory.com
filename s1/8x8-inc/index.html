Moves on the Board

Priced July 02, 1997

In 1997 video over phone lines was a dream that kept almost arriving. 8x8, a Silicon Valley chip company, designed processors that compressed video and audio so they could travel over ordinary connections. Its chips went into videophones and conferencing systems. When it went public in July 1997, 8x8 was a small semiconductor company betting that people would want to see each other when they called.

The Original Bet

Joe Parkinson and Chi-Ping Hsu founded the company in 1987. The bet was that compression was the key to sending rich media through narrow pipes. 8x8 designed chips and complete systems for video calling. As the internet grew, the company saw that voice and video calls could run over data networks, not just phone lines. The company would change its business more than once.

After the Offering

8x8 priced its offering at $6.50 a share in July 1997. The stock rose in the boom and fell in the crash, when the company was close to running out of money. 8x8 reinvented itself as a service provider, selling internet phone service to small businesses. It grew into a cloud communications company and remains publicly traded today. Few companies of its era changed course so completely.

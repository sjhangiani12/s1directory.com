The Bell That Calls You Back

Priced November 01, 2007

SoundBite ran the automated messages companies send to their customers. Banks, utilities, retailers and collection agencies used its hosted system to place reminder calls, payment notices and alerts by voice, text and email. It sold the service over the internet, so clients needed no equipment of their own. It filed to go public in 2007, when hosted software was still a new way to sell.

The Original Bet

SoundBite started in 2000 with a bet that outbound customer contact could move from call center hardware to a hosted service. Its first big market was collections, where reaching more people at the right time had a direct value. It later added more channels and pitched its service for friendlier messages, such as appointment reminders and fraud alerts. Clients paid by usage, so revenue rose with each campaign they ran.

After the Offering

SoundBite priced its IPO at $8 a share in November 2007, weeks before the financial crisis began to bite. Its stock stayed low for most of its time as a public company. In 2013 Genesys, the contact center software maker, bought SoundBite for about $100 million. Genesys folded SoundBite's outbound tools into its larger contact center platform, giving clients one place to handle calls in both directions.

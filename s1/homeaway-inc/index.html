A Cottage for Every Season

Priced June 28, 2011

HomeAway gathered the scattered world of vacation rentals into one place. Before it, renting a beach house meant classified ads, phone calls and word of mouth. HomeAway bought up listing sites such as VRBO and charged owners to advertise their homes to travelers. When it filed in 2011, Airbnb was still a young startup, and HomeAway was the largest vacation rental marketplace in the world.

The Original Bet

Brian Sharples and Carl Shepherd started in Austin in 2005 with a roll-up plan. They raised a large amount of venture money early and used it to buy established rental sites in the United States and Europe, including VRBO. The business model was simple: owners paid a yearly subscription to list their properties, and travelers browsed for free. The scale of its listings drew travelers, and travelers drew more owners.

After the Offering

HomeAway priced its IPO at $27 a share in June 2011 and closed its first day well above that. Over the next few years it began to charge travelers a booking fee as Airbnb grew into a serious rival. In 2015 Expedia bought HomeAway for about $3.9 billion. The business later took the name of one of its oldest pieces, Vrbo.

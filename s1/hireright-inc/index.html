The Watchful Eye at the Door

Priced August 07, 2007

HireRight checked people's pasts before they started a new job. Employers ordered criminal, education, employment and drug screens through its website, and HireRight gathered the results from courts and records across the country and abroad. It worked mostly for large companies that hired in volume. When it filed in 2007, background screening had become standard practice in American hiring. Its customers included many Fortune 500 companies.

The Original Bet

HireRight was founded in 1995 with a bet that the slow, paper-based business of employment screening could move onto the internet. It built software that plugged into employers' hiring systems, so a check could start with a click and the results could flow straight back. That integration helped it win large corporate accounts. International checks became a strength as global companies screened candidates in many countries.

After the Offering

HireRight priced its IPO at $15 a share in August 2007 on the Nasdaq. Its time as a public company was short. In 2008 US Investigations Services, a large provider of government background checks, bought HireRight for $19.75 a share in cash. The HireRight name lived on. It later returned to the public market as a separate company and was taken private again.

Measured to the Penny

Priced June 26, 2015

Xactly worked out what salespeople should be paid. Many companies still tracked commissions in spreadsheets, which led to errors and disputes. Xactly's cloud software calculated pay automatically, showed reps their earnings and gave managers data on which plans worked. When it filed to go public in 2015, it was one of a wave of cloud companies selling to specific business departments.

The Original Bet

Christopher Cabrera founded Xactly in 2005 after years in sales leadership. He had seen how much time and trust was lost to commission mistakes. The bet was that a cloud service could make pay accurate and clear, and that the data it collected across many companies would become valuable on its own. Xactly called this incentive compensation management. It sold to companies of many sizes.

A Short Public Life

Xactly priced at $8 a share in June 2015, below its expected range. The stock traded quietly for two years as the company grew. In 2017 Vista Equity Partners bought Xactly for about $564 million and took it private. Under Vista, Xactly continued as a private company focused on sales pay, planning and forecasting. The deal was one of several in which Vista bought small public software firms in that period.

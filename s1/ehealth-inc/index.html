Shelter from the Storm

Priced October 12, 2006

eHealth let people shop for health insurance online. Its site, eHealthInsurance.com, showed plans from many insurers side by side, so individuals, families and small businesses could compare prices and coverage before they bought. The company was paid commissions by insurers. In 2006 most Americans got insurance through work, and those buying on their own had few easy ways to compare.

The Original Bet

eHealth started in 1997 with a bet that buying health insurance, a confusing and paper-heavy process, could work like other online shopping. It became a licensed broker in every state and built tools to quote and apply for plans online. Its customers were mostly people who bought insurance for themselves, such as the self-employed. Insurers paid it commissions for the policies it sold, much as they paid traditional agents.

After the Offering

eHealth priced its IPO at $14 a share in October 2006 on the Nasdaq. The Affordable Care Act and its public exchanges later changed the individual market. eHealth turned toward Medicare plans for older Americans, which became most of its business. It remains publicly traded. Its Medicare business grew quickly as baby boomers aged into the program, though rising marketing costs and changing commissions made profits uneven.

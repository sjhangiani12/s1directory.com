Crossing Every Language

Priced August 20, 1999

Software companies of the 1990s wanted to sell everywhere, but every market needed its own language, date formats and manuals. Lionbridge did that work for them, translating and testing products for global release. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, the web was making every company international overnight, and someone had to make their products speak to the world.

The Original Bet

Rory Cowan founded Lionbridge in 1996, building it partly by acquiring translation businesses around the world. The bet was that localization would grow from a small cottage industry into a core service for global companies. Lionbridge offered one partner for translation, engineering and testing across many languages, with teams spread across lower-cost locations. As the web spread, the volume of content needing translation grew quickly, and websites joined software as a core part of the work.

A Long Bridge

Lionbridge priced at $10 a share in August 1999. It survived the downturn and grew by acquisition, most notably buying Bowne Global Solutions in 2005, which made it one of the largest companies in its field. It added testing and data services for technology companies. In 2017 H.I.G. Capital took Lionbridge private in a deal worth about $360 million. It remains a large localization company.

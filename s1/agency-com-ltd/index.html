The Poster Goes Digital

Priced December 08, 1999

In 1995, few big companies had a website, and fewer knew what one should do. Agency.com was one of the New York shops that stepped in, building sites and online campaigns for major brands. When it filed to go public in late 1999, interactive agencies were among the hottest stocks on the market, valued less like ad firms and more like the internet itself.

The Original Bet

Chan Suh and Kyle Shannon founded Agency.com in 1995. Their bet was that the web would need its own kind of agency, one that mixed design, technology and marketing in a way traditional ad firms could not. Omnicom, the advertising giant, invested early, and the agency grew quickly by winning large corporate clients and buying smaller interactive shops in the United States and Europe.

Back Into the Fold

Agency.com priced at $26 a share in December 1999, at the height of the interactive agency craze. When the dot-com market fell, corporate budgets for websites fell with it. In 2001, Seneca Investments, a vehicle formed by Omnicom and Pegasus Partners, took the agency private for $3.35 a share. It continued for years inside the Omnicom family before merging into TBWA.

Everything Must Go

Priced May 30, 2002

When the dot-com crash left warehouses full of unsold goods, Overstock.com was ready to buy them. Founded in 1999 by Patrick Byrne in Salt Lake City, the company sold excess inventory from other retailers and manufacturers at deep discounts. By the time it went public in May 2002, through an unusual Dutch auction offering, it had become one of the better-known bargain sites on the web.

The Original Bet

Overstock bet that the internet was the ideal place to clear surplus stock. Liquidation had always been a messy business of brokers and outlet stores. A single website could reach millions of bargain hunters at once. Over time the company shifted from pure closeouts toward new home furnishings, where it found steadier supply and better margins. Its quirky ads and low prices made it a household name.

Many Lives After the Auction

Overstock priced its offering at $13 a share in May 2002 and listed on Nasdaq. The company became known for its outspoken founder, its campaigns against short sellers, and an early push into blockchain projects. Byrne left in 2019. In 2023 the company bought the Bed Bath & Beyond brand, renamed itself Beyond, Inc., and continues to trade publicly. Its path from closeouts to home goods is one of the longer stories in American e-commerce.

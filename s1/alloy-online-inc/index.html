The Mall in a Teenager's Pocket

Priced May 14, 1999

In 1999 teenagers were the first generation to grow up online, and marketers were eager to reach them. Alloy built a business around girls aged roughly 10 to 24. It mailed a fashion catalog to millions of homes and ran a website with quizzes, chat, and content written in their voice. The catalog paid the bills, and the website built loyalty. Alloy went public in May 1999 as the company that understood teens on the web.

The Original Bet

Matt Diamond and James Johnson founded Alloy in New York in 1996. The bet was that a brand could be both a store and a gathering place for young people. Alloy used its website to grow its customer list and its catalog to turn that audience into sales. Advertisers that wanted to reach teens also paid for access to the community.

After the Offering

Alloy priced its offering at $15 a share in May 1999. As the dot-com market cooled, it moved toward marketing and media services, and it bought magazines, school marketing programs, and a book packager. That unit, Alloy Entertainment, developed series such as Gossip Girl and Pretty Little Liars. ZelnickMedia took Alloy private in 2010. The company had come a long way from a catalog and a chat room, and it showed how a youth brand could become a media business.

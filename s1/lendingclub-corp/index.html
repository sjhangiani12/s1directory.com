The Moneylender's Stage

Priced December 11, 2014

LendingClub let ordinary people lend to each other. Borrowers applied online for personal loans, often to pay off credit cards, and investors funded those loans in small pieces. The company earned fees in the middle. When it filed to go public in 2014, it was the largest such marketplace in the United States and a symbol of fintech's promise to bypass banks.

The Original Bet

Renaud Laplanche founded LendingClub in 2006. It first launched as an app on Facebook. Laplanche believed banks charged too much on credit cards and paid savers too little. The bet was that an online marketplace could cut out the middle and give both sides a better deal. Over time large institutions became the main investors on the platform. It grew quickly.

From Club to Bank

LendingClub priced at $15 a share in December 2014 and jumped on its first day. In 2016 a scandal over loan sales forced Laplanche to resign, and the stock fell hard. The company later bought Radius Bank in 2021 and became a bank itself. LendingClub remains a public company. Scott Sanborn took over as chief executive after Laplanche left. As a bank, LendingClub can hold loans and take deposits.

The Envelope That Ate Hollywood

Priced May 23, 2002

In 2002 most Americans rented movies from a video store and paid late fees when they forgot to return them. Netflix offered a monthly subscription instead. Customers built a queue on the website, and red envelopes carrying DVDs arrived in the mail. Founded in 1997 by Reed Hastings and Marc Randolph, the company had several hundred thousand subscribers when it went public in May 2002. Many investors still doubted it could survive against Blockbuster.

The Original Bet

Netflix bet on two things. The first was that the cheap, light DVD could travel by mail. The second was that a subscription with no late fees would win customers' loyalty. Its recommendation engine helped people find older films they had never heard of, which made the large catalog valuable. From the start, the founders talked about one day delivering movies over the internet.

From Mailbox to Stream

Netflix priced its IPO at $15 a share in May 2002 and listed on Nasdaq. It began streaming in 2007, started producing original series in 2013, and expanded to nearly every country in the world. Blockbuster went bankrupt in 2010. Netflix is still public and trades as NFLX, and it is now one of the most valuable media companies on earth.

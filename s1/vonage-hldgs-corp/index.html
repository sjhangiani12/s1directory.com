Setting Sail on a New Line

Priced May 23, 2006

Vonage turned the home phone into an internet service. Customers plugged a small adapter into their broadband connection and kept a regular phone number, with unlimited calling across the country for a flat monthly fee. Its ads were everywhere, and by 2006 it had more than a million and a half subscribers. It was the best-known challenger to the old phone companies, and the cable companies were coming for the same customers.

The Original Bet

Jeffrey Citron, a former electronic trading entrepreneur, started Vonage in 2001. The bet was that broadband homes would drop expensive landline plans for internet calling if it felt just like a normal phone. Vonage spent heavily on marketing to win customers fast, and it lost a lot of money doing so. Its price, a flat monthly fee for unlimited calls, undercut traditional phone plans by a wide margin.

After the Offering

Vonage priced its IPO at $17 a share in May 2006 on the New York Stock Exchange. The stock fell on its first day and kept falling, and customers who had been offered shares in the deal were angry. Patent suits from Verizon added to its trouble. Vonage later rebuilt itself around business communications, and Ericsson bought it in 2022 for about $6.2 billion.

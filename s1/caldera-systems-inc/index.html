Linux From the Crater

Priced March 21, 2000

In 1999 Linux was the darling of the technology world. Red Hat and VA Linux had both soared on their first days of trading, and investors wanted more. Caldera Systems, based in Orem, Utah, sold a polished Linux distribution aimed at businesses. It went public in March 2000, within days of the Nasdaq peak, and its shares jumped on the first day.

The Original Bet

Caldera bet that businesses would pay for a supported, easy-to-install version of Linux, just as they paid for other operating systems. It offered training and services alongside its software. The company had roots in Novell's circle, and it aimed squarely at corporate networks. Its product line included both desktop and server versions of Linux. Its founders had worked at Novell before starting the company.

From Linux to Lawsuits

Caldera priced its IPO at $14 a share in March 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. In 2001 it bought the Unix business of the Santa Cruz Operation and later renamed itself The SCO Group. SCO then sued IBM and others, claiming Linux used its Unix code. The lawsuits went badly, and SCO filed for bankruptcy in 2007. Its story became one of the strangest in the history of open source software.

Threads of Customer Service

Priced May 05, 1999

By 1999 customers expected companies to answer them by email and on the web, not just by phone. Most companies had no good system to track those conversations. Silknet, a New Hampshire software company, sold a platform it called eService. It gave support agents and customers a shared view of questions, answers, and history across channels. Silknet went public in May 1999 as businesses scrambled to serve customers online.

The Original Bet

The bet was that customer service would become a web business, and that companies would need software to run it. Silknet's platform let customers find answers on their own and passed harder questions to human agents with the full history attached. It sold to large companies that handled huge volumes of support requests. The name suggested a fine, strong thread connecting a company to each of its customers. Large firms in telecom, technology, and finance were among the buyers.

After the Offering

Silknet priced its offering at $15 a share in May 1999, and the stock climbed quickly in the boom. In 2000 Kana Communications agreed to buy Silknet in an all-stock deal valued at billions of dollars at signing. The value of that deal fell sharply as internet stocks collapsed later that year. The merger made Silknet's eService suite part of Kana's product line. Kana itself struggled through the downturn that followed, and the combined company spent years trying to recover the momentum of 1999.

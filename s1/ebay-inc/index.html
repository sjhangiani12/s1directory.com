Going Once, Going Twice, Online

Priced September 24, 1998

In 1998 eBay was already a strange and wonderful place. Collectors traded toys, coins, and comics, and ordinary people sold things from their attics to strangers who paid by mailed check. eBay owned no inventory and shipped nothing. It simply ran the auctions and charged a fee for each listing and sale. That made it one of the few internet companies of its day that turned a profit, and it went public in September 1998.

The Original Bet

Pierre Omidyar, a software engineer, launched the site as AuctionWeb in 1995 from his apartment in the San Francisco Bay Area. The bet was that people would trust each other to trade if they had a fair market and a way to rate one another. eBay's feedback system let buyers and sellers build reputations. Its users grew by word of mouth. Collectors made up the earliest community, and their enthusiasm drew in everyone else.

After the Offering

eBay priced its offering at $18 a share in September 1998, and the stock nearly tripled on its first day. Under Meg Whitman, eBay grew into a global marketplace, bought PayPal in 2002, and became a pillar of online commerce. It spun off PayPal in 2015. eBay remains a public company today. It grew far beyond collectibles into a global marketplace for new and used goods.

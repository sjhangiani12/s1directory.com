Warehouses on the Water

Priced June 30, 2017

Tintri built storage hardware for the age of virtual machines. Older storage systems thought in terms of disks and volumes. Tintri's systems understood each virtual machine, so administrators could see and tune performance for each application. By the time Tintri filed to go public in 2017, the storage market was moving fast toward flash, cloud services and larger rivals with deep pockets.

The Original Bet

Kieran Harty, a former VMware engineering leader, founded Tintri in 2008 with a small team of storage engineers. They saw that virtualization had changed how data centers worked, but storage had not caught up. Their bet was that storage designed around virtual machines would be simpler to run and faster to fix. The company raised large venture rounds and won hundreds of enterprise customers.

A Hard Landing

Tintri cut its price range and priced at $7 a share in June 2017, well below its early hopes. The stock fell soon after. Losses were heavy and cash ran short as competition grew. In July 2018, about a year after its offering, Tintri filed for bankruptcy. DataDirect Networks bought its main assets and kept the product alive. The collapse came quickly for a company that had raised so much venture money.

Moving Goods by Plan

Priced October 07, 1997

In the 1990s manufacturers and retailers were learning that much of their cost hid in the supply chain. Too much stock tied up cash, and too little lost sales. Logility, a unit of the Atlanta company American Software, sold planning software to address both. It forecast demand, planned inventory, and coordinated transport across a company's suppliers and warehouses. American Software spun out part of Logility in an offering in October 1997 and kept majority control.

The Original Bet

The bet was that supply chain planning was a large market of its own, separate from general business software. Logility sold to consumer goods makers, distributors, and retailers. Its tools let planners look ahead, test scenarios, and share forecasts with partners, which cut waste and improved service. That focus suited companies that moved large volumes of goods through complex networks.

After the Offering

Logility priced its offering at $14.50 a share in October 1997. It stayed a small public company, with American Software as its controlling owner. In 2009 American Software bought the shares it did not already own and took Logility back in-house. The brand lived on as the company's supply chain business. The software it built continued to serve supply chain planners for years after.

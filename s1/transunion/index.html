Keeping Score in Chicago

Priced June 25, 2015

TransUnion keeps records of how people borrow and repay. Lenders use its credit reports to decide who gets a card, a car loan or a mortgage. It is one of three national credit bureaus in the United States, alongside Equifax and Experian. When TransUnion filed in 2015, it had been owned by private equity for years and was returning to the public market as a data and analytics company.

The Original Bet

TransUnion began in 1968 as a holding company for a railcar leasing business. A year later it bought a Chicago credit bureau, and the credit business grew into its main line of work. The bet was that credit files, gathered on a national scale and kept up to date, would become essential to lending. Computers made that possible. Over time it gathered files on hundreds of millions of people.

Back to the Market

TransUnion priced at $22.50 a share in June 2015. Advent and Goldman Sachs had taken it private in 2012. As a public company it bought data firms, including Neustar in 2021, and expanded overseas. TransUnion remains a public company with headquarters in Chicago. Its data is now used for fraud prevention, marketing and identity checks, as well as lending. Like its rivals, it faces close scrutiny from regulators.

Radio Parts for the Wireless Age

Priced August 18, 2000

WJ Communications grew out of Watkins-Johnson, a Silicon Valley electronics firm founded in 1957 that built microwave and defense equipment. By 2000, after a recapitalization led by the private equity firm Fox Paine, the company had refocused on radio frequency parts for the booming wireless industry. It went public in August 2000, when phone carriers were racing to build networks and RF chips were in high demand.

The Original Bet

WJ bet that the wireless build-out would need large numbers of high-performance amplifiers and mixers. Its parts sat inside the base stations that carried mobile calls and in broadband equipment. The company had decades of RF know-how from its parent. Its fortunes, though, depended on the spending cycles of a few big equipment makers. Its engineering roots reached back more than forty years.

A Long Fade

WJ priced its IPO at $16 a share in August 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. Within a year, the telecom slump cut orders sharply. The company shrank, moved to an outsourced manufacturing model and kept selling RF parts to a narrower market. In 2008 TriQuint Semiconductor acquired WJ Communications. The deal brought its RF know-how into a larger chip company focused on wireless and networking.

Asking the Crowd Online

Priced December 06, 1999

For decades, polling meant calling people at home during dinner. Harris Interactive, owner of the famous Harris Poll, bet that the internet could do it better, faster and at far larger scale. When it filed to go public in late 1999, it had built a panel of millions of people willing to answer surveys online, and the polling industry was watching to see if the method would hold up.

The Original Bet

Gordon Black founded his research firm in Rochester in 1975 and later bought Louis Harris and Associates, the pollster behind the Harris Poll. The bet was that online panels could replace telephone surveys for much of market research. Online surveys were cheaper, could include images and video, and could reach thousands of people in days rather than weeks. It was a bold move for a firm with a well-known brand to protect.

Proof and Pressure

Harris Interactive priced at $14 a share in December 1999. Its online polls drew skepticism at first, but they performed well in several elections and helped make internet research mainstream. Over time, cheaper online survey tools put pressure on prices, and the company lost money for several years. In 2014 Nielsen completed its acquisition of Harris Interactive for $2 a share, about $117 million.

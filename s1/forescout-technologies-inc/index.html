The Sentinel at the Gate

Priced October 27, 2017

Forescout answered a basic question that many networks could not: what is plugged in right now. Its software spotted laptops, phones, printers, cameras and factory machines as they joined, without needing an agent installed on each one. It then applied rules to block or isolate risky devices. When Forescout filed to go public in 2017, internet-connected devices were multiplying in offices, hospitals and plants.

The Original Bet

Forescout was founded in Israel in 2000 by a group of researchers and engineers led by Professor Hezy Yeshurun. Its early product focused on stopping network attacks. Over time it shifted toward visibility, on the view that a company cannot protect devices it does not know about. The company moved its headquarters to San Jose and kept research in Tel Aviv.

Watching the Pass

Forescout priced at $22 a share in October 2017. It grew by selling to large enterprises and government agencies. In 2020 Advent International agreed to buy the company. The two sides fought over the deal during the pandemic, then settled on a lower price, and Forescout became private later that year. Its customers included hospitals, factories and government agencies, where unmanaged devices were a growing worry.

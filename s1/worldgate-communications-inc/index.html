The Web Through the Television

Priced April 15, 1999

In 1999 most American homes had a television and a cable box, but far fewer had a personal computer. WorldGate wanted to bring the internet to the TV instead. Its service ran through existing cable set-top boxes, so viewers could check email or browse the web with a remote and a wireless keyboard. It also let a TV show or ad link straight to a related website. WorldGate went public in April 1999 with cable operators as partners.

The Original Bet

The bet was that the television, not the PC, would be most people's door to the internet. WorldGate's system did the heavy work on servers at the cable company, so it could run on boxes already in living rooms. Cable operators saw it as a way to sell a new service without new hardware in every home. It was an early attempt at the connected television that many companies would try again in later decades.

After the Offering

WorldGate priced its offering at $21 a share in April 1999. Interactive TV proved a hard sell, and cheap PCs and broadband pulled people toward computers instead. WorldGate shifted its focus to video phones in the 2000s. It remained small and struggled financially in the years after its offering. Its offering came at the high point of enthusiasm for interactive TV. The idea of watching and browsing on the same screen would return years later, carried by smart TVs and streaming boxes rather than by cable set-top boxes.

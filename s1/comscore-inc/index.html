Surveying the Digital Crowd

Priced June 26, 2007

Comscore measured who was doing what online. It recruited a large panel of internet users who agreed to have their browsing tracked, and sold the resulting data to websites, advertisers and investors. Its monthly rankings of the most visited sites became a standard reference. In 2007 online advertising was growing fast, and buyers wanted numbers they could trust. Its data also fed investor research on internet companies.

The Original Bet

Gian Fulgoni and Magid Abraham, veterans of the market research firm Information Resources, founded Comscore in 1999. Their bet was that the internet needed a ratings system like television had, but based on far richer data about behavior. They built the panel first and then sold subscriptions to the reports. The panel let Comscore see not just traffic, but what people bought and searched for.

After the Offering

Comscore priced its IPO at $16.50 a share in June 2007 on the Nasdaq. In 2016 it merged with Rentrak to measure television too. Accounting problems led to its delisting in 2018 and a restatement, after which it returned to the Nasdaq. It remains publicly traded. Its business now centers on measuring audiences across television, streaming and digital media platforms.

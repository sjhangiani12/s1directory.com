A Bridge to Coverage

Priced December 12, 2014

Connecture built software for buying health insurance online. Insurers, brokers and state exchanges used its tools to show plans, compare prices and enroll customers. The Affordable Care Act had just opened public exchanges, and the market for online health insurance shopping was growing fast. Connecture filed to go public in 2014, at the end of the first full enrollment season.

The Original Bet

Connecture was based in the Milwaukee area and spent years building quoting and enrollment software for health insurers. As health reform pushed more people to shop for their own coverage, the company saw a larger role. The bet was that buying a plan would become a retail experience, and that insurers and exchanges would need software to support it. It sold to insurers across the country.

After the Bell

Connecture priced at $8 a share in December 2014, below its target range. The stock fell as some large customers left and losses grew. Francisco Partners invested in the company in 2016, then agreed to take it private in 2017 for a small fraction of its offering value. The deal closed in early 2018. Its stock had traded on Nasdaq for about three years.

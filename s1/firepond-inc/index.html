Sparks Over Still Water

Priced February 04, 2000

Selling a truck, a tractor or an industrial machine meant choosing from thousands of options, many of which did not work together. FirePond made the software that kept those choices straight, so a salesperson or a buyer on the web could configure a product, see a price, and get a valid quote. When it filed to go public in early 2000, companies were rushing to move their sales online.

The Original Bet

FirePond grew out of an older company in Minnesota that had built configuration tools for manufacturers. The bet was that the web would turn this back-office tool into a front-door one. If customers could configure and price their own orders online, manufacturers could sell more, make fewer mistakes, and spend less on the long back-and-forth of complex sales. It was a practical problem, and FirePond already had paying customers when it filed.

After the Offering

FirePond priced at $22 a share in February 2000, weeks before the Nasdaq peaked. Spending on e-business software fell steeply the next year, and the company cut staff and faced shareholder lawsuits from the offering period. In 2003 an affiliate of Jaguar Technology Holdings bought FirePond for $3.16 a share in cash, about $11.7 million. The deal ended its run as a public company less than four years after the offering.

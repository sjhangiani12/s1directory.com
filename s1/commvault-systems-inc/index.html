The Vaults Beneath the City

Priced September 21, 2006

CommVault protected the data of large organizations. Its software backed up, archived and recovered data across servers and storage systems, all from one platform and one index. Many rivals sold separate tools for each task. In 2006 corporate data was growing fast, and new rules required businesses to keep and find their records. Its customers included many large enterprises and public agencies.

The Original Bet

CommVault grew out of a data management group at Bell Labs and became an independent company in 1996. Its bet was that companies would want one integrated system for every kind of data protection, instead of a patchwork. It sold mainly through partners such as Dell and storage resellers. Its single code base and shared index set it apart from rivals that stitched together products from acquisitions.

After the Offering

CommVault priced its IPO at $14.50 a share in September 2006 on the Nasdaq. It grew into one of the leading backup software companies. Later it moved its products to the cloud and added protection against ransomware. It remains publicly traded. Its customers include large enterprises and government agencies around the world. Many of them first bought its software for basic backup and later used it for archiving and compliance.

Paperwork for the Physician

Priced April 06, 1999

American healthcare has always run on paper, and in the 1990s insurance claims were among the heaviest piles. Each doctor's visit produced a form that had to be checked, coded, and sent to the right insurer. Claimsnet.com offered small medical practices a way to do this over the web. Providers entered claims in a browser, and Claimsnet checked them and sent them on electronically. It went public in April 1999 as healthcare moved online.

The Original Bet

The bet was that small medical offices would use the web to cut the cost and delay of filing claims. Large hospitals already had expensive systems for this work, but smaller practices often still used paper. Claimsnet charged by the claim and promised faster payment with fewer errors. Its pitch was simple: a browser and an internet connection were all a practice needed.

After the Offering

Claimsnet.com priced its offering at $8 a share in April 1999. The company stayed small, and the healthcare internet market proved slower and more crowded than investors had hoped. Its stock later moved off the Nasdaq to the over-the-counter market. Claimsnet continued to operate in claims processing as a small company. It never reached the scale its offering had promised, but it showed how early the industry tried to move claims onto the web.

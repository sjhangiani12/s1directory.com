Every Night a Festival

Priced September 20, 2018

Eventbrite gave every event organizer a box office. A yoga teacher, a club promoter or a conference planner could set up a page, sell tickets and check guests in at the door. The company made money from small fees on each ticket. It filed to go public in 2018, after a decade of steady growth and a recent purchase of the European ticketing company Ticketfly. Live events were booming, and Eventbrite served the long tail of organizers.

The Original Bet

Julia Hartz, Kevin Hartz and Renaud Visage started Eventbrite in 2006. At the time, ticketing was controlled by a few large companies that served arenas, not neighborhood venues. The founders bet that the internet made it possible to serve millions of small events with the same software. Julia Hartz later became chief executive and led the company through its public offering.

Lights Over the River

Eventbrite priced at $23 a share in September 2018 and jumped on its first day. The 2020 pandemic halted live events and hit the business hard, and the company cut staff to survive. It rebuilt slowly as events returned. In late 2025 the Italian software group Bending Spoons agreed to buy Eventbrite for about $500 million in cash, and the deal closed in 2026.

Spinning the Broadband Web

Priced May 17, 1999

In 1999 phone companies were rolling out DSL and needed a way to manage a flood of new customers. Each subscriber had to be logged in, given an address, and routed to the right internet provider. Redback Networks built the box that did this, called the Subscriber Management System. It sat at the edge of the carrier network, and it went public in May 1999 as broadband was moving from trial to mass market.

The Original Bet

Redback was founded in 1996 in Silicon Valley. The bet was that broadband would create a new layer of the network, between the access lines and the internet core, and that this layer needed its own equipment. Redback's SMS product won quick adoption among carriers deploying DSL. It was one of the most celebrated networking start-ups of its moment. Its name came from the redback spider, a fitting emblem for a company spinning connections across the web.

After the Offering

Redback priced its offering at $23 a share in May 1999, and the stock leapt on its first day. It bought Siara Systems in 1999 in a stock deal worth billions, and its value soared before the telecom crash erased most of it. Redback restructured through a prepackaged bankruptcy in 2003 and emerged as a smaller company. Ericsson acquired Redback in 2007 for about $2.1 billion.

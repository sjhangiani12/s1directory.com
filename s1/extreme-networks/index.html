Gigabit at the Falls

Priced April 08, 1999

In 1999 corporate networks were straining under new traffic from the web, email, and file sharing. Ethernet, the standard for local networks, had just reached gigabit speeds. Extreme Networks built switches that delivered that speed at a lower cost per port than older designs. Its Summit and BlackDiamond lines used custom chips to switch traffic at full speed. Extreme went public in April 1999, the second wave of a networking boom.

The Original Bet

Gordon Stitt, Stephen Haddock, and Herb Schneider founded Extreme in 1996. The bet was that Ethernet would scale from the desktop to the network core, and that a focused start-up could beat bigger rivals on speed and price. Extreme built its switches around custom chips that handled routing and switching in hardware. Customers ranged from universities to data centers. The company competed head on with Cisco and with other new switch makers.

After the Offering

Extreme priced its offering at $17 a share in April 1999, and the stock rose sharply through 2000 before falling with the market. The company survived the crash and grew by acquisition in later years, buying wireless and data center businesses from Motorola, Avaya, and Brocade. Extreme remains a public company today. It moved its headquarters from Silicon Valley to North Carolina.

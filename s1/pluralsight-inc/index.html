Many Ways of Seeing

Priced May 17, 2018

Pluralsight sold technology skills by subscription. Its library held thousands of video courses on programming languages, cloud platforms and security, taught by working experts. Companies bought seats for their engineering teams to keep up with fast-changing tools. When Pluralsight filed to go public in 2018, every business was becoming a software business, and the shortage of skilled developers was a common complaint.

The Original Bet

Aaron Skonnard and three fellow trainers started Pluralsight in 2004 as a classroom training company. They taught developers in person, which was costly and hard to scale. In 2008 they moved the whole business online and began recording courses. The bet was that a large catalog of expert video, sold as a subscription, could reach far more learners than any classroom.

Out of Utah

Pluralsight priced at $15 a share in May 2018 and gained on its first day. The company grew its business sales and added skill assessments and analytics for managers. Growth slowed, and the stock never held its early highs. In 2021 Vista Equity Partners bought Pluralsight for about $3.5 billion and took it private. Aaron Skonnard led the company as chief executive throughout its public years.

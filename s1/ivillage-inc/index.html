A Village Square for Women

Priced March 18, 1999

In the late 1990s the web was still seen as a place built by and for young men. iVillage set out to change that. Its sites and message boards covered parenting, health, careers, and relationships, and women used them to trade advice with each other. When it went public in March 1999, iVillage was among the largest destinations for women online and one of the most watched media start-ups in New York.

The Original Bet

Candice Carpenter, Nancy Evans, and Robert Levitan founded iVillage in New York in 1995. The bet was that women would come online for community, not just content. The founders built the site around message boards where members helped each other, then sold advertising and sponsorships to brands that wanted to reach those women. Early investors included America Online. That focus set iVillage apart from portals built for a general audience.

After the Offering

iVillage priced its offering at $24 a share in March 1999, and the stock more than doubled on its first day. Losses were heavy in the years that followed, and the share price fell sharply when the bubble burst. iVillage cut costs, bought rival Women.com, and eventually turned a profit. NBC Universal acquired the company in 2006 for about $600 million. Its message boards had long outlasted the hype that surrounded its debut.

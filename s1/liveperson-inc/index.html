A Voice Behind the Website

Priced April 06, 2000

In 2000, a shopper with a question on a website usually had to call a phone number or send an email and wait. LivePerson, founded by Robert LoCascio, put a chat button on the page so a real person could answer right away. Companies paid a monthly fee for each agent. It went public in April 2000, just weeks after the Nasdaq peak, with a product that few companies yet knew they needed.

The Original Bet

LivePerson bet that live conversation would help websites sell more and serve customers better. Its chat software was hosted, so companies could add it without new servers. Online retailers, banks and telecom companies became major customers. The company later moved from website chat to messaging across apps and channels, and added automated assistants. Its agents could handle several chats at once, which saved money for its clients.

Surviving the Silence

LivePerson priced its IPO at $8 a share in April 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. Its shares fell below $1 in the crash, but the company cut costs and survived. It grew steadily through the 2000s and 2010s as online chat became normal. LivePerson is still public and trades as LPSN. It remains a pioneer of the chat window that now appears on so many websites.

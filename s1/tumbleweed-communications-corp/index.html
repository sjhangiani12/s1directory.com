Messages on the Wind

Priced August 05, 1999

By the late 1990s, businesses wanted to send contracts, statements and records over the internet instead of by courier, but plain email was far from safe. Tumbleweed made software to deliver those messages and files securely, with proof of receipt. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, banks and other regulated companies were looking for a way to trust the web with sensitive documents.

The Original Bet

Jeffrey Smith and Jean-Christophe Bandini started Tumbleweed in the early 1990s. The bet was that the internet would replace overnight couriers for important business documents, if someone could make delivery secure and traceable. Tumbleweed's software encrypted messages, tracked delivery, and gave both sides a record, which mattered in finance, health care and government. Its customers needed to prove that a message arrived intact, and to whom, which plain email could not do.

After the Offering

Tumbleweed priced at $12 a share in August 1999 and soared during the boom before falling sharply in the crash. It shifted toward email security and secure file transfer, selling widely to banks and government agencies, and bought several rivals. In 2008 the French software company Axway, part of Sopra Group, acquired Tumbleweed. Its products became part of Axway's secure file transfer and email security lines.

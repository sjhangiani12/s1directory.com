Earth's Biggest Bookstore

Priced May 15, 1997

In May 1997 Amazon was a bookstore with no stores. It sold books through a website, shipped them from a warehouse in Seattle, and claimed a catalog of more than a million titles. Few people bought anything online yet, and many doubted they ever would. Amazon was losing money and planned to keep losing it to grow. Its offering that spring was modest by later standards, and the company called itself Earth's Biggest Bookstore.

The Original Bet

Jeff Bezos left a hedge fund job in New York in 1994 and drove west to start the company. He chose books because there were millions of titles, too many for any shop to stock. The bet was that the web's unlimited shelf would beat physical stores on selection, and that customers would reward low prices and convenience. Bezos told investors to expect a long wait for profits.

After the Offering

Amazon priced its offering at $18 a share in May 1997. It added music, video, and then almost everything else, opened its site to other sellers, and survived the dot-com crash. It launched Prime, the Kindle, and Amazon Web Services, which turned its own computing systems into a business. Amazon remains a public company and one of the largest in the world.

Drawing Circuits From Words

Priced October 12, 2000

Chip designers in the 1990s increasingly described circuits in code rather than drawing them gate by gate. Synthesis software turned that code into a real design. Synplicity, based in Sunnyvale, built fast synthesis tools for programmable chips known as FPGAs, which engineers could configure after manufacturing. It went public in October 2000, as the chip industry was enjoying a record year and demand for design tools was high.

The Original Bet

Synplicity bet that FPGAs would grow from prototyping tools into chips used in real products, and that their designers would want specialized software. Its tools were quick and worked with chips from many vendors, which gave engineers a neutral choice. The company later added tools for verifying and prototyping larger custom chips. Many hardware engineers came to rely on its tools in their daily design work.

Folded Into a Giant

Synplicity priced its IPO at $8 a share in October 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. The chip downturn of 2001 slowed its growth, but the company stayed focused and kept a loyal base of engineers. In 2008 Synopsys, one of the largest chip design software companies, acquired Synplicity and added its tools to its own line. Programmable chips went on to become a large and important market, much as Synplicity had expected.

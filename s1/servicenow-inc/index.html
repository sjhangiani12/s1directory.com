The Clock Is Running

Priced June 28, 2012

ServiceNow modernized the IT help desk. When something broke, employees filed a ticket in its cloud software, and IT staff tracked the fix. The company sold this as a subscription and built a platform that customers could extend. When it filed to go public in 2012, large companies were starting to trust cloud software with core work, and ServiceNow was growing quickly.

The Original Bet

Fred Luddy founded the company in San Diego in 2004 as Glidesoft. He had been chief technology officer at Peregrine Systems, a maker of IT management software. He wanted to build a platform that made it easy to create business workflows. The bet was that IT service management was a good first use, and that the same platform could handle much more.

After the Bell

ServiceNow priced at $18 a share in June 2012 and rose on its first day. It grew far beyond IT into human resources, customer service and security work. Its value climbed into the hundreds of billions. ServiceNow remains a public company and one of the largest software makers. Frank Slootman led the company at the time of its offering. Later chief executive Bill McDermott pushed it further into AI.

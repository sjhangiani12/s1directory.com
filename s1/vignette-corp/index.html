Pages That Write Themselves

Priced February 18, 1999

In 1999 big websites were growing faster than anyone could update them by hand. Newspapers, retailers, and banks needed a way for editors to publish thousands of pages and change them daily. Vignette sold software that did this. Its StoryServer product stored content in a database and built web pages on demand, and it could tailor pages to each visitor. Vignette went public in February 1999 as one of the stars of Austin's growing tech scene.

The Original Bet

Ross Garber founded Vignette in Austin in 1995. The bet was that websites would become as complex as publishing houses and would need software to match. Vignette sold to media companies first, then to any business with a large website. It charged high license fees, and the market for its product grew as every major company moved online. Its product was expensive and complex, but for a large website in 1999 there were few alternatives.

After the Offering

Vignette priced its offering at $19 a share in February 1999, and the stock soared through the following year. At its peak the company was worth billions of dollars. Then the market crashed, and cheaper content tools crowded in. Vignette shrank but stayed in business for a decade. Open Text acquired it in 2009 for about $310 million. Its story traced the full arc of the enterprise software boom.

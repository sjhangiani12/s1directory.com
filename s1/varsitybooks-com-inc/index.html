Textbooks Without the Line

Priced February 15, 2000

Every semester, college students stood in long lines at the campus bookstore and paid full price for books they would use for four months. VarsityBooks.com set out to fix that from a laptop. When it filed to go public at the start of 2000, online retail was the hottest story in business, and selling textbooks to a captive, wired audience of students looked like an easy win.

The Original Bet

The company bet that students would gladly order textbooks online if the price was lower and the books arrived before class. It promised discounts off list price and fast shipping, and it spent heavily on campus marketing to build the brand. The deeper idea was that the campus bookstore, protected for decades by convenience, would lose its hold once ordering online became just as easy.

A Different Kind of Campus

VarsityBooks priced at $10 a share in February 2000. Deep discounts proved hard to sustain, and the company soon changed course, renamed itself Varsity Group, and began running online bookstores for schools rather than competing with them. It built a modest business serving private high schools and colleges. In 2008 Follett, the large bookstore operator, bought the company for a fraction of its offering price.

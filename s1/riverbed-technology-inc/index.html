Clear Water Over Stone

Priced September 20, 2006

Riverbed made far-away offices feel close. Its Steelhead appliances sat at each end of a company's wide area network and cut the data crossing it, so files and applications opened quickly in branch offices. In 2006 companies were pulling servers back into central data centers to save money, and slow links to remote offices had become a daily complaint. Its customers ranged from law firms to global manufacturers.

The Original Bet

Jerry Kennelly and Steve McCanne, a computer scientist known for internet video research, founded Riverbed in 2002. Their bet was that smarter software, not more bandwidth, could fix slow networks. Riverbed combined data deduplication and protocol tricks in a box that was easy to install, and sales grew quickly. Customers often saw results in days, which made the boxes an easy sale.

After the Offering

Riverbed priced its IPO at $9.75 a share in September 2006 on the Nasdaq and soon traded well above it. It led the WAN optimization market for years. As applications moved to the cloud, growth slowed, and in 2015 Thoma Bravo took Riverbed private for about $3.6 billion. It went through a debt restructuring in 2021, and still sells network performance tools.

A Butler for Every Question

Priced July 01, 1999

Early search engines expected people to think like librarians and type keywords. Ask Jeeves asked them to type a question, as they would to a person. A friendly cartoon butler stood on the home page, and editors behind the scenes matched common questions to good answers. When the company went public in the summer of 1999, the web was full of new users who found keyword search confusing, and Jeeves was one of the most recognized faces online.

The Original Bet

Garrett Gruener, a venture capitalist, and David Warthen, a software engineer, started the company in Berkeley in 1996. Their bet was that plain language would win over keywords. The system combined software that parsed questions with a large library of answers that people had written and checked. The butler, borrowed from P. G. Wodehouse's fictional valet, gave the service a personality that set it apart from the plainer search pages of the day.

After the Offering

Ask Jeeves priced its offering at $14 a share on July 1, 1999, and the stock soared on its first day of trading. Google soon showed that good ranking mattered more than plain-language parsing, and Ask Jeeves bought the Teoma search engine in 2001 to keep up. IAC acquired the company in 2005 for about $1.85 billion. The butler retired in 2006, and the service carried on as Ask.com.

Cover: Study of Valet with Coffee Pot, François Boucher. Art Institute of Chicago, public domain. Chosen for a valet with a coffee pot (the butler).

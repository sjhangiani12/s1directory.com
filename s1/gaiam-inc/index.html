Goods for a Gentler Life

Priced October 28, 1999

Gaiam took its name from Gaia, the Earth personified, and sold to a group marketers had begun to call cultural creatives: people who cared about health, the environment and personal growth. Through catalogs, the web and stores, it offered yoga mats, organic goods and wellness videos. When it filed to go public in late 1999, it was one of the few lifestyle brands built around a set of values.

The Original Bet

Jirka Rysavy, who also founded the office supply company Corporate Express, started Gaiam in 1988. His bet was that a meaningful number of shoppers wanted products matching their values, and that one company could serve them across many categories. Gaiam combined direct sales with media, producing fitness and yoga videos that also drew customers to its products. The name itself signaled the mission, borrowed from the Greek goddess of the Earth.

From Goods to Streaming

Gaiam priced at $5 a share in October 1999, a modest offering in a frothy year. It grew into a well-known yoga and fitness brand sold in large retail chains. Over time the company split its paths: it sold the Gaiam consumer brand business in 2016 and kept its streaming video service, renaming itself Gaia. Gaia still trades on Nasdaq.

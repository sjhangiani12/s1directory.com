Answers on the Clock

Priced August 05, 2004

RightNow was a software company from Bozeman, Montana, far from Silicon Valley. Greg Gianforte started it in 1997 to help companies handle customer questions online. Its software was hosted and sold by subscription, years before that became the standard way to buy business software. When RightNow filed to go public in the summer of 2004, the idea of renting software over the internet was still unproven to many investors.

The Original Bet

RightNow bet that companies would rather rent customer-service software than install it themselves. Its first product was a searchable knowledge base that let customers find answers without calling. It then added email handling, call-center tools and feedback surveys. The company built a large base of customers, including government agencies and big consumer brands, from its office in the mountains. Its success helped turn Bozeman into a small hub for software work.

From Bozeman to Redwood

RightNow priced its IPO at $7 a share in August 2004 and listed on Nasdaq. Its timing proved good, as software-as-a-service took off over the following years. Revenue grew steadily, and the company became one of the better-known names in customer-service software. Oracle agreed to buy RightNow in 2011 for about $1.5 billion and closed the deal in early 2012.

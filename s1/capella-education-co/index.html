Guided by a Northern Star

Priced November 09, 2006

Capella University taught working adults entirely online. Most of its students were mid-career and pursuing master's and doctoral degrees in fields like business, education, psychology and nursing. It was regionally accredited, which mattered to employers and students alike. When it went public in November 2006, online higher education was growing fast and drawing new investor interest. Its students came from every state and many countries.

The Original Bet

Stephen Shank, a former chief executive of the toy company Tonka, founded Capella in 1991 as The Graduate School of America. The bet was that adults with jobs and families would pursue advanced degrees if they could study on their own schedule. Capella built its programs online from the start, rather than adapting a campus model. It later added competency-based programs that let students move at their own pace.

After the Offering

Capella priced its IPO at $20 a share in November 2006 on the Nasdaq. Enrollment grew for several years, then slowed under tighter federal rules for for-profit schools. In 2018 Capella merged with Strayer Education, and the combined company became Strategic Education. Capella University continues to operate as part of Strategic Education, alongside Strayer University. It remains one of the largest online universities in the country.

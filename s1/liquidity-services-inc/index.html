Going Once, Going Twice

Priced February 22, 2006

Liquidity Services sold what others no longer needed. It ran online auctions for surplus and returned goods, from excess inventory at big retailers to used vehicles and equipment from government agencies. Its biggest early contract was selling U.S. military surplus. When it filed in 2006, it was turning a dusty corner of commerce into a steady online business. Buyers ranged from small resellers to large exporters.

The Original Bet

William Angrick founded the company in 1999. The bet was that surplus goods, usually sold in slow local auctions or scrapped, would fetch better prices online in front of many more buyers. Liquidity Services won large contracts with the Defense Department and built sites such as Liquidation.com and GovDeals. Its sellers paid a share of each sale, so the company earned more as prices rose.

After the Offering

Liquidity Services priced its IPO at $10 a share in February 2006 on the Nasdaq. It grew for years on its military surplus contracts, then lost some of them, and the stock swung widely. The company refocused on retail returns and government surplus. It remains publicly traded. Its marketplaces still handle large volumes of surplus goods from public agencies and retailers.

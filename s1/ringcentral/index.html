The Evening Bell

Priced September 27, 2013

RingCentral moved the office phone system into the cloud. Instead of buying hardware, a business paid a monthly fee for phone numbers, extensions and voicemail that worked on desk phones, computers and smartphones. It later added messaging, video and contact center software. When it filed to go public in 2013, small and midsize companies were switching to cloud services, and phone systems were next.

The Original Bet

Vlad Shmunis and Vlad Vendrow started RingCentral in 1999. Their early service offered online fax and phone features for small offices. The bet was that a full phone system could run over the internet, with no switches or wiring in the office. The company grew steadily through the 2000s and moved toward larger customers. Its headquarters were in Belmont, California.

Ringing Out

RingCentral priced at $13 a share in September 2013 and rose on its first day. It grew quickly and formed partnerships with large telecom companies. The stock soared in 2020 as remote work spread, then fell back. Founder Vlad Shmunis stepped down as chief executive in 2023. RingCentral remains a public company. It also partnered with other communications companies to reach more customers.

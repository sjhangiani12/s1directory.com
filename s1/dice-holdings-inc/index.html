A Roll of the Dice for Talent

Priced July 17, 2007

Dice was where technology workers looked for jobs. Its job board listed openings for programmers, engineers and IT staff, and employers paid to post jobs and search its database of résumés. By 2007 Dice Holdings also owned boards for finance and energy professionals. It filed as the tech job market was strong and hiring was brisk. Its audience was narrow, but highly sought after.

The Original Bet

Dice began in 1990 as an electronic bulletin board for computer contractors, years before the web. The bet was that specialists wanted a place built for their field, not a general job board. That focus let Dice charge employers more, because its audience was hard to reach any other way. Dice charged employers for job postings and résumé searches, while job seekers used the site free.

After the Offering

Dice Holdings priced its IPO at $13 a share in July 2007 on the New York Stock Exchange. Its business rose and fell with tech hiring, and LinkedIn became a strong rival for recruiters' budgets. The company renamed itself DHI Group and remains publicly traded. It later sold its finance and energy boards and returned its focus to technology careers.

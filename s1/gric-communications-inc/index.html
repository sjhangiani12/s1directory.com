Dial In From Anywhere

Priced December 15, 1999

Before Wi-Fi was everywhere, a business traveler who wanted email abroad had to find a local dial-up number and pay for an expensive call home. GRIC built an alliance of internet providers around the world so their customers could connect through one another. When it filed to go public in late 1999, mobile workers were multiplying and laptops were becoming standard business equipment.

The Original Bet

The bet was that the internet needed roaming the way mobile phones did. GRIC signed up providers in many countries and handled the settlement between them, so a customer of one provider could log on through another and be billed at home. The model let small providers offer global reach without building networks abroad, and GRIC earned a share of each connection.

From Roaming to Remote

GRIC priced at $14 a share in December 1999. As corporate laptops spread, the company shifted from serving internet providers to selling remote access directly to enterprises. It renamed itself GoRemote Internet Communications in 2004. In 2006 its larger rival iPass completed an all-cash acquisition of GoRemote valued at about $76.5 million. Its customers were mostly large companies with traveling staff. The two companies had long competed for the same business travelers, and the deal joined them.

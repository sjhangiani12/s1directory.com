The Poster on the Wall

Priced March 28, 2012

Millennial Media sold ads on phones. It connected advertisers with apps and mobile websites and used data to target the right users. Smartphones were spreading fast, but advertisers had only begun to shift budgets to mobile. When it filed to go public in 2012, it was one of the largest independent mobile ad networks, competing with Google and Apple. Its timing was early.

The Original Bet

Paul Palmieri co-founded Millennial Media in Baltimore in 2006, before the iPhone. The company bet that the phone would become a major ad medium, and that an independent network could gather enough apps to matter. It grew as smartphones spread and as app makers looked for ways to earn money. It built tools for app developers to place ads inside their apps.

Sold to AOL

Millennial Media priced at $13 a share in March 2012 and jumped on its first day. Competition from Facebook and Google soon squeezed its business. It bought Jumptap in 2013 to add scale. In 2015 AOL bought Millennial Media for about $238 million. AOL, then owned by Verizon, folded it into its own advertising business. The Millennial Media name faded soon after.

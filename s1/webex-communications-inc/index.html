A Meeting Without the Flight

Priced July 27, 2000

In 2000, sharing a presentation with colleagues in another city usually meant a plane ticket or a fuzzy phone call. WebEx, founded by Subrah Iyar and Min Zhu, let people join a meeting in a web browser and see the same screen. It ran the service on its own global network and sold it by subscription. WebEx went public in July 2000, as the dot-com market wobbled, but its product kept selling.

The Original Bet

WebEx bet that companies would pay to meet online, and that the service should be hosted rather than installed. That made it an early software-as-a-service business. It built its own network to keep meetings fast and reliable. After the September 11 attacks, when many businesses cut back on travel, demand for online meetings grew quickly. Sales teams were early adopters, using it to give demonstrations to customers far away.

Acquired by the Network

WebEx priced its IPO at $14 a share in July 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. It turned profitable and became the leading name in web conferencing. In 2007 Cisco bought WebEx for about $3.2 billion. Under Cisco, Webex became the core of its collaboration business, and its use surged during the pandemic of 2020. The product name is still in wide use today.

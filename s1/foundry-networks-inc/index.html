Forged for Speed

Priced September 27, 1999

By the late 1990s, networks were moving from megabits to gigabits, and the companies building them needed faster switches. Foundry Networks made high-performance Ethernet gear that competed directly with Cisco on speed and price. When it filed to go public in the fall of 1999, networking stocks were among the market's favorites, and Foundry arrived with something rare for the time: real profits.

The Original Bet

Bobby Johnson founded Foundry in 1996. The bet was that gigabit Ethernet would spread from the backbone to the data center and the enterprise, and that a focused company could build faster switches than the incumbents. Foundry designed its own chips, brought products to market quickly, and sold heavily to internet providers and hosting companies that needed raw speed. It grew quickly.

From Spark to Steel

Foundry priced at $25 a share in September 1999 and closed its first day above $156, a gain of more than 500 percent. The crash cut its value sharply, but the company stayed profitable and independent for nearly a decade. In 2008 Brocade bought Foundry for about $2.6 billion, after lowering its original offer during the financial crisis. It became part of Brocade's data center networking line.

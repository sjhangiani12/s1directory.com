The Apothecary Online

Priced July 28, 1999

drugstore.com set out to move the corner pharmacy online: vitamins, shampoo, cold medicine and prescriptions delivered to the door. It was backed by famous investors, including Amazon, and led by former Microsoft executive Peter Neupert. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, online retail was the market's favorite story, and drugstore products seemed like the perfect thing to buy again and again.

The Original Bet

The bet was that the drugstore, with its frequent, routine purchases, would move online once ordering became easy. Customers would save trips and gain privacy, and drugstore.com would win loyal repeat buyers. The company formed a partnership with Rite Aid to handle prescriptions and spent heavily on marketing to build its brand quickly. Amazon was an early investor and a close partner in its first years.

After the Offering

drugstore.com priced at $18 a share in July 1999. It lost large sums for years as shipping low-priced goods proved costly. Over time it focused on beauty and health products, bought Beauty.com, and reached hundreds of millions in annual sales. In 2011 Walgreens bought the company for about $409 million, and it later shut the site in 2016. It was a long run for a site once seen as a pure dot-com bet.

Wiring the Voice Network

Priced April 04, 2007

Veraz Networks sold gear that helped telephone companies move calls onto internet technology. Its switches, media gateways and voice compression products let carriers handle voice traffic over IP networks, cutting costs and adding services. Its customers were mostly phone carriers outside the United States. In 2007 carriers worldwide were in the middle of this long shift. Few consumers knew its name, but many international calls ran through equipment like it.

The Original Bet

Veraz bet that telephone carriers would replace their old circuit switches with software-based equipment for internet calling. It combined that with voice compression technology, which carriers in developing markets valued for saving scarce bandwidth. The company had close ties to ECI Telecom, which owned a large stake. Its products were especially popular with carriers in emerging markets, where bandwidth was costly. Its main rivals were much larger equipment makers.

After the Offering

Veraz priced its IPO at $8 a share in April 2007 on the Nasdaq. Competition from larger equipment makers and the 2008 downturn hurt sales. In 2010 Veraz merged with Dialogic, a communications technology company, and the combined business took the Dialogic name. The merger was in effect a reverse acquisition, with Dialogic's owners holding most of the combined company.

Trades at the Speed of a Click

Priced June 11, 1999

In 1999 online trading was one of the defining stories of the bull market. Millions of people opened accounts with internet brokers, and low commissions drew a new crowd of active traders. onlinetradinginc.com was a small player in this boom, a Florida brokerage that sold self-directed trading through its website. It went public in June 1999 with a modest offering, hoping to grow as more investors moved their accounts online.

The Original Bet

The bet was simple: as trading moved to the web, a lean online broker could win customers on price and service. Like its competitors, the firm earned money from commissions on each trade, so its revenue rose and fell with the market's mood. Smaller brokers like this one faced well-funded rivals that spent heavily on advertising to win accounts. Price and speed were its main selling points.

After the Offering

onlinetradinginc.com priced its offering at $7 a share in June 1999, one of the smaller deals of that busy summer. Trading volume fell when the market turned in 2000, which put pressure on every online broker that lived on commissions. The industry consolidated quickly in the years that followed, as larger firms bought up smaller ones and fought over price. Its story is a reminder of how crowded that corner of the boom became.

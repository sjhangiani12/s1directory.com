Sowing Sound Across the Web

Priced July 16, 1998

In 1998 most people reached the internet by dial-up modem, and streaming audio was crackly and unreliable. Broadcast.com streamed it anyway. It carried hundreds of radio stations, college and professional sports, press conferences, and live events, letting people listen to hometown games from anywhere. The Dallas company went public in July 1998 and became one of the defining stocks of the early internet boom.

The Original Bet

The company began as AudioNet, started by Chris Jaeb in 1995. Todd Wagner and Mark Cuban joined soon after and turned it into Broadcast.com. The bet was that the internet would become a broadcast medium, and that whoever controlled the content and the servers would win. Broadcast.com earned money from advertising and from companies that paid to stream their own events.

After the Offering

Broadcast.com priced its offering at $18 a share in July 1998, and the stock rose about 250 percent on its first day. Less than a year later, Yahoo agreed to buy the company in a stock deal worth about $5.7 billion. The deal made Mark Cuban a billionaire. Yahoo struggled to make the business work, and it faded within a few years.

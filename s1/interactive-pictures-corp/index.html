The Room All Around You

Priced August 05, 1999

In 1999, a photo on a website was a flat rectangle. IPIX offered something new: a spherical image you could drag to look in every direction, as if standing in the room. It grew from robotics work done near Oak Ridge National Laboratory. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, real estate sites and online travel were looking for ways to show places, not just pictures.

The Original Bet

The company began as TeleRobotics International, founded by H. Lee Martin to build camera systems for remote work in hazardous places. Its fisheye imaging technology could turn two wide photos into a full sphere. The bet was that this immersive view would become a standard way to show homes, hotels and products online, with IPIX earning a fee each time an image was used.

After the Offering

IPIX priced at $18 a share in August 1999 and soon merged with bamboo.com, a rival in virtual tours for real estate. The combined company did well on real estate listings during the boom, but losses mounted as the market fell and pricing for virtual tours dropped. After years of restructuring and new products, IPIX went bankrupt in 2006. Its name lived on in later licensing.

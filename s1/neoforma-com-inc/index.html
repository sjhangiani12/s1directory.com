Supplies for the Ward

Priced January 24, 2000

Hospitals buy an enormous range of things, from gauze to imaging machines, mostly through paper orders, phone calls and group contracts. Neoforma set out to move that purchasing online. When it filed to go public in early 2000, health care was one of the largest markets the internet had not yet touched, and business-to-business exchanges were promising to strip cost out of entire industries.

The Original Bet

Neoforma began in 1996 as a way to help hospitals find medical equipment. The bet grew into something larger: that hospitals, suppliers and distributors would all meet on one electronic exchange. The company signed long agreements with VHA and University HealthSystem Consortium, two large hospital purchasing groups, which gave it volume and also made them major shareholders. Those partnerships brought hospitals onto the system, but they also tied the company closely to two customers.

After the Offering

Neoforma priced at $13 a share in January 2000 and soared in its first months. The broad exchange vision faded with the market, and the company settled into supplying purchasing software and data services to its partner hospitals. In 2006 Global Healthcare Exchange, an industry-backed rival, completed its acquisition of Neoforma in a deal valued at about $200 million. Its software lived on inside the larger exchange.

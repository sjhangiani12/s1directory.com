Wires Strung Across the Country

Priced November 10, 2017

Bandwidth sells the plumbing behind modern calling and texting. Software companies use its APIs to place calls, send messages and connect emergency 911 service, all running on Bandwidth's own nationwide network. Many people have used it without seeing its name. When it filed to go public in 2017, cloud communications was a crowded field, but Bandwidth was unusual because it owned much of its network.

The Original Bet

David Morken and Henry Kaestner started the company in North Carolina in 1999, first selling internet connections. Over time they built a voice network and won large customers that needed reliable phone service in software. The bet was that owning the network, rather than renting it, would give better quality and lower cost. The company stayed in Raleigh and grew without much venture money.

Building the Network

Bandwidth priced at $20 a share in November 2017. The company grew as video meetings, contact centers and messaging apps moved to the cloud. It bought the European carrier Voxbone in 2020 to expand overseas. Demand surged during the pandemic and later cooled. Bandwidth remains a public company in Raleigh. Its customers came to include Microsoft, Google and Zoom. David Morken stayed on as chief executive.

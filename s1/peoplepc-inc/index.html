A Computer for Everyone

Priced August 16, 2000

In 1999 many American households still did not own a computer. PeoplePC, founded in San Francisco that year, offered a new PC, internet service and support for one monthly payment, like a phone plan. Its biggest moment came in 2000, when Ford and Delta Air Lines announced they would give home computers to their employees through PeoplePC. The company went public in August 2000.

The Original Bet

PeoplePC bet that the cost of a computer was the main barrier to getting online, and that spreading it out monthly would bring in new users. Corporate programs gave it a fast way to reach hundreds of thousands of households. The model, though, carried heavy hardware costs up front and relied on customers staying for years. It also sold through partners outside the United States.

Absorbed by an ISP

PeoplePC priced its IPO at $10 a share in August 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. The stock dropped quickly as the market turned and losses grew. The company cut back the hardware offer and focused on low-cost internet access. In 2002 EarthLink acquired PeoplePC, and the brand lived on for years as a budget internet service. Its early idea, a computer paid for monthly, later returned in phone plans.

The Bank Behind the Bank

Priced September 30, 1999

In 1999, big banks were building online banking for their customers. Thousands of community banks and credit unions could not afford to. Digital Insight offered them a hosted service, branded with their own names, that gave their members online banking and bill payment. When it filed to go public in the fall of 1999, small institutions feared losing customers to larger banks with better websites.

The Original Bet

The bet was that smaller financial institutions would rather rent online banking than build it. Digital Insight ran the systems in its own data centers and presented them under each client's brand, so customers saw their local credit union, not a vendor. The company earned recurring fees that grew as more account holders logged in. It was a steady, subscription-like business in a market full of uncertain bets.

A Steady Climb

Digital Insight priced at $15 a share in September 1999. Unlike many peers, its business kept growing after the crash, because online banking became a basic expectation. It bought competitors and became one of the leading providers to community banks and credit unions. In 2007 Intuit completed its purchase of Digital Insight for about $1.35 billion. NCR later bought the business from Intuit in 2014.

Everyone's a Critic

Priced March 01, 2012

Yelp turned word of mouth into a website. People wrote reviews of restaurants, dentists, mechanics and more, and others read them before choosing where to go. Local businesses paid for ads and enhanced listings. When Yelp filed to go public in 2012, it had tens of millions of reviews, and its biggest rival was Google. Smartphones made it easy to check reviews on the spot.

The Original Bet

Jeremy Stoppelman and Russel Simmons founded Yelp in 2004. Both had worked at PayPal. The original idea was an email system for asking friends for recommendations, but users liked writing reviews more. The bet was that honest reviews from ordinary people would become the main guide for local decisions. The site started in San Francisco and spread city by city.

After the Bell

Yelp priced at $15 a share in March 2012 and jumped on its first day. It kept growing as people searched for local businesses on phones. Yelp fought with Google over search results and with businesses over its review filter. It remains a public company. Its business came to rely on ads from local businesses and on services such as restaurant waitlists. Jeremy Stoppelman led the company as chief executive.

Aiming at the Audience

Priced July 20, 1999

Engage was one of the companies in CMGI's sprawling internet empire, and it promised advertisers a sharper aim. By building anonymous profiles of what people did online, it could show each visitor ads more likely to match their interests. When it filed to go public in the summer of 1999, online advertising was growing fast, and targeting was the idea everyone wanted to own.

The Original Bet

The bet was that the web could make advertising far more precise than print or television. Engage collected anonymous behavior data across many sites and used it to sort visitors into interest groups. It combined that with ad networks and ad-serving software, including AdKnowledge and Flycast, which CMGI folded into it, aiming to be a full system for online marketing.

After the Offering

Engage priced at $15 a share in July 1999 and became a high-flying CMGI stock. When the online ad market collapsed in 2000 and 2001, revenue fell hard and losses mounted. CMGI gave up its stake in 2002. In 2003 Engage filed for Chapter 11, and its remaining software assets were sold in the bankruptcy process. Its ad-serving unit had already been sold to its managers the year before.

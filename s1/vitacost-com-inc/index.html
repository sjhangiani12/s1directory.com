An Orchard Delivered by Mail

Priced September 23, 2009

Vitacost sold vitamins and health products online at low prices. Alongside national brands, it made and sold its own lines of supplements, which gave it better margins. It shipped from its own warehouses to customers across the country. It went public in September 2009, a time when few retailers were growing and online shopping kept gaining ground. Customers liked its deep discounts and wide range of brands that local stores did not carry.

The Original Bet

Wayne Gorsek founded the business in 1994 as a catalog company. He bet that customers who bought supplements regularly would shop by price and convenience, and that a direct seller could beat the markup of health food stores. Vitacost moved online early and invested in its own manufacturing. Free shipping thresholds and frequent coupons kept customers reordering every few months.

After the Offering

Vitacost priced its IPO at $12 a share in September 2009. Its years as a public company were difficult, with management turnover, accounting questions and losses. In 2014 the grocery chain Kroger bought Vitacost for about $280 million to strengthen its online health business. Kroger kept Vitacost running as an online store and used its warehouses and know-how to ship health products to its own shoppers.

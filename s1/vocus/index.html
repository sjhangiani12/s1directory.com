A Beacon for the Message

Priced December 06, 2005

Vocus gave public relations teams their software. Its online service kept databases of journalists and outlets, sent press releases, tracked news coverage and measured results, all in a web browser. It sold to corporations, agencies and government offices by subscription. In 2005, PR work still ran largely on phone calls and spreadsheets, and the move to online tools was just starting.

The Original Bet

Rick Rudman and Bob Lentz founded the company in 1992. They bet that public relations, a craft run on personal contacts, would adopt software for its daily work. Vocus moved to a hosted subscription model early, and later bought PRWeb, the online press release service. Its customers ranged from small agencies to large corporations and government offices. Subscriptions made its revenue steady and predictable.

After the Offering

Vocus priced its IPO at $9 a share in December 2005 on the Nasdaq. It grew steadily and added marketing tools for small businesses, with mixed results. In 2014 the private equity firm GTCR took Vocus private for about $446 million and combined it with Cision. The combined company kept the Cision name and became one of the largest providers of public relations software in the world.

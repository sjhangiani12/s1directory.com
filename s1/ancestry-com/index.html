The Roots Beneath the Branches

Priced November 05, 2009

Ancestry.com put the archive in the living room. It digitized billions of records, such as census rolls, ship manifests and military files, and sold subscriptions to people building their family trees. It filed to go public in late 2009, during a deep recession, with steadily rising subscribers and a popular television tie-in, Who Do You Think You Are? Many subscribers stayed for years, adding branches as new records came online.

The Original Bet

The company's roots go back to a genealogy publisher in Utah, a state with a deep tradition of family history research. Its bet was that the hobby of genealogy could become a mass market if the records were searchable from home. Ancestry spent years and large sums scanning and indexing records, which gave it a collection rivals could not easily copy.

After the Offering

Ancestry priced its IPO at $13.50 a share in November 2009. Subscribers kept growing, and the company began selling DNA tests that would later become a major business. In 2012 a group led by Permira took Ancestry private for about $1.6 billion. Blackstone bought the company in 2020. AncestryDNA, launched in 2012, sold millions of saliva kits and linked customers to relatives they had never met.

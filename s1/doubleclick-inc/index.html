Twice the Ad, Half the Guess

Priced February 20, 1998

By early 1998 banner ads paid the bills for much of the web, but placing them was crude. DoubleClick ran the servers that delivered ads to thousands of sites, and it used data about each visit to decide which ad to show. That let advertisers target people by interest, location, or time of day. The New York company went public in February 1998 as the leading name in online advertising.

The Original Bet

Kevin O'Connor and Dwight Merriman founded DoubleClick in 1996. The bet was that technology could make web advertising measurable in a way print and television never were. DoubleClick sold ads across a network of sites and also licensed its ad-serving tools to large publishers. Its system counted every impression and click. That data made online advertising measurable in a new way.

After the Offering

DoubleClick priced its offering at $17 a share in February 1998, and the stock rose dramatically through 1999. Its plan to merge web data with offline customer records led to a privacy uproar in 2000. Hellman & Friedman took the company private in 2005, and Google acquired DoubleClick in 2008 for about $3.1 billion, folding it into its advertising business. DoubleClick's ad tools became a core part of Google's advertising business.

Gods at the Go Board

Priced April 01, 2015

GoDaddy sold the internet's addresses. Millions of small businesses bought their domain names there, then added hosting, email and website tools. For years it was known for loud Super Bowl ads more than for its products. When GoDaddy filed to go public in 2015, a new leadership team was trying to change its image and focus on small business owners.

The Original Bet

Bob Parsons started the company in 1997 as Jomax Technologies, after selling his accounting software business. He renamed it GoDaddy in 1999, a name chosen partly because it was memorable. The bet was that the growing web would need cheap, simple domain names and hosting for everyone. Bold marketing and low prices made GoDaddy the biggest registrar. Its customer support ran around the clock.

A New Image

GoDaddy priced at $20 a share in April 2015, years after an earlier attempt to go public was withdrawn. KKR, Silver Lake and other investors had bought control in 2011. As a public company GoDaddy dropped its old ad style and built more software for small businesses. It remains public. Blake Irving was chief executive at the time of the offering. The company later bought several hosting businesses abroad.

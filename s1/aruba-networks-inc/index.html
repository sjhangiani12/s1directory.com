An Island Without Wires

Priced March 26, 2007

Aruba Networks built the Wi-Fi inside large organizations. Its access points and central controllers let a university, hospital or corporate campus run thousands of wireless connections securely from one place. In 2007 laptops had made Wi-Fi a must, and the first iPhone was months away. Companies were deciding that wireless, not cables, would be how people connected at work. Hospitals, schools and offices were early customers.

The Original Bet

Keerti Melkote and Pankaj Manglik founded Aruba in 2002. Their bet was that enterprise Wi-Fi needed central management and security, not standalone access points scattered around a building. Aruba competed directly with Cisco, which dominated networking, and won customers that needed large, mobile and secure wireless networks. Its design put the intelligence in a central controller, which made large networks easier to secure and manage.

After the Offering

Aruba priced its IPO at $11 a share in March 2007 on the Nasdaq. Smartphones and tablets soon flooded offices and campuses, and demand for its gear grew. In 2015 Hewlett-Packard bought Aruba for about $3 billion, and the Aruba name lived on as HP's networking brand. By then it had grown into a leading rival to Cisco in enterprise wireless networks. The Aruba brand remains at the center of HP's networking business.

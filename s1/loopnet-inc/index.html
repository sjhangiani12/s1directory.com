A Tower on Every Corner

Priced June 06, 2006

LoopNet was the listings site for commercial property. Brokers posted offices, shops, warehouses and apartment buildings for sale or lease, and buyers and tenants searched them free or with premium memberships. It became the largest marketplace of its kind in the United States. When it went public in 2006, commercial real estate was booming. It also offered data on property sales and comparables.

The Original Bet

Dennis DeAndre started LoopNet in San Francisco in 1995. The bet was that commercial real estate, a business of private networks and printed flyers, would move its listings online. LoopNet let brokers list for free and charged the heaviest users for premium features, which grew a large base of listings quickly. Its free listings drew the scale that rivals could not match.

After the Offering

LoopNet priced its IPO at $12 a share in June 2006 on the Nasdaq. It stayed profitable through the property downturn of 2008 and 2009. In 2012 CoStar, the commercial property data company, bought LoopNet for about $860 million after regulators approved the deal. LoopNet continues to run as a CoStar brand, and its listings remain a standard stop for commercial property searches.

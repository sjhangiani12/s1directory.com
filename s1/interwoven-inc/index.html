Many Hands, One Fabric

Priced October 07, 1999

As corporate websites grew from a few pages to thousands, they became impossible to manage by hand. Many people needed to write, edit and approve content without breaking the site. Interwoven built software to let them work together on it. When it filed to go public in the fall of 1999, every large company was struggling with the same problem, and content management became one of the era's key categories.

The Original Bet

Peng Ong founded Interwoven in 1995. The bet was that websites would need the same kind of disciplined workflow that software teams used for code: versions, reviews, approvals and controlled releases. Interwoven's TeamSite product let writers, designers and developers work in parallel and publish safely, weaving many contributions into one coherent site. The product became a standard choice for large corporate websites in the late 1990s, and it drew big customers in media, finance and technology.

After the Offering

Interwoven priced at $17 a share in October 1999 and became a high-flying stock during the boom. It survived the crash, merged with iManage in 2003, and found a strong niche selling document management to law firms and professional services companies. In 2009 the British software company Autonomy bought Interwoven for about $775 million. Its software lived on inside the larger company's products.

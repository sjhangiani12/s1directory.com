A Tower for Every Industry

Priced February 10, 1999

In 1999 investors believed that the biggest internet opportunity lay in trade between businesses, not in selling to consumers. VerticalNet was an early leader. It ran dozens of industry websites, each a community for professionals in one field, with news, product listings, and job boards. It earned money from advertising and from deals made between suppliers and buyers. VerticalNet went public in February 1999 as the leading example of business-to-business commerce.

The Original Bet

Michael McNulty and Douglas Alexander founded VerticalNet in Pennsylvania in 1995. The bet was that each industry would gather online in its own vertical community. VerticalNet built or bought sites for fields such as water treatment, semiconductors, and food service. The plan was to start with content and grow into trading marketplaces once buyers and sellers were in one place. Each community gave suppliers a focused audience and gave buyers a place to compare options.

After the Offering

VerticalNet priced its offering at $16 a share in February 1999, and its stock rose many times over within a year. At its peak the company was worth billions of dollars. When the business-to-business bubble burst in 2000, it sold most of its communities and turned to procurement software. BravoSolution acquired VerticalNet in 2008. Its rise and fall tracked the whole business-to-business boom almost exactly.

Every Part for Every Car

Priced February 08, 2007

U.S. Auto Parts sold replacement car parts online. Bumpers, headlights, mirrors and engine parts for thousands of makes and models were listed across its websites and on eBay, often at prices well below dealers and repair shops. It shipped from its own warehouses and sourced many products directly from manufacturers in Asia. It went public in early 2007, as do-it-yourself buyers were moving online.

The Original Bet

The company started in 1995 selling auto parts, and moved its business onto the internet. The bet was that car owners and small repair shops would buy parts online if the catalog was deep and the price was right. Matching the right part to the right vehicle was hard, and U.S. Auto Parts built its catalog data to solve it.

After the Offering

U.S. Auto Parts priced its IPO at $10 a share in February 2007 on the Nasdaq. Search engine changes and competition hurt its traffic in the years that followed, and the stock spent long stretches at low levels. The company renamed itself CarParts.com in 2020 and remains publicly traded. The company later invested in new distribution centers to ship parts faster across the country.

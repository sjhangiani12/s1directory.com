A Letter for Every Reader

Priced November 22, 1999

In 1999, email was becoming the most direct way a company could reach its customers, and the most abused. Digital Impact offered large brands a professional way to do it: personalized messages, sent at scale, with tracking to see what worked. When it filed to go public in late 1999, online retailers were building customer lists fast and wanted a way to bring shoppers back.

The Original Bet

The bet was that email would become a core marketing channel for large companies, and that they would hire specialists to run it. Digital Impact built the systems to send millions of personalized messages, measure opens and clicks, and tune campaigns. It sold mainly to big retailers and consumer brands, positioning itself as a full-service partner rather than a simple sending tool.

After the Offering

Digital Impact priced at $15 a share in November 1999. Its shares fell with the internet market, and the email business grew more crowded and cheaper over time. The company kept its large-brand focus and expanded into broader marketing services. In 2005, after an unsolicited bid from InfoUSA, the data company Acxiom agreed to buy Digital Impact for about $140 million.

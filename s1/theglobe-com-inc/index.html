The Whole World Logged In

Priced November 12, 1998

theglobe.com began as a project of two Cornell University students who believed the web was a place to meet people, not just to read. It offered free personal home pages, chat rooms, and forums, years before social networks became a business. By late 1998 it had millions of members and a New York headquarters. Its offering in November 1998 became one of the defining moments of the dot-com era.

The Original Bet

Stephan Paternot and Todd Krizelman started the company while they were students at Cornell. The bet was that online communities would draw huge audiences, and that advertisers would pay to reach them. theglobe.com grew by giving users tools to publish and connect, and by buying other community and gaming sites. Its members were young, and many were building their first web page. That made the site a gathering place for a generation learning to be social online.

After the Offering

theglobe.com priced its offering at $9 a share on November 13, 1998. On its first day the stock rose more than 600 percent, a record at the time. It became a symbol of the era's excess. Advertising revenue did not keep up with costs, and the stock collapsed in 2000 and 2001. The company sold or closed most of its businesses in the years that followed. Its founders went on to other ventures.

At the Summit of the Network

Priced October 12, 2006

Acme Packet made the gatekeepers of internet calling. Its session border controllers sat at the edges of carrier and enterprise networks, connecting voice and video calls between them, enforcing security and keeping the quality high. In 2006 telephone companies were moving their networks onto internet technology, and every border between networks needed a guard. Its main customers were telephone carriers and cable operators.

The Original Bet

Andy Ory and Patrick MeLampy, who had worked together at earlier networking companies, founded Acme Packet in 2000. Their bet was that internet calling would need a dedicated device at each network border, and that carriers would pay for one built for the job. It became the leader in a category it helped define. Those early relationships with major carriers set the stage for rapid growth.

After the Offering

Acme Packet priced its IPO at $9.50 a share in October 2006 on the Nasdaq. It was profitable and grew quickly as carriers and large businesses adopted internet calling. In 2013 Oracle bought Acme Packet for about $2.1 billion. Oracle used the business as the base of its communications unit, selling the same products to carriers and large enterprises. Its products remain in use in many carrier networks.

Spun by Hand

Priced April 16, 2015

Etsy built a marketplace for things made by hand. Knitters, jewelers, potters and vintage sellers set up shops, and buyers came looking for items they could not find in big stores. The company also stood out for its focus on its sellers and its social mission. When Etsy filed to go public in 2015, it was a certified B Corporation, an unusual status for a public company.

The Original Bet

Rob Kalin started Etsy in Brooklyn in 2005 with Chris Maguire and Haim Schoppik, and Jared Tarbell soon joined. Kalin was a craftsman and saw that eBay did not serve makers well. The bet was that handmade goods deserved their own market, with a warmer feel and lower fees. Etsy grew slowly at first, then fast, as millions of sellers joined.

Selling the Thread

Etsy priced at $16 a share in April 2015 and rose sharply on its first day. The stock then fell as growth slowed and new leaders cut costs. Sales boomed in 2020 when sellers made face masks during the pandemic. Etsy later dropped its B Corp status and moved its listing to the New York Stock Exchange in 2025. Josh Silverman led much of that turnaround.

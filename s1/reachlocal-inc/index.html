Main Street Goes Online

Priced May 19, 2010

ReachLocal sold the internet to Main Street. Plumbers, dentists and restaurants knew they needed customers to find them on Google, but few knew how to buy search ads. ReachLocal's salespeople signed them up, then its software spread their budgets across search engines and tracked the calls that came back. In 2010 the company was growing fast across the United States and abroad.

The Original Bet

Zorik Gordon and Michael Kline founded ReachLocal in 2004. They bet that small businesses would pay a premium to have someone else handle online advertising for them. The company built a large sales force and its own technology for managing campaigns. It earned its margin on the difference between what clients paid and the cost of the media it bought. Its pitch was simple: a business owner paid one monthly budget, and ReachLocal handled the rest.

After the Offering

ReachLocal priced its IPO at $13 a share in May 2010. Over time, small businesses found cheaper ways to advertise, and the costly sales model became a burden. Revenue fell and the stock sank far below its offering price. In 2016 Gannett, the newspaper publisher, bought ReachLocal for about $156 million. Its decline mirrored a broader shift, as Google and Facebook made self-serve advertising easy for even the smallest shops.

Rules of the Game

Priced March 03, 2000

In 2000, businesses were rushing to put their operations onto the web, and programmers were scarce. Versata, based in the San Francisco Bay Area, offered a different way to build applications. Developers described business rules, such as how to calculate a price or approve an order, and Versata's software produced the working program. It went public in March 2000, at the very peak of the market.

The Original Bet

Versata bet that business rules could be separated from code, which would let companies build and change applications faster. Its tools ran on popular Java application servers. The approach appealed to large companies with complex logic. Sales, however, depended on big projects, and those slowed sharply when tech budgets were cut. Its software was used for order processing, pricing and similar tasks.

Checkmate

Versata priced its IPO at $24 a share in March 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. Its shares traded well above the offer price at first, then fell steeply as the market crashed. The company cut staff and shrank for several years. In 2006 Trilogy, the Austin software company, acquired Versata. Under Trilogy, the Versata name became an umbrella for many acquired software products.

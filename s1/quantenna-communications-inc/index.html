Signals Through the Air

Priced October 28, 2016

Quantenna designed the chips that carry Wi-Fi through a house. Its chipsets used many antennas at once to send more data and reach farther. Cable and telecom companies put them in the gateways and set-top boxes they gave to customers. When Quantenna filed to go public in 2016, video streaming was pushing home networks hard, and faster Wi-Fi had become a selling point.

The Original Bet

Behrooz Rezvani and Stanford professor Andrea Goldsmith founded Quantenna in 2006. Goldsmith was an expert in wireless theory, and the company focused early on an antenna method called MIMO. The bet was that Wi-Fi could become good enough to stream high-definition video through walls, replacing cables. Quantenna shipped some of the first chips for each new Wi-Fi standard. Its chips reached homes through gear sold by service providers.

Bought by a Bigger Chipmaker

Quantenna priced at $16 a share in October 2016. The company grew with the arrival of faster Wi-Fi standards and won major service provider customers. In 2019 ON Semiconductor bought Quantenna for about $1.07 billion in cash, adding Wi-Fi to its line of chips. Service providers around the world used its chips in gateways and extenders. The deal gave ON Semiconductor a foothold in home and enterprise wireless.

A Bargain in the Shop Window

Priced March 06, 2014

Coupons.com moved the Sunday coupon onto the web. Shoppers printed or loaded discounts from its site and partner retailers, and consumer brands paid for each coupon redeemed. It later added digital promotions on grocery store loyalty cards. When it filed to go public in 2014, it was one of the oldest consumer internet companies still growing, and its debut drew strong demand.

The Original Bet

Steven Boal founded the company in 1998. He saw that the paper coupon business was huge but wasteful, and that the internet could aim offers at the right shoppers. The company survived the dot-com crash and built partnerships with major brands and grocery chains. The bet was that promotions would become digital, measurable and personal. Many households already clipped coupons each week.

A New Name

Coupons.com priced at $16 a share in March 2014 and soared on its first day. The stock then fell back as growth slowed. In 2015 the company renamed itself Quotient Technology to reflect its wider promotions business. In 2023 Neptune Retail Solutions bought Quotient and took it private. Its digital promotions ran through major grocery chains. Steven Boal led the company for most of its public life.

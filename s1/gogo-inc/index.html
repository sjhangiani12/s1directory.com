Wi-Fi Above the Waves

Priced June 20, 2013

Gogo brought the internet onto airplanes. Its air-to-ground network of cell towers let passengers on U.S. flights use Wi-Fi for a fee. Airlines installed the gear, and Gogo shared revenue with them. When Gogo filed to go public in 2013, it was the leader in in-flight internet, but satellite rivals promised faster service. Getting online in the air was still a novelty for many travelers.

The Original Bet

Gogo began in 1991 as Aircell, which made phones for private planes. In 2006 it won the auction for air-to-ground radio spectrum, which let it build its own network for commercial jets. The bet was that travelers would pay to stay online in the air. Gogo launched its service in 2008 with American Airlines. Its network covered the continental United States.

Turbulence

Gogo priced at $17 a share in June 2013. The stock rose, then fell as airlines moved to satellite service. In 2020 Gogo sold its commercial airline business to Intelsat and focused on business aviation. The company remains public and serves private jets. Gogo moved its headquarters to Colorado. In 2024 it bought Satcom Direct to add satellite service for business jets.

Reading Every Letter

Priced April 19, 2012

Proofpoint protected corporate email. Its software screened messages for spam, phishing and malware, and helped companies keep sensitive data from leaking out. Email was the most common way attackers reached employees. When Proofpoint filed to go public in 2012, it was moving its products to the cloud and adding archiving and compliance tools. Phishing attacks were growing more common. Email remained the main tool of business.

The Original Bet

Eric Hahn founded Proofpoint in 2002. He had been chief technology officer at Netscape. Spam was flooding inboxes, and companies needed a better filter. The bet was that email would stay at the center of business, and that the attacks against it would keep getting more targeted and costly. Gary Steele became chief executive in its first year and led it for many years.

Guarding the Inbox

Proofpoint priced at $13 a share in April 2012. It grew steadily as phishing became the main way into companies. The stock rose for years. In 2021 Thoma Bravo bought Proofpoint for about $12.3 billion, one of the largest software buyouts of that period. It bought several smaller security companies along the way. Under private ownership Proofpoint kept buying companies and widening its products.

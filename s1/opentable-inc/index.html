A Table Waiting for You

Priced May 21, 2009

OpenTable let diners book a restaurant table online in seconds. Behind that simple service was a computer system that sat at the host stand of thousands of restaurants, managing seating and reservations. Restaurants paid a monthly fee for the system and a small charge for each diner the website sent. It went public in May 2009, in the depths of the recession, when restaurants were hurting and few tech companies dared to list.

The Original Bet

Chuck Templeton founded the company in 1998 after struggling to book a dinner reservation by phone. The bet was that both sides needed help: diners wanted to book at any hour, and restaurants wanted to fill seats and know their guests. OpenTable spent years installing its system restaurant by restaurant, city by city, before the network effects took hold. Its early markets were San Francisco and a handful of other big cities.

The Road to the Bell

OpenTable priced its IPO at $20 a share in May 2009 and rose sharply on its first day, a bright spot in a grim market. The company expanded to more cities and to mobile booking. In 2014 Priceline, now Booking Holdings, bought OpenTable for about $2.6 billion. The deal gave Priceline a foothold in dining, and OpenTable kept growing in cities around the world.

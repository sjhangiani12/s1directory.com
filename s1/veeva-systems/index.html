A Cloud for the Life Sciences

Priced October 16, 2013

Veeva Systems sold cloud software to one industry, life sciences. Its first product helped drug company sales reps track visits to doctors, built on top of Salesforce. It then built its own platform for clinical, quality and regulatory content. When Veeva filed to go public in 2013, it was unusual for a young cloud company: it was already profitable. That was rare.

The Original Bet

Peter Gassner, a former Salesforce executive, and Matt Wallach founded Veeva in 2007. They believed that a narrow industry focus would let them build deeper products than general software vendors could. The bet was that pharmaceutical companies would pay well for software that fit their regulated world. Veeva also used a small amount of outside capital. It kept that single-industry focus.

Steady Growth

Veeva priced at $20 a share in October 2013 and rose sharply on its first day. The company kept growing and stayed profitable. In 2021 it became a public benefit corporation, one of the first large public companies to do so. Veeva later moved its customer software off Salesforce onto its own platform. It remains public. Peter Gassner remains chief executive.

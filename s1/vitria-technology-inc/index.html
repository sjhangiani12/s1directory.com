Light Through Many Panes

Priced September 16, 1999

Large companies in the 1990s ran dozens of separate systems for orders, billing, inventory and customers, and those systems rarely talked to one another. Vitria made software to connect them and coordinate the work moving between them. When it filed to go public in the fall of 1999, integration was becoming urgent as companies tried to link their old systems to the web.

The Original Bet

JoMei Chang and Dale Skeen founded Vitria in 1994, drawing on Skeen's work in messaging systems. The bet was that a company's processes, not just its data, needed to flow across systems. Vitria's BusinessWare platform passed events between applications and modeled the steps of a business process, so an order could move from website to warehouse to invoice automatically. Telecom and finance were early markets.

After the Offering

Vitria priced at $16 a share in September 1999 and became one of the most valuable software stocks of the boom. Its sales fell as integration spending dropped after 2000, and larger vendors moved into its market. The founders remained in charge throughout. In 2007 they took Vitria private through a company they controlled, ending its run as a public company.

A Seat in the Loge

Priced November 03, 1999

In 1999, buying a concert or ball game ticket usually meant a phone line, a box office or an outlet in a record store. Tickets.com set out to challenge Ticketmaster by putting the sale online and giving venues their own ticketing systems. When it filed to go public in late 1999, it promised fans an easier way to buy and venues a choice beyond the dominant player.

The Original Bet

The bet was that the internet could break the hold of a single ticketing giant. Tickets.com combined several ticketing software companies and offered venues technology they could run themselves, along with online sales. It worked with theaters, arts centers and sports teams, many of which wanted more control over their customers and their fees. It was a hard fight, because the market leader held long exclusive contracts with many large venues.

Into the Ballpark

Tickets.com priced at $12.50 a share in November 1999. It lost money for years as it fought a much larger rival, and its share price fell far below the offering. Its strongest ties proved to be with baseball teams. In 2005 Major League Baseball Advanced Media bought the company for $1.10 a share, about $66 million. The league used it to build its own ticketing for clubs across baseball.

Servers Under the Moonlight

Priced August 07, 2008

Rackspace ran other companies' servers and answered the phone when they broke. Customers rented dedicated machines in Rackspace data centers and paid extra for a team that managed them day and night. The company called this 'Fanatical Support' and built its culture around it. When it went public in August 2008, the financial system was weeks from collapse, and Amazon's new cloud was beginning to change what hosting meant.

The Original Bet

Three Trinity University students, Richard Yoo, Dirk Elmendorf and Pat Condon, started the company in San Antonio in 1998, and investor Graham Weston soon joined to lead it. The bet was that most businesses did not want to run their own servers, and that service, not hardware, would set a hosting company apart. Rackspace grew quickly by serving small and midsize businesses that had no IT staff.

After the Offering

Rackspace priced its IPO at $12.50 a share in August 2008 on the New York Stock Exchange. It launched a cloud business and co-founded the OpenStack project with NASA in 2010. Competition from Amazon, Microsoft and Google squeezed it, and Apollo took it private in 2016 for about $4.3 billion. It returned to the public market in 2020. Today it mostly helps customers run workloads on the big public clouds.

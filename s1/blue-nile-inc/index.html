Diamonds by Mail

Priced May 19, 2004

Few people expected the internet to sell engagement rings. Diamonds were costly, emotional purchases, and they were sold in hushed jewelry stores with heavy markups. Blue Nile, founded in Seattle in 1999, put thousands of certified diamonds online with their cut, color, clarity and carat listed in plain view. When it went public in May 2004, it had shown that careful buyers would spend thousands of dollars on a ring they had only seen on a screen.

The Original Bet

Blue Nile bet that information would beat atmosphere. By listing grading reports and prices openly, it let shoppers compare stones the way they compared other goods. It did not hold most of its diamonds in inventory. Instead it listed stones held by suppliers, which kept costs low and let it charge far less than a typical store. Its return policy and certified grading gave buyers the confidence to spend.

Beyond the Screen

Blue Nile priced its IPO at $20.50 a share in May 2004 and listed on Nasdaq under the ticker NILE. The stock rose sharply on its first day. Blue Nile became the best-known online jeweler in America and expanded abroad. In 2017 a group led by Bain Capital took the company private for about $500 million, and it later opened physical showrooms.

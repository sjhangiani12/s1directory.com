Counting the Crowd

Priced December 08, 1999

Television had Nielsen ratings. The web, by 1999, had a flood of claims about traffic and very few trusted numbers. NetRatings built a panel of internet users whose browsing was measured, then sold the results to publishers and advertisers. When it filed to go public in late 1999, online ad spending was soaring, and buyers badly wanted to know who was actually watching.

The Original Bet

The bet was that the internet would need an independent ratings service, just as broadcasting had. Advertisers would not move large budgets online without credible audience numbers, and NetRatings offered them. It formed a partnership with Nielsen Media Research early on, which lent its data the name that advertisers already trusted, and the service became known as Nielsen//NetRatings. That trust helped its numbers become a common currency in the online ad market.

Back to Nielsen

NetRatings priced at $17 a share in December 1999. It bought several rivals and research firms as the market consolidated after the crash, and it remained one of the standard sources for web audience data. Nielsen held a large stake throughout. In 2007 Nielsen bought the shares it did not already own for $21 each, valuing the deal at about $327 million.

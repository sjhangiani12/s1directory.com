Fans Meet the Band

Priced March 27, 2000

In the late 1990s, musicians and fans were discovering each other online, and record labels were slow to follow. ARTISTdirect, founded in Los Angeles by music industry veterans, built a network of artist sites and a store for music and merchandise. It also launched a record label with well-known industry figures. The company went public in March 2000, as Napster was shaking the music business.

The Original Bet

ARTISTdirect bet that artists would want a direct line to their fans, and that a site built around them could earn money from merchandise, advertising and music sales. It gave musicians a share of what their fans spent. The idea was early. Fans were moving to free downloads, and online music sales were still years from catching on. Its artist pages offered news, tour dates and merchandise in one place.

Fading Out

ARTISTdirect priced its IPO at $12 a share in March 2000 and listed on Nasdaq. Losses mounted as the dot-com market fell and the label struggled. The company cut back sharply and spent much of the decade searching for a stable business. It never regained the attention it had at its offering. Its early idea of a direct link between artists and fans became common on later platforms.

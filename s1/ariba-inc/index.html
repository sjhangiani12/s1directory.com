A Harbor for Every Purchase

Priced June 22, 1999

Large companies spend huge sums on everyday supplies, from pens to computers, and in the 1990s most of it ran through paper forms and phone calls. Ariba put that buying on the web. Employees chose items from an online catalog, managers approved them, and orders went to suppliers electronically. When Ariba went public in June 1999, business-to-business commerce was the hottest idea on Wall Street, and Ariba was its clearest example.

The Original Bet

Keith Krach and a group of co-founders started Ariba in 1996 in Silicon Valley. The bet was that procurement, a dull back-office task, was a large market waiting for the web. Ariba's software cut the cost of processing a purchase order and helped companies steer spending to preferred suppliers. Ariba also built a network so that suppliers could connect once and reach many buyers.

After the Offering

Ariba priced its offering at $23 a share in June 1999, and the stock soared on its first day of trading. At its peak the company was worth tens of billions of dollars, and then it lost most of that value when the bubble burst. Ariba cut costs and refocused on its supplier network, which became its lasting strength. SAP bought Ariba in 2012 for about $4.3 billion.

The Cash Register of the Web

Priced June 23, 1999

In 1999 a business that wanted to sell online had to solve a hard problem on its own: how to accept a credit card from a stranger and know it was real. CyberSource offered that as a service. A merchant's website passed the order to CyberSource, which authorized the card, checked for fraud, and handled tax and export rules. It was one of the first companies to sell payment processing over the internet to other internet companies.

The Original Bet

William McKiernan started CyberSource in 1996 inside software.net, an early online software store. The store had built tools to take payments and spot fraud, and McKiernan saw that other merchants needed the same tools. The bet was that the plumbing of online commerce would be a business of its own. CyberSource charged per transaction, so it grew as online shopping grew.

After the Offering

CyberSource priced its offering at $11 a share in June 1999. The stock climbed fast and then collapsed with the dot-com crash, but the company kept its core business and stayed independent. It bought Authorize.Net in 2007, adding thousands of small merchants. Visa acquired CyberSource in 2010 for about $2 billion, and the name lives on as Visa's platform for online payments.

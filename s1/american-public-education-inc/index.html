Lessons in Uniform

Priced November 08, 2007

American Public Education ran an online university built for people in uniform. Its American Military University and American Public University offered degrees that soldiers, sailors and veterans could complete from anywhere, often paid for by military tuition assistance. When it filed in 2007, for-profit online colleges were growing fast, and APEI stood out for its low tuition and its military focus.

The Original Bet

James Etter, a retired Marine officer, founded American Military University in 1991. The bet was that service members, who move often and deploy far from campus, needed a university that went with them. The school kept its prices close to the military's tuition assistance cap, which made it an easy choice for many students. Its admissions were open and courses started every month, which suited students on shifting military schedules.

After the Offering

APEI priced its IPO at $20 a share in November 2007 on the Nasdaq, and the stock rose strongly in its early years. Enrollment later slowed as for-profit education faced tighter federal rules. The company expanded into nursing education through acquisitions and remains publicly traded. The American Public University System remains its core, and it serves many civilian students alongside service members.

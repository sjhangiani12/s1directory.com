Eyes Open While America Sleeps

Priced February 08, 2006

NightHawk Radiology read X-rays and CT scans while American radiologists slept. Hospitals sent their overnight emergency images over the internet to U.S.-trained, U.S.-licensed radiologists working in Sydney and Zurich, where it was daytime. Reports came back in minutes. In 2006 emergency rooms were scanning more patients than ever, and small radiology groups struggled to cover every night. Its doctors worked days abroad and read scans for hospitals across the country.

The Original Bet

Paul Berger, a radiologist in Idaho, co-founded NightHawk in 2001. The bet was that time zones could solve the overnight staffing problem, and that radiology groups would gladly pay to give their doctors a full night's sleep. The company handled licensing and credentials across many states, which was the hard part. Its radiologists held licenses in many states at once.

After the Offering

NightHawk priced its IPO at $16 a share in February 2006 on the Nasdaq. Competition and pricing pressure grew, and many radiology groups began to cover nights themselves. In 2010 Virtual Radiologic bought NightHawk for about $170 million. The combined company, under the vRad name, became the largest teleradiology provider in the United States. Its overnight model is now standard practice in American hospitals.
